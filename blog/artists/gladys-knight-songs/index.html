Gladys Knight Songs: 12 Hits Played, 1 at Number One

By Charlie · Published October 15, 2026

▶ Jump to the playable list

Gladys Knight songs ruled the soul charts through the first half of the 70s.

One of them, Midnight Train to Georgia, reached number one on the Hot 100, and six topped the R&B chart.

This page plays twelve of them, from If I Were Your Woman to Part Time Love.

Six reached the Hot 100's top ten, and the group left Motown for Buddah Records in the middle of it all.

Many began as songs by other writers, including Jim Weatherly and Curtis Mayfield.

Gladys Knight Songs on the Charts: One Number One, Six Top Tens

Midnight Train to Georgia held the top spot in the weeks starting October 27 and November 3, 1973.

Wikipedia says it replaced Angie by the Rolling Stones.

What the Chart Numbers Say

Six of the twelve hits on this page reached the Hot 100's top ten.

  • Midnight Train to Georgia was the only number one, in 1973.
  • Neither One of Us stopped at number two, and Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me at number three.
  • I've Got to Use My Imagination reached number four, and On and On reached number five.
  • If I Were Your Woman rounded out the top ten at number nine.
  • Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me ranks #34 on the 1974 Year-End Hot 100, ahead of I've Got to Use My Imagination at #41 and On and On at #69.
  • Six songs on this page reached number one on the R&B chart, per Wikipedia.

The Pips and Motown: Who Sang on Every Gladys Knight Song

The Gladys Knight songs of the 70s were group records.

Wikipedia says the lineup all through the decade was Gladys Knight, her brother Merald Bubba Knight, William Guest and Edward Patten.

Patten joined in 1959, and the three men sang the call-and-response parts behind her lead.

Leaving Motown for Buddah

The group started the decade on Motown, where Neither One of Us became their biggest seller.

Wikipedia says contract talks failed, and they left Motown in February 1973 for Buddah Records.

Neither One of Us came out in December 1972, during the dispute.

Midnight Train to Georgia followed on Buddah in August 1973.

The Group on Television

The group also reached viewers through television.

Their Soul Train appearance is in a Billboard section about the show, dated September 28, 1974.

1970 to 1972: If I Were Your Woman, I Don't Want to Do Wrong and Neither One of Us

The first three Gladys Knight songs here were all Motown hits.

Two reached the top two of the soul chart, and one reached number one.

If I Were Your Woman

If I Were Your Woman was recorded on March 13, 1970 at Hitsville USA in Detroit.

Pam Sawyer, Clay McMurray and Gloria Jones wrote it, and McMurray produced it, with Paul Riser as arranger.

Motown released it on its Soul Records label in November 1970.

It reached number nine on the Hot 100 and number one on the soul chart for one week in January 1971, and it ranks #90 on the 1971 Year-End Hot 100.

George Michael performed it at the Nelson Mandela 70th Birthday Tribute at Wembley Stadium on June 11, 1988, and Stephanie Mills charted with it in 1988.

Alicia Keys recorded it in 2003, and her full version on Unplugged earned a 2006 Grammy nomination for Best Traditional R&B Vocal Performance.

I Don't Want to Do Wrong

I Don't Want to Do Wrong came out in 1971, from the album If I Were Your Woman.

Johnny Bristol produced it, and Bristol, the four members of the group and Katherine Anderson-Schaffner wrote it.

It reached number 17 on the Hot 100 and number two on the soul chart.

It is on no 1970s year-end list, so its peak above is a weekly peak only.

Neither One of Us (Wants to Be the First to Say Goodbye)

Jim Weatherly wrote Neither One of Us and recorded it before the group did.

Joe Porter picked it from Weatherly's publishing catalog, and Knight recorded her lead vocal in one take at MoWest in Los Angeles.

The group later returned to add background vocals and to redo her lead.

It reached number two on the Hot 100, held from number one by Vicki Lawrence's The Night the Lights Went Out in Georgia.

It was number one on the soul chart on March 17, 1973 and stayed there for four weeks.

It ranks #45 on the 1973 Year-End Hot 100, and it won the Grammy for Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo or Group on March 2, 1974.

See where it landed among 1973 hits on the page for top songs of 1973.

More Motown Hits

Three more Motown singles reached the Hot 100 in these years.

  • You Need Love Like I Do (Don't You) reached number 25 on the Hot 100 and number three on the R&B chart in 1970.
  • Make Me the Woman That You Go Home To reached number 27 and number three on the R&B chart in 1971.
  • Help Me Make It Through the Night reached number 33 and number 13 on the R&B chart in 1972.

1973: Midnight Train to Georgia, I've Got to Use My Imagination and More

1973 gave Gladys Knight songs their one Hot 100 number one.

The year also gave the album Imagination, the group's first on Buddah Records.

Wikipedia says it sold a million copies and produced two top ten singles.

Midnight Train to Georgia

Jim Weatherly wrote Midnight Train to Georgia, and first recorded it as Midnight Plane to Houston.

Sonny Limbo wanted to record it with Cissy Houston, and asked Weatherly if he minded changing the title.

Weatherly agreed, saying: "Just don't change the rest of the song."

Houston recorded it as Midnite Train to Georgia in 1973, and the group kept her title.

Knight wrote that she and Bubba Knight worked with Weatherly to rewrite the lyrics so she would be more comfortable with them.

Tony Camillo and the group produced their version, and Buddah released it in August 1973 from Imagination.

Number One on the Hot 100

It entered the Hot 100 at number 71, and reached number one on October 27, 1973.

This site's weekly data gives it two weeks at the top.

It also topped the soul chart.

  • The RIAA certified it gold on October 18, 1973, for one million copies.
  • It won the 1974 Grammy for Best R&B Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus.
  • It entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1999.
  • It also won Favorite Soul/R&B Single at the 1975 American Music Awards.
  • It ranks #49 on the 1973 Year-End Hot 100.

Its week at the top is one of the stories on the page about 70s number one hits.

I've Got to Use My Imagination

Gerry Goffin and Barry Goldberg wrote I've Got to Use My Imagination, and Kenny Kerner produced it.

Buddah released it in November 1973 from Imagination.

It reached number four on the Hot 100 and number one on the soul chart for one week in early 1974.

It ranks #41 on the 1974 Year-End Hot 100, and Wikipedia says it eventually sold more than one million copies.

Joan Osborne, Bobby Bland and Joe Cocker are among the singers who covered it.

It was also sung by characters on the television series A Different World.

Daddy Could Swear, I Declare and Where Peaceful Waters Flow

Two more Gladys Knight songs from 1973 reached the Hot 100 without making a year-end list.

The discography article gives the details.

  • Daddy Could Swear, I Declare reached number 19 on the Hot 100 and number two on the R&B chart, from the album Neither One of Us.
  • Where Peaceful Waters Flow reached number 28 on the Hot 100 and number six on the R&B chart, from the album Imagination.

1974: Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me, On and On and I Feel a Song

Gladys Knight songs in 1974 included three on the Year-End Hot 100.

Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me ranks #34 on that list, I've Got to Use My Imagination #41 and On and On #69.

Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me

Jim Weatherly wrote Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me.

He told Tom Roland he wrote it in 1971, in about 30 minutes to an hour.

Ray Price's country version reached number one on Hot Country Singles on October 6, 1973.

The group's version came out in February 1974 on Buddah, from Imagination.

It reached number three on the Hot 100 and number one on the soul chart for two weeks.

It also reached number seven in the UK, and the RIAA certified it gold.

The Carpenters sat on the same charts, and their hits are on the page for Carpenters songs.

On and On

Curtis Mayfield wrote On and On and produced it for the film Claudine.

The Impressions had first recorded it in 1972 as Our Love Goes On and On.

Mayfield changed some lyrics and recut it with the group in 1974.

Buddah released it in April 1974, with The Makings of You on the flip.

It reached number five on the Hot 100, number two on the R&B chart and number 13 in Canada.

It ranks #69 on the 1974 Year-End Hot 100, and it was nominated for a Golden Globe for Best Original Song.

I Feel a Song (In My Heart)

Tony Camillo and Mary Sawyer wrote I Feel a Song (In My Heart).

Sandra Richardson made the original recording in 1971, and the group's version was the song's fourth.

Linda Carr and the Stairsteps recorded it in 1972, and the Stairsteps used more spiritual lyrics.

Buddah released it in October 1974, from the album I Feel a Song.

It reached number 21 on the Hot 100 and number 13 in Canada.

It was number one on the soul chart for two weeks in late 1974.

It is on no 1970s year-end list, so its peak above is a weekly peak only.

A Song That Kept Being Sampled

Cassius sampled it in I <3 U So in 2010.

Jay-Z and Kanye West later sampled that record in Why I Love You, in 2011.

Darkchild sampled it earlier, in Megan Rochell's Heartbreak, in 2006.

1975: The Way We Were/Try to Remember and Part Time Love

The Gladys Knight songs of 1975 were smaller hits than the Buddah years.

Two of them were still on the Hot 100, and one was on the 1975 Year-End Hot 100.

The Way We Were/Try to Remember

The group joined The Way We Were with Try to Remember in a medley.

Wikipedia credits Tom Jones and Harvey Schmidt with writing Try to Remember.

It reached number 11 on the Hot 100, and it ranks #54 on the 1975 Year-End Hot 100.

Wikipedia lists a 1975 Grammy nomination for it, for Best Pop Vocal Performance.

Part Time Love

Part Time Love, one of the last Gladys Knight songs on this page, reached number 22 on the Hot 100 and number four on the R&B chart in 1975.

It is on no 1970s year-end list, so its peak above is a weekly peak only.

The decade's soul sound has its own page, 70s soul music.

More Hot 100 Entries

The discography lists three more Gladys Knight songs on the Hot 100 from 1975 to 1977.

  • So Sad the Song reached number 47 in 1976, from the soundtrack of Pipe Dreams.
  • Baby, Don't Change Your Mind reached number 52 and number 10 on the R&B chart in 1977, and earned a Grammy nomination.
  • Money reached number 50 in 1975, from the album 2nd Anniversary.

Beyond the Charts: Awards, Television and Contract Fights

The group's work did not stop at the singles chart.

Wikipedia lists these milestones from the decade, alongside the Gladys Knight songs above.

  • 1973: The group left Motown in February and signed with Buddah Records.
  • 1974: Neither One of Us and Midnight Train to Georgia won Grammys.
  • 1974: The Claudine soundtrack, produced by Curtis Mayfield, included On and On.
  • 1975: They won two American Music Awards, Favorite Pop/Rock Band/Duo/Group and Favorite Soul/R&B Band/Duo/Group.
  • 1975: Imagination won Favorite Soul/R&B Album at the American Music Awards.
  • 1975: Their variety series, The Gladys Knight & the Pips Show, aired four episodes on NBC.
  • 1976: They won Favorite Soul/R&B Band/Duo/Group again.
  • 1977: They were in contract disputes with both Motown and Buddah.

From 1978 to 1980, Knight released two solo albums and the Pips released two side albums.

The group signed with Columbia Records in 1980.

Wikipedia says the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inducted them in 1996.

Writers Behind the Hits

Several Gladys Knight songs came from writers with songs of their own.

  • Jim Weatherly: Neither One of Us, Midnight Train to Georgia and Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me.
  • Gerry Goffin and Barry Goldberg: I've Got to Use My Imagination.
  • Curtis Mayfield: On and On.
  • Tony Camillo and Mary Sawyer: I Feel a Song (In My Heart).
  • Pam Sawyer, Clay McMurray and Gloria Jones: If I Were Your Woman.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Gladys Knight's most famous song?

Gladys Knight's most famous song is Midnight Train to Georgia, which she recorded with the Pips in 1973.

It reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 on October 27, 1973, and stayed there for two weeks.

It won the 1974 Grammy for Best R&B Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus.

It entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1999.

How many number one songs did Gladys Knight and the Pips have in the 70s?

Gladys Knight & the Pips had one Hot 100 number one in the 70s, Midnight Train to Georgia, with two weeks at the top in 1973.

They also topped the R&B chart with six songs on this page.

Those include If I Were Your Woman, Neither One of Us and Best Thing That Ever Happened to Me.

Who wrote Midnight Train to Georgia?

Jim Weatherly wrote Midnight Train to Georgia, according to Wikipedia.

He first recorded it himself as Midnight Plane to Houston.

Cissy Houston recorded it as Midnite Train to Georgia in 1973, and Gladys Knight & the Pips kept that title.

Knight wrote that she and Bubba Knight worked with Weatherly to rewrite the lyrics so she would be more comfortable singing them.

Did Gladys Knight and the Pips win a Grammy for Neither One of Us?

Neither One of Us won the Grammy for Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo or Group, at the March 2, 1974 ceremony, according to Wikipedia.

It reached number two on the Hot 100, held from number one by Vicki Lawrence's The Night the Lights Went Out in Georgia.

It was number one on the R&B chart for four weeks from March 17, 1973.

Who were the Pips in Gladys Knight and the Pips?

The Pips were Merald Bubba Knight, William Guest and Edward Patten, who sang with Gladys Knight throughout the 1970s, according to Wikipedia.

Patten had joined in 1959.

The group left Motown in February 1973 after contract talks failed and signed with Buddah Records, where they made Midnight Train to Georgia.
