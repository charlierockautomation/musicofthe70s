Cher's 12 Biggest 1970s Hits, from Gypsys to Take Me Home

By Charlie · Published November 23, 2026 · Updated November 23, 2026

The biggest Cher songs of the 1970s are Gypsys, Tramps & Thieves, Half-Breed and Dark Lady, which each reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100.

Gypsys, Tramps & Thieves topped the chart in November 1971, Half-Breed followed in October 1973, and Dark Lady in March 1974.

This page plays 12 of her solo singles, from 1971 to 1979.

Which Cher Songs Reached No. 1?

  • Three of these songs reached No. 1: Gypsys, Tramps & Thieves for two weeks, Half-Breed for two weeks, and Dark Lady for one week.
  • Five made this site's Billboard year-end lists: Half-Breed at No. 20 in 1973, Gypsys, Tramps & Thieves at No. 39 in 1971, Dark Lady at No. 33 in 1974, Take Me Home at No. 56 in 1979, and The Way of Love at No. 62 in 1972.
  • Every peak here is a weekly Hot 100 peak, not a year-end rank.

The Number One Run, 1971 to 1974

Of all the Cher songs on this list, Gypsys, Tramps & Thieves was the first to reach No. 1, in November 1971, and it stayed two weeks.

Grammy.com says she performed it on The Sonny and Cher Show, and that the song is about the prejudice and poverty traveling Romani people faced.

The same Grammy.com piece reports the show drew 30 million viewers a week.

The Way of Love and Two More from 1972

The Way of Love reached No. 7 in 1972 and ranked No. 62 among that year's biggest hits.

Living in a House Divided peaked at No. 22 and Don't Hide Your Love at No. 46, both in 1972.

The first two reached No. 2 on the Adult Contemporary chart, the highest Adult Contemporary peaks on this list.

Half-Breed

Half-Breed reached No. 1 for two weeks in October 1973 and ranked No. 20 among the year's biggest hits.

Half-Breed reached No. 3 on the Adult Contemporary chart.

It also reached No. 1 in Canada, according to the Wikipedia discography.

Dark Lady

Dark Lady reached No. 1 in March 1974 for one week, and it ranked No. 33 among that year's hits.

Dark Lady was her third solo No. 1 in under three years, counting from November 1971.

Songfacts adds that two videos exist, a live performance on The Sonny & Cher Comedy Hour and an animated version.

Train of Thought and I Saw a Man

Among the Cher songs of 1974, Train of Thought reached No. 27 and No. 9 on the Adult Contemporary chart.

I Saw a Man and He Danced with His Wife peaked at No. 42 the same year.

American Songwriter credits John Robert Durrill, who also wrote Dark Lady, with writing I Saw a Man and He Danced with His Wife.

Pirate and the Disco Turn, 1977 to 1979

The last songs on this list come from 1977 and 1979.

Pirate, from 1977, reached No. 93 and is the weakest chart showing on the list.

Cher returned to the Top 10 in 1979 with Take Me Home, which reached No. 8 and ranked No. 56 among that year's biggest hits.

The Wikipedia discography lists it at No. 2 on the US dance chart.

Take Me Home

Take Me Home was released on Casablanca Records in January 1979, according to SessionDays.

SessionDays calls it the lead single from her 15th album.

Grammy.com calls it a brief 1979 foray into disco that Cher reportedly did not want to make.

Wasn't It Good and Hell on Wheels

Wasn't It Good peaked at No. 49 in 1979, and Hell on Wheels at No. 59.

Both came out in the wake of Take Me Home, and neither reached the Adult Contemporary chart.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote Gypsys, Tramps & Thieves?

Bob Stone wrote it for Cher, and Snuff Garrett produced it, according to Stereogum.

Stereogum adds that the Wrecking Crew session players backed her on the recording.

Who wrote Dark Lady?

John Durrill, a keyboard player in the Ventures, wrote it, as Songfacts and Stereogum both report.

Stereogum says he sent new lyrics by telegram while the Ventures toured Japan.

Who wrote Half-Breed?

Mary Dean and Al Capps wrote it with Cher in mind, according to Songfacts.

Stereogum reports that producer Snuff Garrett held the song for months after planning to quit working with Sonny and Cher.

Who wrote and produced Take Me Home?

Michele Aller and Bob Esty wrote it, and Esty produced it, according to Songfacts.

SessionDays lists Wah Wah Watson on guitar and Ed Greene on drums for the 1979 recording.

Did Cher have another No. 1 after Dark Lady?

Not in the 1970s, since Songfacts calls Dark Lady her last US No. 1 until Believe in 1998.

Songfacts also names Take Me Home as her first big hit since Dark Lady.
