Anni-Frid Lyngstad: Born in Wartime, Found on Live TV

Anni-Frid Lyngstad was born into wartime chaos years before anyone called her Frida.

Her father was a German soldier.

Her mother was a young Norwegian woman who died before Anni-Frid turned two.

None of that history appears anywhere else on this site's own ABBA coverage.

The real story starts in 1945, in a small Norwegian village, decades before ABBA existed.

Anni-Frid Lyngstad Was Born in Wartime Norway

A Real Lebensborn Child

She was born November 15, 1945, in Ballangen, a small village in northern Norway.

Her father, Alfred Haase, was a German army sergeant stationed there during the wartime occupation.

Her mother, Synni Lyngstad, was a young Norwegian woman.

Anni-Frid is widely regarded as the most well-known surviving child born under Germany's wartime Lebensborn program.

Haase left Norway before his daughter was even born.

A New Life in Sweden After Tragedy

Norway offered the family little safety once the war ended.

Children of German soldiers faced real, open hostility there.

Synni fled with her own mother and infant daughter to Sweden in early 1947.

She died of kidney failure months later, only 21 years old.

Anni-Frid wasn't yet two.

Her grandmother raised her in the small town of Torshälla, outside Eskilstuna.

The Talent Contest Win Sweden Almost Missed

Anni-Frid Lyngstad won a national talent competition on the one night nearly all of Sweden happened to be home.

The contest, called New Faces, took place September 3, 1967, at Skansen in Stockholm.

She sang "En ledig dag," a Swedish version of "Weekend in Portofino."

First prize came with a real recording contract from EMI's Swedish label.

It also came with an unplanned appearance that same night.

The show was Hylands Hörna, the country's most popular television program.

September 3, 1967 happened to be Dagen H, the day Sweden switched from driving on the left side of the road to the right.

Officials discouraged driving entirely that day.

Nearly the whole country stayed home and watched television instead.

Anni-Frid Lyngstad's real breakthrough happened in front of the largest possible audience, by pure accident.

She Recorded Her First Single in One Take

EMI signed her almost immediately after that win.

She recorded the vocals for "En ledig dag" on September 11, 1967.

She sang the whole thing in a single take.

She met Benny Andersson two years later, in 1969.

He began producing her records not long after they became a couple.

One of the first songs he produced for her, "Peter Pan," was an early Andersson-Ulvaeus composition.

It went nowhere on the charts.

A later single fared much better.

"Min egen stad," Swedish for "My Own Town," gave her a real number-one hit of her own before ABBA existed at all.

Only five of her pre-ABBA singles became real radio hits, most of them modest ones.

She built a genuine, working career on that small handful of songs.

Her EMI contract ran out in 1972, the same spring ABBA recorded its very first single together.

She signed with Polar Music instead, the label that would carry ABBA's entire career.

The Father She Didn't Meet Until 1977

Anni-Frid Lyngstad grew up believing her father was dead.

Family accounts held that Alfred Haase's ship sank on the trip back to Germany after the war.

He hadn't died at all.

A German magazine published the real story of her origins in 1977, in the middle of ABBA's own biggest fame.

Her half-brother, Peter Haase, saw the story and looked into it himself.

He confirmed his father was the same man.

Anni-Frid and Alfred Haase met in person in Stockholm not long after, decades after he'd left Norway.

Frida ensam: The Solo Album That Outsold Expectations

Anni-Frid Lyngstad kept a real solo career running the entire time ABBA existed.

Her second solo album, Frida ensam, arrived in 1975.

Bandmate Benny Andersson, her fiancé at the time, produced it himself.

This site's own Agnetha Fältskog post already mentions the album's real Swedish chart run.

The specific details behind it haven't been told anywhere else on this site until now.

The album leaned on Swedish-language covers: David Bowie's "Life on Mars?," 10cc's "The Wall Street Shuffle," the Beach Boys' "Wouldn't It Be Nice."

Its real centerpiece was a Swedish-language version of ABBA's own "Fernando," written and produced by Andersson and Björn Ulvaeus.

That version spent nine weeks at number one on Svensktoppen, Sweden's national radio chart.

It was never released as a proper single in Sweden itself, only Norway got a physical copy.

Anyone who wanted it at home had to buy the whole album.

The album itself spent 38 weeks on the Swedish chart, six of them at number one, and earned Lyngstad a real platinum disc.

Frequently Asked Questions

How did Anni-Frid Lyngstad get her big break?

She won a national talent competition called New Faces on September 3, 1967, singing "En ledig dag."

That same night, she made an unplanned appearance on Sweden's most popular television show, Hylands Hörna, which coincided with Dagen H, the day the country switched which side of the road it drove on.

Almost the entire nation was home watching television instead of driving.

Did Anni-Frid Lyngstad ever meet her real father?

She grew up believing he had died at sea after the war, but he hadn't.

A 1977 German magazine story revealed he was still alive, and her half-brother confirmed it directly with him.

Anni-Frid Lyngstad met Alfred Haase in person in Stockholm shortly after, more than three decades after he left Norway.

What was Anni-Frid Lyngstad's solo album Frida ensam?

Frida ensam was her 1975 solo album, produced by bandmate and then-fiancé Benny Andersson.

Its centerpiece was a Swedish-language version of ABBA's own "Fernando," which spent nine weeks at number one on Sweden's Svensktoppen radio chart.

The full album spent 38 weeks on the Swedish chart, six of them at number one.
