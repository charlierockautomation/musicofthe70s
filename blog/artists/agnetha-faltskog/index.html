Agnetha Fältskog: The Solo Star Before ABBA Existed

Agnetha Fältskog topped Sweden's own chart years before ABBA existed at all.

This site's own ABBA coverage names her only as one-quarter of the group.

The real story starts earlier, and runs on its own track alongside the band's.

She was already a real solo star by the time she met her future husband and bandmate.

She kept making solo records the entire time ABBA existed, too.

Agnetha Fältskog Was a Star Before ABBA Existed

A Telephone Operator Who Wrote Songs on the Side

Agnetha Fältskog wrote her very first song at age six.

It was called "Två små troll," Swedish for "Two Small Trolls."

By 16, she was singing in a real local dance band, Bernt Enghardt's.

She worked a day job as a telephone operator at a car company at the same time.

She sent a demo recording made with that same dance band to Cupol Records.

The label wanted her voice, not the band behind it.

They signed her alone in 1967.

A Number One While Still a Teenager

Agnetha Fältskog wrote and recorded her own debut single at seventeen.

"Jag var så kär" was recorded on October 16, 1967, and released the following month through Cupol Records.

She wrote it herself.

The song topped the Swedish chart on January 28, 1968.

It sold more than 80,000 copies.

ABBA didn't form until 1972, a full four years later.

A Real Songwriter, Not Just a Voice

Most female pop singers of that era performed songs written for them.

Fältskog wrote many of her own instead.

Her first two albums, Agnetha Fältskog (1968) and Agnetha Fältskog Vol. 2 (1969), built on that same self-written foundation.

"Om tårar vore guld," a single from her 1970 album Som jag är, reached number three on the Svensktoppen chart.

It stayed on that chart for 15 weeks, between April and July 1970.

Som jag är marked another real turning point, too.

Björn Ulvaeus produced the album himself, his first time working on one of her records.

That creative partnership started years before either of them sang together in ABBA.

How She Met Björn Ulvaeus

Fältskog was already the more established name when she met her future husband.

She first crossed paths with Björn Ulvaeus in 1968, through Sweden's small pop music scene.

He was a member of the Hootenanny Singers at the time.

Benny Andersson, a friend of Björn's, was part of that same circle.

Fältskog and Ulvaeus reconnected in 1969, and the relationship became serious.

They married on July 6, 1971, in the small village of Verum.

Benny Andersson played the organ at the wedding.

Anni-Frid Lyngstad, already close friends with the couple, was there too.

That same circle of friends and couples became ABBA a little more than a year later.

Each of the four had real, separate careers already running by the time that happened.

Fältskog's was the most commercially proven of them all.

She had a genuine, verified number-one hit and a real following before either future bandmate had matched it on their own.

The Solo Album She Made Alongside ABBA's Own Rise

Fältskog didn't stop making solo records once ABBA took off.

She began work on a new solo album, Elva kvinnor i ett hus, in 1973.

A pregnancy that same year, followed by ABBA's own sudden success with Waterloo, pushed the record back again and again.

It finally came out in December 1975.

She wrote nearly every song on it herself, the only one of her albums built entirely that way.

The one exception was a Swedish-language version of ABBA's own "SOS."

Recording it required real discipline: full ABBA rehearsals and tours during the day, her own songs squeezed in whenever time allowed.

The album spent 53 weeks on the Swedish chart despite peaking at a modest number 11.

That run outlasted every ABBA album released up to that point.

The title translates to "Eleven Women in a House."

Bandmate Anni-Frid Lyngstad was working on a strikingly similar project of her own at the very same time.

Her own solo album, Frida ensam, actually reached number one in Sweden, recorded during those same stretched-thin ABBA years.

Two members of the same globally famous group were quietly building real solo careers in parallel, largely unnoticed outside Scandinavia.

This site's own ABBA post covers the band's name origin and its members' real marriages and divorces in depth.

Its own Dancing Queen post gives her one brief line about the song's own certainty.

Neither one spends real time on the solo career that came before, and ran alongside, the band itself.

None of it made her a global name.

Outside Sweden, almost nobody knew "Jag var så kär" or Elva kvinnor i ett hus existed.

ABBA gave her that international reach; her own solo records never got the chance to.

The songwriting instinct behind both careers was the same one, years apart.

Frequently Asked Questions

Was Agnetha Fältskog famous before ABBA?

She was already one of Sweden's biggest solo pop stars years before ABBA existed.

Her self-written debut single, "Jag var så kär," topped the Swedish chart on January 28, 1968, selling more than 80,000 copies.

ABBA didn't form until 1972, four years after that first number one.

How did Agnetha Fältskog meet Björn Ulvaeus?

They first met in 1968 through Sweden's small pop music scene and reconnected in 1969.

Björn was a member of the Hootenanny Singers at the time, working alongside Benny Andersson.

The couple married on July 6, 1971, in the village of Verum, with Andersson playing the organ at their wedding.

Did Agnetha Fältskog release solo music during ABBA?

She released the solo album Elva kvinnor i ett hus in December 1975, recorded in fragments between 1973 and 1975 around ABBA's own busy schedule.

It was the only album where she wrote nearly every song herself.

It spent 53 weeks on the Swedish chart, longer than any ABBA album had managed up to that point.

What was Agnetha Fältskog's biggest pre-ABBA hit?

"Jag var så kär" was her biggest, a self-written number one from January 1968.

"Om tårar vore guld" followed in 1970, reaching number three on the Svensktoppen chart and staying there for 15 weeks.

Both songs came years before she ever sang a note as part of ABBA.
