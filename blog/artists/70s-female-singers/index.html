70s Female Singers: The Biggest Solo Women, by the Charts

By Charlie · Published September 29, 2026

Olivia Newton-John was the most successful of all the 70s female singers by the charts.

She placed 8 solo singles on Billboard's year-end Hot 100 between 1971 and 1979.

Helen Reddy and Anne Murray were next, with 7 and 6.

This page ranks every solo woman with at least two year-end hits in the decade.

That's 22 singers, each with their number ones, weeks at the top, and biggest hit.

Every one of those biggest hits can be played right from the table.

The ten solo women with the most Billboard year-end Hot 100 hits, 1970 to 1979.

The Short Answer: The Top 10 70s Female Singers

Ranked by solo singles on the Billboard year-end Hot 100, these were the decade's ten biggest names:

  1. Olivia Newton-John: 8
  2. Helen Reddy: 7
  3. Anne Murray: 6
  4. Donna Summer: 5
  5. Diana Ross: 5
  6. Cher: 5
  7. Carly Simon: 5
  8. Aretha Franklin: 5
  9. Linda Ronstadt: 5
  10. Barbra Streisand: 4

Ties among 70s female singers are broken by chart points, so a higher-ranked song counts for more.

That's why Donna Summer sits above the five other women tied with five hits apiece.

Three of her five hits landed in the top 12 of 1979 alone, "Bad Girls," "Hot Stuff" and "MacArthur Park."

High rank matters more than raw count once the tiebreaker kicks in.

The year-end list is Billboard's own summary of each year's biggest singles.

Landing on it five or more times across a ten-year span is a real career, not a moment.

Why the Bar Had to Drop: Two Hits, Not Three

The 70s Singers page ranks every solo act, men and women together, with a three-hit qualifying bar.

Only 11 women clear that same three-hit bar, out of 48 acts total on that page.

Built the same way here, this page would be a top-11 list, not a real ranking.

So the bar for this page drops to two year-end hits instead of three.

The Eleven Names That Bar Adds

Dropping the bar to two adds eleven more women, each with a genuine multi-year or multi-song run on the year-end chart.

None of these are one-song flukes either, every name here charted in at least two different years or with two different singles.

Gloria Gaynor's two hits bookend the decade, "Never Can Say Goodbye" in 1975 and "I Will Survive" in 1979.

Roberta Flack's Six Weeks: The Longest Run by Any Woman

Roberta Flack ranks 11th on this list of 70s female singers, with just three year-end hits.

But she holds a record none of the women above her can touch.

"The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face" spent six weeks at number one in the spring of 1972.

That's the longest run at the top by any female singer in this entire decade.

"Killing Me Softly with His Song" then added five more weeks in 1973.

Those two songs alone give her 12 combined weeks at number one, more than any other woman on this list.

Why the Hits-Count Rank Doesn't Show This

Year-end hits reward a woman for showing up on the list, however far down.

Weeks at number one reward the monster hit instead.

Measured that way, Roberta Flack's 12 weeks beat Donna Summer's 11, even though Summer placed five year-end hits to Flack's three.

Two of Summer's three number-one weeks came from "Hot Stuff" and "Bad Girls" during her 1979 run.

Diana Ross is third by weeks, with 7 across two different songs.

Aretha Franklin's Whole Run Happened Early

All five of Aretha Franklin's year-end hits landed between 1970 and 1974.

"Bridge over Troubled Water" and "Spanish Harlem" both charted in 1971 alone.

She doesn't appear on the year-end list again after "Until You Come Back to Me" in 1974.

That doesn't mean her career stopped, only that her year-end chart run was entirely front-loaded.

Linda Ronstadt Was a Late-70s Story Only

Every one of Linda Ronstadt's five year-end hits falls between 1975 and 1979.

"When Will I Be Loved" and "You're No Good" both arrived in 1975, the same year she broke through.

"Blue Bayou" followed in 1978, one of the most enduring singles on this whole list.

She's the only woman in the top 10 with zero hits before 1975.

1974 and 1979 Were the Only Three-Hit Years

Olivia Newton-John placed three singles on the 1974 year-end list alone.

"Let Me Be There," "If You Love Me (Let Me Know)" and "I Honestly Love You" all charted that year.

Five years later, Donna Summer matched that with "Bad Girls," "Hot Stuff" and "MacArthur Park" in 1979.

No woman on this list has ever placed four in a single year.

Those two three-hit years bookend the entire ranking's most crowded stretch.

Women Who Charted as Bands, Not Solo

Plenty of major 70s women never appear on this page at all.

That's because they charted as part of a group credit, not under their own name.

  • Karen Carpenter charted as half of The Carpenters, 11 year-end hits, second place on the 70s Bands page.
  • Gladys Knight charted as Gladys Knight & the Pips, 7 year-end hits, eighth place on that same page.
  • Stevie Nicks and Christine McVie both charted as Fleetwood Mac, 5 year-end hits.

The full group rankings, with the same year-end method used here, are on the 70s Bands page.

Four Women's Groups That Clear Neither Bar

Some all-female and mostly-female groups land in an odd gap between the two pages.

The Pointer Sisters, Sister Sledge, Honey Cone and Silver Convention each placed exactly two year-end hits as a group.

Two hits is enough to qualify a solo woman for this page.

It is not enough to clear the Bands page's three-hit bar.

So none of those four groups show up in either ranking, a real gap in what chart-only counting can capture.

How This Ranking Was Built

Every number on this page comes from two of this site's own Billboard data files.

  • The year-end Hot 100 file, 100 songs for each year from 1970 to 1979.
  • The weekly number one file, one record for every week of the decade.

To check any figure yourself, Billboard's charts are reproduced year by year on Wikipedia.

  • The year-end lists, for example the Billboard Year-End Hot 100 singles of 1979.
  • The weekly chart toppers, in the list of Billboard Hot 100 number ones of the 1970s.

The Counting Rules

  • Only credits under a woman's own solo name count toward the ranking.
  • Group credits, like Karen Carpenter's records as The Carpenters, are counted on the companion 70s Bands page instead.
  • Shared credits with a male singer, like "You're the One That I Want," are shown separately and not counted for either name.
  • The qualifying bar is 2 or more year-end hits, lower than the 70s Singers page's 3-hit bar, since only 11 women clear that higher bar.
  • Ties on year-end hits are broken by chart points: 101 minus the year-end rank, added up across every hit.

Where the Weekly Data Comes In

Number ones and weeks at number one are counted straight from the weekly rows.

That's why Roberta Flack's full 12 weeks show up even though she has only three year-end hits.

It's also why a singer like Aretha Franklin can have five year-end hits and zero weeks on top.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who was the biggest female singer of the 70s?

Olivia Newton-John was the biggest female singer of the 1970s by Billboard's year-end charts. She placed 8 solo singles on the year-end Hot 100 between 1971 and 1979, more than any other woman charting under her own name. Helen Reddy was second with 7, and Anne Murray third with 6.

Which 70s female singer had the most weeks at number one?

Roberta Flack spent more combined weeks at number one than any other 70s female singer, 12 in total. "The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face" held the top spot for six weeks in 1972, and "Killing Me Softly with His Song" added five more in 1973. She ranks 11th by total year-end hits but first by weeks on top.

Why isn't Karen Carpenter or Stevie Nicks on this list of 70s female singers?

This page ranks women who charted under their own solo name. Karen Carpenter charted as half of The Carpenters and Stevie Nicks as a member of Fleetwood Mac, both group credits counted on the companion 70s Bands page instead. Counting them again here would double-count the same hits under two different rankings.

What's the difference between this list and the 70s Singers list?

This page is the female subset of the 70s Singers ranking, using the same Billboard year-end data and the same counting method. The qualifying bar is lower here, two year-end hits instead of three, because only 11 women clear the three-hit bar that qualifies 48 acts on the full Singers page.

How is this 70s female singers ranking calculated?

Each woman is ranked by how many solo singles she placed on Billboard's year-end Hot 100 from 1970 to 1979, using this site's own chart data. Ties are broken by chart points, which is 101 minus each song's year-end rank, added up. Group credits and duets are shown separately and not counted toward the total.
