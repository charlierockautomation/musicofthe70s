Elton John: 13 Hits Played, 6 at Number One

By Charlie · Published October 10, 2026

▶ Jump to the playable list

Elton John put 13 songs on the 1970s Year-End Hot 100, and six of them reached number one.

Those six songs held the top spot for 15 weeks, and none of them repeated a run.

His best year-end rank came on a duet, not a solo record.

This page plays all 13 songs, then follows the stories behind the biggest ones.

Three Ways to Read the Same Career

Year-end rank and weekly number ones are different measures, and his catalog shows it.

  • Philadelphia Freedom ranked #3 for 1975, his best solo rank.
  • Island Girl held number one for 3 weeks, yet sits at #65 on the 1976 year-end list.
  • Daniel ranks #48 for 1973 and never appears in this site's weekly number one data.

Island Girl's run began on Nov 1, 1975, so it topped the weekly chart a year before its year-end entry.

The Road to 1972

Two Strangers and One Ad

Elton John and lyricist Bernie Taupin first met in 1967.

They had both answered the same advertisement.

Wikipedia calls it a decades-long partnership, and it is the spine of nearly every song on the list above.

The Troubadour and a New Name

His first American concert was at the Troubadour in Los Angeles on August 25, 1970.

By January 7, 1972, he had legally changed his name to Elton Hercules John.

That same year Honky Chateau became the first of seven number one albums in a row.

Rocket Man followed in 1972 and landed at #40 on that year's year-end list.

This site's weekly data shows six Elton John songs reaching number one on the Hot 100 in the 1970s.

Together they held the top spot for 15 weeks.

No song of his repeated a run, and each credit line was slightly different.

The Early Run: Crocodile Rock and Bennie and the Jets

Crocodile Rock began its 3 weeks at number one on Feb 3, 1973.

Bennie and the Jets took one week at the top, starting Apr 13, 1974.

Both landed inside the top ten of their year-end lists, at #7 and #9.

Both also appear on the full list of 70s number one hits.

One Act, Three Credit Lines

The weekly data credits his number ones three ways.

Most are listed under his own name.

Philadelphia Freedom is credited to the Elton John Band.

Don't Go Breaking My Heart lists both singers, with Kiki Dee second.

1975: Three Number Ones in One Year

He spent 7 weeks at number one in 1975 across three different songs.

That was more than any other act that year, according to this site's weekly data notes.

Lucy and the Promise Lennon Kept

Elton John released his cover of Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds on November 15, 1974.

John Lennon played guitar and sang backing vocals under the name Dr. Winston O'Boogie.

Lennon had promised to join him on stage if Whatever Gets You Thru the Night reached number one.

It did, and Lennon kept his word on Thanksgiving night, November 28, 1974, at Madison Square Garden.

The Lucy single then took two weeks at number one, starting Jan 4, 1975.

Philadelphia Freedom for Billie Jean King

Elton John asked Bernie Taupin to write Philadelphia Freedom as a favor to tennis star Billie Jean King.

King played for the Philadelphia Freedoms, a professional tennis team.

Wikipedia says she did not want the song to be about tennis.

It was credited to the Elton John Band and produced by Gus Dudgeon.

It began two weeks at number one on Apr 12, 1975, then ranked #3 on the 1975 year-end list.

Don't Go Breaking My Heart: Two Singers, Two Cities

The Kiki Dee duet gave him his longest run, four weeks at number one starting Aug 7, 1976.

It ranked #2 on the 1976 year-end list, behind only Wings.

He and Taupin wrote it under the pseudonyms Ann Orson and Carte Blanche.

Wikipedia calls it an affectionate pastiche of the Motown style.

Recorded Apart

The band and his vocals were recorded in March 1976 at Eastern Sound in Toronto.

Kiki Dee's vocals were recorded separately at Marquee Studios in London.

The song had first been offered to Dusty Springfield, but the offer was withdrawn.

It was released June 25, 1976, and Wikipedia says it also spent six weeks at number one in the UK.

Seven Number One Albums in a Row

Between 1972 and 1975, he put seven albums at number one on the US album chart in a row.

Wikipedia names these as the run:

  1. Honky Chateau (1972)
  2. Don't Shoot Me I'm Only the Piano Player (1973)
  3. Goodbye Yellow Brick Road (1973)
  4. Caribou (1974)
  5. Elton John's Greatest Hits (1974)
  6. Captain Fantastic and the Brown Dirt Cowboy (1975)
  7. Rock of the Westies (1975)

Captain Fantastic debuted at number one, which Wikipedia says no album had done before.

It stayed there seven weeks.

Albums are a separate chart from the singles above, so none of these counts toward the weeks at number one in this post.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many number one hits did Elton John have in the 1970s?

Elton John reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 six times in the 1970s, based on this site's weekly chart data.

Those songs spent 15 weeks at the top between February 1973 and September 1976.

They were Crocodile Rock, Bennie and the Jets, Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds, Philadelphia Freedom, Island Girl, and Don't Go Breaking My Heart.

What was Elton John's biggest song of the 1970s on the year-end chart?

Elton John's highest Year-End Hot 100 rank of the 1970s was #2 for 1976, with the Kiki Dee duet Don't Go Breaking My Heart.

Under his own name, Philadelphia Freedom ranked highest, at #3 on the 1975 year-end list.

Year-end rank measures a whole year of chart performance, not a weekly peak.

Did John Lennon play on an Elton John record?

John Lennon played guitar and sang backing vocals on Elton John's 1974 cover of Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds.

He used the pseudonym Dr. Winston O'Boogie on the record.

The single reached number one on the Hot 100 for two weeks in January 1975, according to this site's weekly data and Wikipedia.

How many number one albums in a row did Elton John have in the 1970s?

Elton John had seven consecutive number one albums in the United States between 1972 and 1975.

The run started with Honky Chateau in 1972 and ended with Rock of the Westies in 1975.

Wikipedia lists the albums in its article on him, linked in the sources below.

When did Elton John change his name?

Elton John legally changed his name to Elton Hercules John on January 7, 1972, according to Wikipedia.

That was the year his first number one album, Honky Chateau, came out.

His first American concert had been at the Troubadour in Los Angeles on August 25, 1970.
