Michael Jackson Songs of the 70s: 12 Tracks You Can Play

By Charlie · Published November 21, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026

The biggest Michael Jackson songs of the 1970s are Ben, which spent a week at No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1972, and Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough, which did the same in 1979.

Jackson's solo career began in 1971, and Rockin' Robin reached No. 2 in 1972, months before Ben went to No. 1.

This page plays 12 solo tracks from his five 1970s albums, from Motown in 1971 to Off the Wall in 1979.

The Motown Solo Years, 1971 to 1975

The first Michael Jackson songs released under his own name, starting with Got to Be There in 1971, came out on Motown, and that single peaked at No. 4 on the Hot 100.

Elliot Willensky wrote it, and American Songwriter reports it launched his solo career and gave its name to his debut solo album.

That album, Got to Be There, was released on January 24, 1972, and reached No. 14 on the Billboard 200.

Rockin' Robin and I Wanna Be Where You Are

Rockin' Robin is a cover of the 1958 hit by Bobby Day, and Jackson's version reached No. 2 in 1972.

It ranked No. 41 among that year's Billboard year-end hits.

I Wanna Be Where You Are, written by Arthur "T-Boy" Ross and Leon Ware, peaked at No. 16 the same year.

Ben

Ben was the theme of the 1972 horror film of the same name, and Jackson's version won the Golden Globe for Best Original Song and was nominated for the Oscar.

American Songwriter says Jackson recorded it at Motown's Los Angeles studio in October 1971.

Lee Montgomery sings the song in the film itself, and Jackson's recording plays over the end credits.

The single reached No. 1 for a week in October 1972, and it ranked No. 20 among the year's biggest hits.

Music & Me and Forever, Michael

With a Child's Heart, from the 1973 album Music & Me, reached No. 50, and that album peaked at No. 92.

The 1975 album Forever, Michael, released on January 16, 1975, reached No. 101.

Its singles We're Almost There and Just a Little Bit of You, both written by Eddie and Brian Holland, reached No. 54 and No. 23.

Off the Wall and the Move to Epic, 1979

The Michael Jackson songs of 1979 all come from Off the Wall, which came out on August 10, 1979, and was his first album after leaving Motown for Epic.

Grammy.com reports that Quincy Jones produced it and that Jackson co-produced three tracks, including the opener.

The album reached No. 3 on the Billboard 200 and has been certified Diamond in the United States.

Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough

Jackson wrote Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough himself, and it was released on July 10, 1979.

It spent one week at No. 1 in October 1979 and ranked No. 91 among that year's biggest hits.

At the 22nd Grammy Awards in 1980, Grammy.com says, it earned him his first career Grammy, for Best R&B Vocal Performance, Male.

Rock with You and the Title Track

Rod Temperton, a former keyboard player in Heatwave, wrote Rock with You, and Stereogum reports Quincy Jones flew him to Los Angeles to work on the album.

The single came out on October 9, 1979, and went on to top the Hot 100 the following winter.

The title track, Off the Wall, followed in November 1979 and peaked at No. 10.

Workin' Day and Night and Get on the Floor are two more tracks from the same album.

The Four Top 10 Singles

Off the Wall was the first album by a solo artist to produce four Billboard Hot 100 Top 10 hits, according to Grammy.com.

The fourth was She's Out of My Life, released in April 1980, which also peaked at No. 10.

What Came Right After the 70s

Two of the Off the Wall singles ran into 1980, and that is where this list stops.

Those later releases sit outside this site's decade, so the list above ends with the 1979 album.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Michael Jackson songs spent the longest at No. 1?

Stereogum reports that Rock with You reached No. 1 on January 19, 1980 and stayed four weeks.

No other single on this list stayed at the top that long.

Who wrote Ben?

Walter Scharf wrote the music and Don Black the lyrics, as the Academy's Oscars site credits them.

American Songwriter reports the song was first meant for Donny Osmond, who was on tour, so it went to Jackson at 13.

Who wrote Rockin' Robin?

Leon Rene wrote it under the name Jimmie Thomas, according to Motown Classic.

Songfacts adds that Rene was inspired by a mockingbird that kept singing at him.

Did Michael Jackson write Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough?

Yes, Songfacts calls it the first chart-topper he wrote himself.

Greg Phillinganes is credited with the rhythm arrangement, and Jackson overdubbed his own voice several times to build the harmonies.
