70s Bands: The 47 Biggest Groups and Every Band by Genre

By Charlie · Published September 28, 2026 · Updated November 9, 2026

The biggest 70s bands by the charts were led by the Bee Gees, with 12 Billboard year-end Hot 100 hits between 1971 and 1979.

The Bee Gees also had the most No. 1 hits of any group, 9, for 27 weeks at the top.

Wings and the Carpenters came next, with 11 year-end hits each.

Dawn, Grand Funk and Dr. Hook

Three more acts appear under two names in the chart data.

  • Dawn, Dawn featuring Tony Orlando, and Tony Orlando and Dawn are one group: 5 year-end hits and 3 No. 1s, for 10 weeks at the top.
  • Grand Funk Railroad shortened its name to Grand Funk, and both No. 1s, "We're an American Band" and "The Loco-Motion," carry the short name.
  • Dr. Hook & the Medicine Show became Dr. Hook partway through the decade, so the two credits are one entry with 6 year-end hits.

Sly & the Family Stone also appears as "Sly and the Family Stone" in the weekly data, for "Family Affair," and both spellings count as one band.

The Carpenters Never Missed a Year Early On

The Carpenters placed at least one song on every year-end list from 1970 through 1975.

Nine of their eleven year-end hits landed in the first half of the decade.

"(They Long to Be) Close to You" was their breakthrough, finishing No. 2 for 1970.

Disco Rebuilt the Late-70s Leaderboard

Ten of the Bee Gees' twelve year-end hits came from 1975 on.

Earth, Wind & Fire and Eagles reached the same chart from very different directions, funk horns on one side and California harmony on the other.

Nine No. 1s, 27 Weeks

The Bee Gees spent 27 combined weeks at No. 1, across nine different singles.

"Night Fever" alone held the top spot for eight weeks in 1978.

"Stayin' Alive" and "How Can You Mend a Broken Heart" each spent four weeks there.

No other band in this data comes within half of that total.

How This Ranking Was Built

Every number on this page comes from two of this site's Billboard data files.

  • The year-end Hot 100 file, 100 songs for each year from 1970 to 1979.
  • The weekly No. 1 file, one record for every week of the decade.

The Counting Rules

  • Only group and duo credits count here, and solo voices have their own ranking.
  • Credits that name the same band are merged into one entry, as shown above.
  • A one-off pairing of two solo acts is not treated as a band.
  • Ties on year-end hits are broken by chart points: 101 minus the year-end rank, added up across every hit.

The ten biggest year-end hits of each year, across every act, are ranked on the 70s songs page.

Frequently Asked Questions

When were the Bee Gees inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame?

The three Gibb brothers entered the Hall as the Bee Gees in 1997.

Their induction plaque carries the signatures of Barry, Robin and Maurice Gibb, the lineup behind all nine of the group's 70s No. 1s.

ABBA, number 16 in the ranking above, followed them into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2010.

What Grammys did the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack win?

The soundtrack won Album of the Year at the 21st Grammy Awards.

The Bee Gees also won Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus for "Saturday Night Fever" and Best Arrangement for Voices for "Stayin' Alive."

They shared Producer of the Year with Albhy Galuten and Karl Richardson the same night.

Did the Carpenters win Best New Artist?

Yes, the Carpenters won Best New Artist at the 13th Grammy Awards, the 1971 telecast.

The same night they won Best Contemporary Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus for "Close to You."

They were also nominated for Record of the Year and Album of the Year, both for Close to You.

What was ABBA's only No. 1 hit in the United States?

"Dancing Queen" was ABBA's only No. 1 on the Hot 100, for one week starting April 9, 1977.

Benny Andersson, Björn Ulvaeus and Stig Anderson wrote it, and it came out as a single in Sweden on August 16, 1976.

The song is from Arrival, ABBA's fourth studio album.
