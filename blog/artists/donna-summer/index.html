Donna Summer: The 1977 Hit Too Influential for the Charts

Donna Summer spent the late 1970s stacking number one singles like almost no other woman in chart history.

Four different songs reached the top of the Billboard Hot 100 between late 1978 and the end of 1979.

Three of them landed in the same calendar year, a real first for a female artist.

But her most influential recording never got anywhere close to number one.

"I Feel Love" peaked at number 6 and doesn't show up anywhere in this site's own 1977 year-end chart data.

David Bowie said Brian Eno called it the sound of the future the first time he heard it.

This is the real, chart-verified story of Donna Summer's 1970s.

It's built from Billboard's own year-end, weekly, and R&B album data, cross-checked against independent chart history.

1974-1976: A Session Singer in Munich Becomes a Star

From Hair to Munich's Session Booth

Donna Summer left Boston as a teenager to join a European touring production of the musical Hair.

She settled in Munich and spent years working as a session singer around the city's studios.

There she met producers Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte at Musicland Studios.

The pair released her first album, Lady of the Night, in 1974 for the European market only.

The Seventeen-Minute Single Neil Bogart Made Longer

In 1975, Moroder brought a slow, moaning demo track to Casablanca Records president Neil Bogart in Los Angeles.

Bogart played it on repeat at a party in his own home that night.

He called Moroder back and asked for a longer version, something closer to twenty minutes.

Summer, Moroder, and Bellotte returned with a seventeen-minute recording instead.

Casablanca released the edited single as "Love to Love You Baby" in November 1975.

It reached number 2 on the Billboard Hot 100 and ranked 41st on this site's own 1976 year-end chart.

The BBC and several American radio stations refused to play it because of the extended vocal.

The controversy only pushed the song further into the wider conversation.

1977: The Hit Too Influential for the Charts

An Entirely Synthesized Backing Track

Summer's fifth studio album, I Remember Yesterday, closed with a track meant to sound like the future.

Moroder built its entire backing track from a Moog synthesizer, with no live band involved at all.

The Moog itself drifted out of tune every twenty or thirty seconds during the Munich session.

Moroder and his engineers worked in short bursts, retuning the machine between each pass.

"I Feel Love" reached record stores on July 2, 1977.

It sits alongside the wider genre history covered in Disco Music of the 70s, the same year this site's own chart data shows the sound taking over.

The Sound Bowie and Eno Heard First

David Bowie later wrote that producer Brian Eno ran into their Berlin studio one day.

Eno told him he had heard the sound of the future.

He played "I Feel Love" and said it would change club music for the next fifteen years.

Bowie called that prediction more or less right.

Despite that reputation, the song only reached number 6 on the Billboard Hot 100.

It never appears anywhere in this site's own year-end Hot 100 data for 1977.

The recording now widely credited as the true starting point of electronic dance music never cracked that year's biggest chart at all.

1978: An Oscar for a Song from a B-Movie

A Song Written for a B-Movie

Songwriter Paul Jabara wrote "Last Dance" for the 1978 film Thank God It's Friday.

Summer's recording reached number 3 on the Billboard Hot 100.

It ranked 34th on this site's own 1978 year-end chart.

An Oscar and a Grammy in the Same Year

"Last Dance" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

It also won the Grammy for Best R&B Vocal Performance, Female.

A song written for a disco-club subplot in a mostly forgotten film ended up an Academy Award winner.

1979: Three Number Ones, One Calendar Year

Village People turned disco into costume and spectacle that same era, but Summer's 1979 was built on pure chart volume instead.

MacArthur Park's Real Place in the Timeline

Summer and Moroder turned Jimmy Webb's 1968 song into a seventeen-minute disco epic for the Live and More album.

The edited single reached number one on the Hot 100 in November 1978 and held the spot for three weeks.

This site's own year-end data files the song's own fun fact as her "third number one of 1978 to 1979."

Independently verified chart history shows the real order runs the other way.

MacArthur Park was actually Summer's first Hot 100 number one, not her third.

Her real number one run started here, then kept going through the rest of the decade.

Three Number Ones, One Calendar Year

"Hot Stuff" reached number one in June 1979 for three weeks, broken up by one week when the Bee Gees pushed it aside.

Moroder added rock guitar to the track at Summer's own insistence.

It became the first disco record to win the Grammy for Best Rock Vocal Performance, Female.

The session behind that guitar part, and the interrupted chart run, is the subject of a separate Donna Summer Hot Stuff post.

"Bad Girls" followed in July, holding number one for five weeks.

It also made Summer the first artist since the Beatles to hold two simultaneous top-five albums on the Billboard 200.

"No More Tears (Enough Is Enough)," her duet with Barbra Streisand, reached number one that November.

Those three songs, all hitting number one in the same calendar year, made Summer the first woman to pull off that run.

Donna Summer's Real 1970s Chart Record

Her decade breaks down into six verified Hot 100 entries, each pulled directly from this site's own Billboard data.

  • "Love to Love You Baby" (1975): Hot 100 peak #2, ranked 41st on the site's 1976 year-end chart.
  • "I Feel Love" (1977): Hot 100 peak #6, absent from the site's 1977 year-end chart entirely.
  • "Last Dance" (1978): Hot 100 peak #3, ranked 34th on the site's 1978 year-end chart, won an Academy Award and a Grammy.
  • "MacArthur Park" (1978): Hot 100 #1 for three weeks, ranked 12th on the site's 1979 year-end chart, her first Hot 100 number one.
  • "Hot Stuff" (1979): Hot 100 #1 for three weeks, ranked 7th on the site's 1979 year-end chart.
  • "Bad Girls" (1979): Hot 100 #1 for five weeks, ranked 2nd on the site's 1979 year-end chart, its album spent three weeks at #1 on Billboard's R&B albums chart.

Four of those six songs reached number one on the Hot 100.

The one that changed music the most never came close.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Donna Summer's biggest 1970s hit?

Donna Summer's biggest 1970s hit is "Bad Girls," which spent five weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in the summer of 1979.

It ranked second on this site's own 1979 year-end chart.

The song also made her the first artist since the Beatles to hold two simultaneous top-five albums on the Billboard 200.

Did I Feel Love reach number one on the Hot 100?

"I Feel Love" peaked at number 6 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1977, so it never reached number one.

It never appears on this site's own year-end Hot 100 data for that year at all.

Despite that modest chart run, Brian Eno reportedly called it the sound of the future the first time he heard it.

It's now widely credited as the record that started electronic dance music.

How many number-one singles did Donna Summer have in the 1970s?

Donna Summer had four number-one singles on the Billboard Hot 100 in the 1970s.

"MacArthur Park" hit number one in November 1978, followed by "Hot Stuff" and "Bad Girls" in the summer of 1979.

Her fourth, "No More Tears (Enough Is Enough)," a duet with Barbra Streisand, reached number one that November.

Why was Love to Love You Baby controversial?

"Love to Love You Baby" built its seventeen-minute album version around Donna Summer's extended, breathy vocal moaning over a steady disco groove.

The BBC and several American radio stations refused to play it because of that vocal.

The edited single still reached number 2 on the Billboard Hot 100 in early 1976.

The controversy only pushed the song further into the wider cultural conversation.

What record did Donna Summer set in 1979?

In 1979, Donna Summer became the first woman to reach number one on the Billboard Hot 100 with three different singles in the same calendar year.

Those songs were "Hot Stuff," "Bad Girls," and "No More Tears (Enough Is Enough)," her duet with Barbra Streisand.

All three hit number one between June and November of that year.
