70s Soul Songs: 195 Hits You Can Play in One List

By Charlie · Published July 29, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

The biggest 70s soul songs on the Billboard charts are The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face by Roberta Flack, the top song of the 1972 Year-End Hot 100 with 6 weeks at No. 1, and her Killing Me Softly with His Song, with 5 more weeks on top in 1973.

Soul's biggest chart year was 1970, when 29 soul records made the Year-End Hot 100.

This page lists all 195, from the Jackson 5 to Earth, Wind & Fire, and 195 of them play right here in one continuous list.

What Makes a 70s Song Soul

Soul is Black American pop built on gospel singing and rhythm and blues, and in the 70s it grew lusher, more political and more personal than the 60s hits that came before.

Most of the records in the list share a few things:

  • A lead voice that preaches, pleads or testifies, straight out of church.
  • Call-and-response between the lead and the backing singers.
  • A live rhythm section, usually bass, drums, guitar and keys.
  • Horns, and on many records, sweeping strings.
  • Songs about love, and more and more about the world outside.

The tag in this site's data marks where a record sits, not every song with a soulful singer.

Sara Smile by Daryl Hall & John Oates and Mama Can't Buy You Love by Elton John both carry the soul tag here.

Funk and disco records have their own tags, so James Brown and Donna Summer sit on other pages.

Motown Lets Its Stars Write

Motown spent the 60s turning out hits from staff writers and producers, with the singers following the plan.

In the 70s its two biggest male stars took the controls.

What's Going On

Marvin Gaye's What's Going On album came out on May 21, 1971.

It was the first Gaye album to credit him as producer.

Berry Gordy held back the title song at first, worried that a political record would cost Gaye his pop audience.

Motown staff shipped it to stores without his go-ahead, and it reached No. 2 on the Hot 100.

Its next two singles, Mercy Mercy Me and Inner City Blues, also made the top 10.

Stevie Wonder's Run

Stevie Wonder placed six songs on the year-end lists in the 70s, and two of them reached No. 1.

You Are the Sunshine of My Life topped the chart in 1973, and Sir Duke followed in 1977.

Living for the City and Don't You Worry 'bout a Thing both made the 1974 year-end list.

Philadelphia Builds a Sound

Kenny Gamble and Leon Huff founded Philadelphia International Records in 1971, with their longtime partner Thom Bell.

The Philly sound put lush strings and horns over a driving rhythm section.

The label's roster included The O'Jays, Harold Melvin & the Blue Notes, Billy Paul, MFSB and Lou Rawls.

From 1971 to the early 1980s it released more than 170 gold and platinum records.

TSOP and Soul Train

Gamble and Huff wrote TSOP (The Sound of Philadelphia) as the theme for the TV show Soul Train.

MFSB recorded it with the Three Degrees on vocals, and in 1974 it became the first TV theme song to reach No. 1 on the Hot 100.

The sound did not stop at Philadelphia's own acts.

Its strings and steady beat set up much of what disco would do a few years later.

Memphis: Hi Records and Shaft

Al Green and Willie Mitchell

Producer Willie Mitchell began working with Al Green at Hi Records in 1968.

Their records ran on horns, a tight groove and gospel organ, with Green's voice kept soft and close.

The early 70s run of hits included Tired of Being Alone, I'm Still in Love with You and Call Me.

The biggest was Let's Stay Together, a No. 1 in 1972.

The Rock & Roll Hall of Fame inducted Green in 1995.

Theme from Shaft

Isaac Hayes recorded Theme from Shaft for Stax Records' Enterprise label.

It reached No. 1 on the Hot 100 in November 1971.

The next year it won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Hayes became the first African American to win an Oscar in a category other than acting.

Disco, Quiet Storm and the Late 70s

Soul did not disappear at the end of the decade, it changed shape and changed names.

Its dance side moved onto the disco charts, and Southern soul lost much of its audience as disco took over.

Quiet Storm

Its slow side found a home on late-night radio.

In 1976 Melvin Lindsey, a Howard University student at WHUR-FM in Washington, D.C., started a show of slow, romantic soul records.

The format, called quiet storm, spread to Black radio stations across the country within a few years.

Songs like After the Love Has Gone and The Closer I Get to You show where the sound was heading as the 80s began.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who won the Grammy for Record of the Year two years in a row in the 70s?

Roberta Flack won Record of the Year at both the 15th and the 16th Grammy Awards, held in 1973 and 1974.

The winning records were her two longest-running weekly chart-toppers, with 11 weeks at No. 1 between them.

At the first of those ceremonies she beat Alone Again (Naturally), American Pie and Song Sung Blue.

Why is the Soul Train theme called TSOP?

Soul Train creator and host Don Cornelius refused to let the single name the show, so the songwriters picked an alternate title for the release.

It won the Grammy for Best R&B Instrumental Performance in 1975.

The Philadelphia Phillies still play it at Citizens Bank Park before every home game.

How long did Marvin Gaye take to record the What's Going On album?

After the title song became a hit, Berry Gordy gave Gaye about 30 days to finish an album, in effect handing him the right to produce his own records.

Gaye recorded the rest of it in ten working days, from March 17 to March 30, 1971.

Rhythm tracks were cut at Hitsville, and strings, horns and vocals at Golden World.

Where did Al Green record his 70s hits?

He recorded them at Willie Mitchell's Royal Studios, a renovated movie theater at 1320 South Lauderdale Street in South Memphis.

The house band was a group of local session players known as the Hi Rhythm Section.

Hi Records was distributed nationally by London Records.

Did Isaac Hayes get to play Shaft?

No, though Hayes told NPR in 2000 that the film's producer, Joel Freeman, had promised him an audition for the lead in return for writing the score.

The role went to Richard Roundtree, then unknown, and Hayes never got the audition.

He kept his end of the deal and scored the whole film anyway.
