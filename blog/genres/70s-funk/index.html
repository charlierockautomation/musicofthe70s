What Counts as Funk

Funk is dance music built on the groove, with a heavy accent on the first beat of every bar.

James Brown's band built that sound in the mid-1960s.

Brown moved the weight of the rhythm from the backbeat to the downbeat.

Musicians call that first beat the One.

Most of the records in the list share a few things:

  • A bass line that carries the tune as much as the singer does.
  • Short, repeated guitar and horn riffs in place of long chord changes.
  • A tempo built for dancing.
  • Lyrics that call out to the crowd, or no lyrics at all.
  • Long grooves that radio cut down into single edits.

The tag in this site's data marks where a record sits.

Smooth soul ballads are on the soul page, and the groove records are here.

The Biggest Funk Records of the Decade

Two records share the longest run at the top, and two more set marks of their own.

Play That Funky Music

Rob Parissi of Wild Cherry wrote it in about five minutes.

The band was a hard rock covers group playing the 2001 Club in Pittsburgh.

A patron asked if they were going to play some funky music.

The single was the first release on the Sweet City label, distributed by Epic, in April 1976.

It reached No. 1 on the Hot 100 on September 18, 1976.

It also topped the Billboard soul singles chart.

Family Affair

Sly & the Family Stone released it on November 6, 1971.

It was the band's third and last No. 1 on the Hot 100, with three weeks on top.

It spent five weeks at No. 1 on the R&B chart.

Billy Preston played the electric piano, and a drum machine kept the beat.

Theme from Shaft

Isaac Hayes wrote and recorded it for the 1971 film Shaft, and it reached No. 1 in November 1971.

It won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Hayes became the first African American to win an Oscar outside the acting categories.

At the 1972 Grammys it also won Best Instrumental Arrangement, and Hayes won Best Original Score for the film.

Come and Get Your Love

Lolly Vegas of Redbone wrote it and played the electric sitar on the record.

It peaked at No. 5 on April 13, 1974.

That made Redbone the first Native American band in the Hot 100 top five.

It finished fourth on the 1974 year-end list, the highest year-end rank of any song here.

War

War placed 7 songs here, more than any other 70s funk act.

None of them reached No. 1.

The band started in Long Beach, California, as the Creators.

In 1968 they became Nightshift and backed the blues singer Deacon Jones.

Producer Jerry Goldstein saw them at the Rag Doll in North Hollywood in 1969.

He teamed them with Eric Burdon of the Animals.

Spill the Wine launched the band.

In 1973 The World Is a Ghetto topped the Billboard 200 and was Billboard's album of the year.

Stevie Wonder and the Ohio Players

Stevie Wonder and the Ohio Players placed four songs each.

The Ohio Players took two of theirs, Fire and Love Rollercoaster, to No. 1.

George Clinton's Two Bands

George Clinton ran the same circle of musicians as two bands on two labels.

Funkadelic signed to Westbound Records.

Parliament recorded Osmium for Invictus in 1970.

It later signed to Casablanca and released Up for the Down Stroke in 1974.

The 1976 P-Funk Earth Tour added science fiction stagecraft and a stage prop called the Mothership.

Two Parliament records are in the list:

  • Give Up the Funk (Tear the Roof off the Sucker), on the 1976 year-end list.
  • Flash Light, on the 1978 year-end list.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did Vanilla Ice record Play That Funky Music?

Yes, Vanilla Ice released his own version on the 1989 album Hooked for Ichiban Records, with Ice Ice Baby as its B-side.

Rob Parissi was not credited on it, and he was later awarded $500,000 in a copyright lawsuit over the song.

Is Pick Up the Pieces an instrumental?

Almost, since the only words on the Average White Band record are the title, shouted a few times.

Arif Mardin produced it, Molly Duncan played the saxophone solo, and it reached No. 1 on the Hot 100 on February 22, 1975, after American radio picked it up from the AWB album.

Which movies use Come and Get Your Love?

Marvel's Guardians of the Galaxy (2014) and Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3 (2023) both feature the Redbone song.

It is also the theme song of the animated sitcom F Is for Family, and those uses brought it a new audience in the 2010s.

Who inducted Sly and the Family Stone into the Rock Hall?

George Clinton of Parliament-Funkadelic inducted Sly and the Family Stone into the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 1993.

Four years later, in 1997, Prince inducted Parliament-Funkadelic, so two of the decade's funk leaders were honored by fellow artists.
