1970s Pop Songs: 230 Year-End Hits in One Playlist

By Charlie · Published August 5, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

The biggest 1970s pop songs on the Billboard charts are You Light Up My Life by Debby Boone, with 10 weeks at No. 1, the longest run of the decade, and Silly Love Songs by Wings, the top song of the 1976 Year-End Hot 100.

Pop's biggest chart year was 1977, when 35 pop records made the Year-End Hot 100.

This page lists all 230, from the Carpenters to ABBA, and 230 of them play right here in one continuous list.

What Makes a Song 70s Pop

A 70s pop song is a radio single built for the widest audience: a strong tune, a clear lead vocal and a polished studio sound, sold to teens and their parents at the same time.

Most of the records in the list share a few things:

  • A hook you can hum after one listen.
  • A clean, upfront lead voice, often a solo singer or a duo.
  • Strings, piano and backing vocals in place of loud guitars.
  • A big chorus that comes back again and again.
  • Love songs, novelty songs and movie themes, more than protest or politics.

The tag in this site's data marks where a record sits, not every hit by a pop star.

Philadelphia Freedom by Elton John carries the pop tag here.

His Crocodile Rock and Bennie and the Jets sit with rock.

Soft rock, soul and disco have their own tags, so Bread and Al Green sit elsewhere.

Tie a Yellow Ribbon

Irwin Levine co-wrote Tie a Yellow Ribbon Round the Ole Oak Tree.

Hank Medress and Dave Appell produced the record.

It topped both the U.S. and UK charts for four weeks in April 1973.

It was also the best-selling single of the year in both countries.

The Way We Were

Barbra Streisand released The Way We Were as a single on September 27, 1973.

It tells the story of the two lead characters in her 1973 film of the same name.

Marilyn and Alan Bergman wrote the words and Marvin Hamlisch the music, and the song won two Academy Awards.

Love Will Keep Us Together

Captain & Tennille were a husband-and-wife duo, Daryl Dragon and Toni Tennille.

Their cover went to No. 1 on June 21, 1975.

It won the Grammy for Record of the Year, ahead of Mandy and Lyin' Eyes.

The pair also hosted their own variety series on ABC in 1976 and 1977.

Silly Love Songs

Wings released Silly Love Songs as a U.S. single on April 1, 1976.

It came from the album Wings at the Speed of Sound.

It spent 5 weeks at No. 1, the longest run of any Wings song in this list.

Movie Songs That Went to No. 1

Hollywood handed pop some of its biggest records of the decade.

  • Raindrops Keep Fallin' on My Head, from Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid, was the first American No. 1 of the 1970s.
  • The Morning After won the Oscar for The Poseidon Adventure before Maureen McGovern cut the single that reached No. 1 in August 1973.
  • Evergreen, the love theme from A Star Is Born, made Barbra Streisand the first woman to win the Oscar for Best Original Song as a composer.
  • Gonna Fly Now, by Bill Conti, spent a week at No. 1 in 1977.

You Light Up My Life

Joseph Brooks wrote You Light Up My Life for his 1977 film of the same name.

Debby Boone, the daughter of singer Pat Boone, recorded the hit cover.

It held No. 1 for ten straight weeks.

She won the Grammy for Best New Artist the following year.

At that same ceremony, Song of the Year ended in a tie between You Light Up My Life and Evergreen.

Television Built the Teen Idols

Television turned several acts in this list into teen idols before radio caught up.

The Partridge Family

The Partridge Family was an ABC sitcom about a family pop band.

It ran from September 1970 to March 1974.

Shirley Jones played the mother, and David Cassidy played Keith and sang lead on the records.

The show was loosely based on the Cowsills, a real singing family of the late 60s.

Doesn't Somebody Want to Be Wanted and I Woke Up in Love This Morning both made the 1971 year-end list.

The Osmonds

George Jackson wrote One Bad Apple with the Jackson 5 in mind.

The Osmonds recorded it instead, at FAME Studios in Muscle Shoals.

It reached No. 1 on February 13, 1971, and stayed for five weeks.

The song later became the theme of The Osmonds cartoon on ABC.

Shaun Cassidy and the Bay City Rollers

Shaun Cassidy's cover of Da Doo Ron Ron, from his first solo album, reached No. 1 in 1977.

Scotland's Bay City Rollers, sold as the tartan teen sensations from Edinburgh, reached No. 1 with Saturday Night in 1976.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote Love Will Keep Us Together?

Neil Sedaka wrote it with his longtime lyricist Howard Greenfield, and Sedaka cut it first in 1973 for The Tra-La Days Are Over, an album with no U.S. release.

Sedaka later said he lifted the main chord progression from Do It Again by the Beach Boys.

The Captain & Tennille cover earned the two writers a Song of the Year nomination.

Who sang You Light Up My Life in the movie?

Kasey Cisyk sang the original for the 1977 film, while actress Didi Conn lip-synced it on screen.

Cisyk's version was released as a single credited to Original Cast, not to her, and it stalled at No. 80 on the Hot 100.

On Record World's chart, Debby Boone's cover ran 13 weeks at the top.

Why did Paul McCartney write Silly Love Songs?

He wrote it with Linda McCartney as a reply to critics, and to John Lennon, who had knocked him for writing lightweight love songs.

It was his 27th American No. 1 as a songwriter, a record for any writer.

McCartney has not played it live since Wings broke up.

Who played on the Partridge Family records?

Los Angeles session players from the Wrecking Crew, including drummer Hal Blaine, played on every Partridge Family studio record.

Studio singers including the Ron Hicklin Singers sang the backing parts.

Their first single, I Think I Love You, sold four million copies and was named the best-selling single of 1970.

Why is Alone Again (Naturally) famous in court?

A 1991 lawsuit over the song set the precedent that sampling a recording without permission can be copyright infringement.

Gilbert O'Sullivan recorded the song in 1971, and it was left off his 1972 album Back to Front until later reissues.

The single sold more than two million copies.
