70s Soft Rock: 174 Songs You Can Play in One List

By Charlie · Published August 9, 2026 · Updated November 11, 2026

The biggest 70s soft rock hit is Tonight's the Night by Rod Stewart, with 8 weeks at No. 1 and the top spot on the 1977 Year-End Hot 100, followed by Bridge Over Troubled Water by Simon & Garfunkel, the top song of 1970.

The genre's biggest chart year came late, in 1979, when 25 soft rock records made the Year-End Hot 100.

This page lists 174 songs, from Carole King to Gerry Rafferty, and every one of them plays right here in one continuous list.

What Counts as Soft Rock

Soft rock is rock with the edges smoothed off: simple tunes, warm full arrangements, and the voice of a singer-songwriter or a close-harmony group up front.

Most of the records in the list share a few things:

  • Acoustic guitar or piano carrying the song, in place of a loud electric riff.
  • Close vocal harmonies.
  • A slow or middle tempo.
  • Clean, polished studio sound.
  • Lyrics about love, loss and looking back.

The sound took shape in the late 1960s in the United States and Britain.

It reached its commercial peak in the middle and late 1970s.

In the 1980s it turned into the synthesizer-heavy adult contemporary format.

The tag in this site's data marks where a record sits, so Hotel California sits with rock while the Eagles' quieter hits are here.

The Three Biggest Records

Two soft rock records finished a whole Billboard year at No. 1, and a third came close.

Bridge Over Troubled Water

Paul Simon wrote it, and Art Garfunkel sang the lead.

The single came out on January 20, 1970.

It spent six weeks at No. 1 and finished as the top song of the 1970 year-end list.

It's Too Late / I Feel the Earth Move

Carole King's double A-side spent 5 weeks at No. 1 in 1971.

It finished third on that year's year-end list.

Tonight's the Night (Gonna Be Alright)

Rod Stewart wrote it for his 1976 album A Night on the Town.

It reached No. 1 on November 13, 1976, and stayed eight weeks, into January 1977.

That run made it the No. 1 song of the 1977 year-end list.

Tapestry and the Singer-Songwriters

Carole King released Tapestry on Ode Records on February 10, 1971.

It spent 15 straight weeks at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 album chart.

At the 1972 Grammys she won Album of the Year, Record of the Year for It's Too Late, and Song of the Year for You've Got a Friend.

James Taylor won the male pop vocal Grammy that same night for his recording of You've Got a Friend.

Other early names of the sound include:

  • James Taylor, with Fire and Rain and How Sweet It Is.
  • Carly Simon, with You're So Vain and Anticipation.
  • Cat Stevens, with Wild World and Morning Has Broken.
  • Bread and America, the soft-harmony groups of the early decade.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who speaks the French words on Tonight's the Night?

Britt Ekland, Rod Stewart's girlfriend at the time, speaks the French lines near the end of the record.

The BBC banned the song over its suggestive words, the Reverend Jesse Jackson protested it, and some American stations played an edited version instead of the album cut.

Who wrote It's Too Late by Carole King?

Toni Stern wrote the words and Carole King wrote the music.

The single, a double A-side with I Feel the Earth Move, spent five weeks at No. 1 on both the Billboard Hot 100 and the Easy Listening chart, so it ruled pop and adult radio at the same time.

How many Grammys did Bridge Over Troubled Water win?

The song won four at the 13th Grammy Awards in 1971.

They were Record of the Year, Song of the Year, Best Contemporary Song, and Best Arrangement, Instrumental and Vocals.

Song of the Year is a songwriter's award, so that one went to Paul Simon alone, while Record of the Year honored the duo's recording itself.

Is yacht rock the same as soft rock?

No, yacht rock is a later and narrower style that only partly overlaps with soft rock.

It covers the late 1970s and the early 1980s.

It grew out of the polished studio work of California session musicians, while soft rock had already been on the charts since the late 1960s.
