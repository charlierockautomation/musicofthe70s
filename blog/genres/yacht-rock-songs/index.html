Yacht Rock Hits of the 70s: 16 Songs You Can Play in One List

By Charlie · Published November 21, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026

The biggest yacht rock songs of the 1970s are Baby Come Back by Player and What a Fool Believes by the Doobie Brothers, the two on this page that reached No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100.

Yacht rock is the smooth, polished California sound that the web series Yacht Rock named in 2005, and this page plays 16 of its singles from 1976 to 1979.

The picks come from the Yacht or Nyacht scores kept by the series creators, and every song here scores at least 56 on their 100-point scale.

What Is Yacht Rock?

Yacht rock is a smooth, polished style tied to soft rock, and the name came in 2005 from the online comedy series Yacht Rock.

Wikipedia notes that the term did not exist when the music was made, which it dates from 1975 to 1984.

The series co-creators include J.D. Ryznar and Hunter Stair.

What Makes Yacht Rock Songs Sound Smooth?

AllMusic listed its defining rules in 2014, including “keep it smooth, even when it grooves” and “always keep it catchy.”

The series creators add an upbeat rhythm they call the Doobie Bounce.

Who Played on These Records?

Many songs in this style lean on the same small circle of Los Angeles session players.

Boz Scaggs recorded Lowdown on the 1976 album Silk Degrees with David Paich, Jeff Porcaro and David Hungate, three future members of Toto.

American Songwriter notes that Paich plays on all 10 tracks of that album, as do Porcaro and Hungate.

Grammy.com lists Lowdown as the winner of Best Rhythm & Blues Song at the 19th Annual Grammy Awards, credited to Paich and Scaggs.

Peg, Kid Charlemagne and FM

Steely Dan's Peg has Michael McDonald on backing vocals, and Songfacts quotes him saying the harmonies were “almost like they made a keyboard from my voice.”

Songfacts adds that Jay Graydon played the guitar solo after other guitarists tried it.

Guitar World says Larry Carlton's solo on Kid Charlemagne was his most famous contribution to The Royal Scam.

The single of FM (No Static at All) features Jeff Porcaro on drums, according to uDiscover Music, and it peaked at No. 22 in July 1978.

Hold the Line

David Paich wrote the piano riff of Hold the Line, a Top 5 hit among these yacht rock songs, during his first few weeks of living on his own, American Songwriter reports.

Songfacts says guitarist Steve Lukather was 19 when the band cut the record.

The Doobie Brothers and Kenny Loggins

What a Fool Believes began as a Michael McDonald song, and Songfacts says Kenny Loggins came in when McDonald got stuck on the bridge.

American Songwriter quotes McDonald recalling the “little piano riff and some words” he had when Loggins asked to work on it.

The Doobie Brothers' version reached No. 1 for one week on April 14, 1979, and won Grammys for Record of the Year and Song of the Year.

The album's title track, Minute by Minute, peaked at No. 14.

Takin' It to the Streets, the first Doobie Brothers album with McDonald on lead vocals, produced a single that reached No. 13.

Kenny Loggins

Kenny Loggins is the other name that runs through the core yacht rock songs.

This Is It reached No. 11, and Songfacts says McDonald sang backup and played keyboards on the record.

Loggins won the Grammy for Best Male Pop Vocal Performance for it in 1981.

Whenever I Call You Friend, which Loggins wrote with Melissa Manchester, is a duet with Stevie Nicks and reached No. 5.

The Singles From Other Acts

Not every yacht rock song on the list came from a core act.

Baby Come Back by Player spent three weeks at No. 1 in January 1978, and Stereogum says both writers had just broken up with their girlfriends.

Love Will Find a Way, from Pablo Cruise's 1978 album Worlds Away, reached No. 6.

Smoke from a Distant Fire

The Sanford-Townsend Band's Smoke from a Distant Fire reached No. 9 in 1977, and Songfacts says it was recorded at Muscle Shoals Sound Studios in Alabama.

American Songwriter credits producer Jerry Wexler.

Songfacts says the title came from a poem Ed Sanford wrote in college.

What You Won't Do for Love and After the Love Has Gone

Bobby Caldwell's What You Won't Do for Love came out in September 1978 and reached No. 9.

Caldwell told Red Bull Music Academy that his label got “skittish” about taking a white singer to Black radio, and that he drew the silhouette idea for his cover himself.

Earth, Wind & Fire's After the Love Has Gone reached No. 2 in 1979, held back by the Knack's My Sharona.

Where This List Stops

This page stops at the end of 1979 because the site covers the 1970s.

Wikipedia dates the style's music from 1975 to 1984, so songs whose runs came in 1980 or later are left out here.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the biggest songs on this list?

Baby Come Back and What a Fool Believes both reached No. 1, about fifteen months apart.

Lowdown, Hold the Line and After the Love Has Gone also peaked in the Top 5.

What does nyacht rock mean?

Nyacht rock is a word invented in 2016 for songs that fans sometimes file under yacht rock but that the style's creators say do not belong.

Their Yacht or Nyacht list scores each song on a 100-point scale and sorts it into one camp or the other.

Who wrote What You Won't Do for Love?

Bobby Caldwell wrote it with Alfons Kettner, and Ann Holloway produced it.

Tupac Shakur sampled it in the 1998 song Do for Love.

Who wrote Love Will Find a Way?

David Jenkins and Cory Lerios of Pablo Cruise wrote it.

A&M Records released the single in May 1978.
