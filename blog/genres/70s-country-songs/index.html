70s Country Songs: Play 60 Hits, See Who Ruled #1

By Charlie · Published October 10, 2026

▶ Jump to the playable list

70s country songs lived on two charts at once, and the two rarely matched.

Billboard's country chart crowned 317 different number ones in the decade.

Only 28 of the 60 country-tagged songs on this site's pop year-end lists were among them.

Most of the acts who ruled the country chart never touched the pop one.

This page plays all 60 songs, then shows who really led the country chart.

What Counts as 70s Country Music

70s country music was never one sound.

The best 70s country songs came from three overlapping styles pulling in different directions.

The table below sets them side by side, with a song from the playable list for each.

Countrypolitan: The Smooth Side

Countrypolitan grew out of the Nashville Sound.

Wikipedia describes it as a smoother sound with lush string arrangements played by a real orchestra.

It sold best from the later 1960s into the mid-1970s.

Producers Billy Sherrill and Glenn Sutton are tied to it there.

Charlie Rich's Behind Closed Doors and Lynn Anderson's Rose Garden both sit on the list above.

Outlaw Country: The Rough Side

Outlaw country pushed back against that polish.

Wikipedia dates its heyday to the mid-to-late 1970s, with signs of decline by 1978.

The 1976 compilation Wanted! The Outlaws collected Waylon Jennings, Willie Nelson, Jessi Colter, and Tompall Glaser.

Wikipedia calls it the first country album certified platinum.

Waylon and Willie on the Country Chart

In this site's weekly data, Waylon Jennings reached number one 8 times, for 19 weeks in all.

His Luckenbach, Texas held the top spot for 6 weeks starting May 21, 1977.

Their duet Mammas Don't Let Your Babies Grow Up to Be Cowboys added 4 more weeks in 1978.

Willie Nelson reached number one 4 times under his own name.

The first was Blue Eyes Crying in the Rain on Oct 4, 1975.

Country Pop and Country Rock: The Crossover Side

Country pop first emerged in the 1970s, according to Wikipedia.

Glen Campbell, John Denver, Olivia Newton-John, Anne Murray, and Linda Ronstadt all had hits on the country charts.

Dolly Parton's Here You Come Again topped the country chart.

Wikipedia says it also reached No. 3 on the pop chart.

Among the 70s country songs on this site's list, Rhinestone Cowboy by Glen Campbell ranks highest, at #2 for 1975.

The rock-leaning end of the crossover has its own page, 70s country rock.

Who Really Ruled the Country Chart

Billboard's Hot Country Singles chart tells a different story about 70s country songs than the pop year-end lists do.

This site's weekly country data covers 522 charts, from Jan 3, 1970 through Jan 4, 1980.

317 different songs reached number one in that stretch.

When the Country and Pop Charts Agreed

Of the 60 70s country songs on the playable list, 28 also reached number one on the country chart.

11 reached number one on the weekly Hot 100.

Only 7 did both.

These are the true double number ones in this site's data:

  • The Most Beautiful Girl by Charlie Rich (1974)
  • Rhinestone Cowboy by Glen Campbell (1975)
  • Before the Next Teardrop Falls by Freddy Fender (1975)
  • Thank God I'm a Country Boy by John Denver (1975)
  • (Hey Won't You Play) Another Somebody Done Somebody Wrong Song by B.J. Thomas (1975)
  • Convoy by C. W. McCall (1976)
  • Southern Nights by Glen Campbell (1977)

Every other country number one stayed on the country side of the radio dial.

Most 70s country songs followed that same path.

That is why a country chart list and a pop year-end list look so different.

The pop side of the story is covered in 70s number one hits.

The Women Who Ran the Country Chart

These seven women alone account for 51 of the decade's 317 country number ones.

Each reached number one at least six times:

  • Dolly Parton: 10 number ones, 18 weeks at the top.
  • Tammy Wynette: 10 number ones, 16 weeks at the top.
  • Loretta Lynn: 8 number ones, 12 weeks at the top.
  • Crystal Gayle: 6 number ones, 11 weeks at the top.
  • Donna Fargo: 6 number ones, 10 weeks at the top.
  • Tanya Tucker: 6 number ones, 6 weeks at the top.
  • Lynn Anderson: 5 number ones, 12 weeks at the top.

Dolly Parton's run is the clearest example.

Jolene first hit number one on Feb 2, 1974.

Frequently Asked Questions

What were the most popular 70s country songs?

The most popular 70s country songs on the pop year-end lists included Rhinestone Cowboy by Glen Campbell (1975), Before the Next Teardrop Falls by Freddy Fender (1975), Why Me by Kris Kristofferson (1973).

Those ranks measure pop-chart success, not the country chart.

On Billboard's country chart, Charley Pride and Conway Twitty logged the most number ones.

Who had the most number one country songs in the 1970s?

Charley Pride and Conway Twitty each reached number one on Billboard's Hot Country Singles chart 20 times in the 1970s.

Pride spent 37 weeks on top and Twitty spent 30 weeks.

Merle Haggard followed with 16 number ones, based on this site's weekly country chart data.

What was the longest-running number one country song of the 70s?

Three songs tied for the longest country chart runs of the 1970s, at six weeks each.

They were My Hang-Up Is You by Freddie Hart (1972); Convoy by C. W. McCall (1975); Luckenbach, Texas (Back to the Basics of Love) by Waylon Jennings (1977).

These counts come from this site's weekly Hot Country Singles data, which follows Wikipedia's year-by-year number one lists.

What is outlaw country?

Outlaw country is a 1970s country subgenre that grew as a reaction to the polished Nashville sound.

Willie Nelson, Waylon Jennings, Jessi Colter, and Tompall Glaser appeared on the 1976 compilation Wanted! The Outlaws.

Wikipedia says it was the first country album certified platinum.

How many 70s country songs also reached number one on the pop chart?

Of 60 country-tagged songs in this site's Year-End Hot 100 data, 11 reached number one on the weekly Hot 100.

7 of those 11 topped the country chart as well, so true double number ones were rare.

Most country number ones never appeared on a pop year-end list at all.
