What Makes a Song Disco

Disco is dance music built on a steady four-on-the-floor bass drum, a busy hi-hat and a heavy, syncopated bass line, usually dressed in strings, horns and electric piano.

Most of the records in the list share the same building blocks:

  • A bass drum on every beat, the four-on-the-floor pulse.
  • An open hi-hat hissing on the off-beat.
  • A bass line that jumps between octaves.
  • String sections and horns over the groove.
  • Electric pianos, and by 1977 synthesizers.

The tag in this site's data marks where a record sits, not every song with a dance beat.

Boogie Wonderland, Heart of Glass and I Was Made for Lovin' You by Kiss all carry the disco tag here.

Rock and funk acts crossed onto the dance floor at the end of the decade, and the chart shows it.

Where Disco Started

The sound began on the dance floor, years before it had a name.

New York clubs were the biggest scene, with Philadelphia, Baltimore, San Francisco, Miami and Washington close behind.

DJs built whole nights out of soul and funk records, and the beat mattered more than any one voice.

Where the Word Came From

Disco is short for discotheque, French for a library of records.

The word was first used for Paris nightclubs that turned to playing records during the Nazi occupation.

The Loft and the Sound System

David Mancuso's New York party, The Loft, set the standard for the sound.

He added tweeter arrays above the floor and extra subwoofers under it.

By the time the major labels caught on, the music had years of dance floor testing behind it.

Disco Demolition Night, July 12, 1979

The year disco had its most songs on the chart was also the year of its loudest backlash.

Chicago radio DJ Steve Dahl staged a promotion between games of a White Sox doubleheader at Comiskey Park.

Fans got in for 98 cents if they brought a disco record to be blown up on the field.

  • The White Sox hoped for a crowd of 20,000.
  • Official attendance was 47,795, and owner Bill Veeck estimated 50,000 to 55,000 inside the park.
  • Dahl blew up a crate of records between games.
  • Between 5,000 and 7,000 people then rushed the field and tore up the grass.
  • Riot police cleared the field, and the second game was forfeited to the Detroit Tigers.

Did It Kill Disco?

The night picked up a nickname almost at once: the night disco died.

By late 1979 and into 1980, record companies began calling their releases dance music instead.

Dahl said in a 2004 interview that disco was "probably on its way out" by 1979, and that the night "hastened its demise."

The backlash changed how the sound was sold, not how well it had just sold.

Studio 54 and Saturday Night Fever

Studio 54

Steve Rubell and Ian Schrager opened Studio 54 on April 26, 1977, at 254 West 54th Street in Manhattan.

The building opened in 1927 as the Gallo Opera House and later served as a CBS studio.

In its first month the club drew about 2,000 guests a night.

Rubell and Schrager pleaded guilty to tax evasion in November 1979.

Saturday Night Fever

The soundtrack came out on RSO Records on November 15, 1977.

It carries Stayin' Alive, Night Fever and More Than a Woman, and has sold more than 40 million copies.

It won the Grammy for Album of the Year, the only disco album ever to do so.

The soundtrack's best-known ballad has its own story in How Deep Is Your Love.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who produced I Feel Love?

Giorgio Moroder produced it, with a prominent Moog synthesizer laying down the beat.

It was one of the first disco records built on a synthesizer.

It peaked at No. 6 on the Hot 100 the week of November 12, 1977, and reached No. 1 on the UK Singles Chart.

Where was the first disco party?

Many histories start with The Loft, where David Mancuso held his first party, called Love Saves the Day, on February 14, 1970, in New York City.

Entry was by invitation only, and no alcohol or food was sold.

Mancuso later chose to play records in full instead of beatmatching them.

Why did the White Sox forfeit the second game on Disco Demolition Night?

American League president Lee MacPhail ruled that the home team was responsible for the field, which the crowd had torn up.

He forfeited the game to the Detroit Tigers, 9 to 0, the day after the event.

Baseball rules allowed a postponement only for an act of God.

How well did the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack sell?

It was the best-selling album in history until Michael Jackson's Thriller.

It spent 24 straight weeks at No. 1 on the album chart, from January to July 1978, and 120 weeks on the chart in all.

In the United States it is certified 16 times platinum.

What happened to the owners of Studio 54?

Steve Rubell and Ian Schrager were each sentenced to three and a half years in prison in January 1980, after pleading guilty to tax evasion.

A felony conviction also put the club's liquor license at risk.

CBS News dates the end of the original party to that 1980 conviction.
