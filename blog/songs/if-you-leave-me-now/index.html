If You Leave Me Now: Chicago's First Number One

By Charlie · Published October 21, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

If You Leave Me Now gave Chicago their first number one in October 1976.

Bass player Peter Cetera wrote it on guitar and sang it, a quiet ballad that opened a new chapter for the band.

It stayed on top for two weeks in the US and three weeks in the UK.

This page covers the writing, the recording, the charts, the two Grammys and the covers.

If You Leave Me Now: How Peter Cetera Wrote It

Peter Cetera was Chicago's bass player, and he wrote and sang this one himself.

He composed it on guitar.

Wikipedia places the writing around the same time as Wishing You Were Here, from Chicago VII.

A Key and a Range

Sheet music listings give the key as B major.

They put Cetera's vocal range between F sharp 3 and D sharp 5.

Those two notes show how high he sings in a song about begging someone to stay.

For more of the decade's quiet side, start at Music of the 70s and follow the soft rock tags.

Where It Sat in Chicago's Story

The record landed on Chicago X, the band's 1976 album.

It was a first in one specific way: no earlier Chicago single had reached number one.

Their earlier hit stopped at number four in 1970, a story told in our 25 or 6 to 4 post.

Recording the Song: Acoustic Demo, Live Take, Strings

The band recorded it in March and April 1976.

James William Guercio produced, and the finished track runs 3:58.

Guercio played acoustic guitar on the demo.

He planned for Terry Kath to replace that part later.

The band liked the demo guitar and kept it.

A Basic Track Cut Live

Per Cetera's 1991 booklet notes for Group Portrait, the song was recorded live with the band.

His first vocal was later redone, and Kath added an electric guitar overdub.

The Radio Test

Walter Parazaider remembered hearing the song on the radio while he cleaned his pool.

He thought it sounded like Paul McCartney.

That is a fair guess for a record built on acoustic guitar, strings and one clear voice.

On the Charts: Number One in October 1976

Columbia Records released the single on July 30, 1976, with Together Again on the B-side.

It reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 on the chart of October 23, 1976.

This site's weekly Hot 100 data confirms two weeks on top, the weeks of October 23 and October 30.

It was Chicago's first number one.

Number One in the US and the UK

  • It also reached number one on the Billboard Easy Listening chart and the Cash Box Top 100.
  • In the UK it spent three weeks at number one.
  • Billboard counted it as one of five non-disco US number ones in a nine-month stretch of 1976.
  • By August 1978 it had sold 1.4 million copies in the US.

Two Grammys and a Chocolate Kiss

The song won two Grammy Awards and was nominated for Record of the Year.

The first win was Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus, the group's first Grammy.

The second was Best Arrangement Accompanying Vocalist(s), for the strings.

What the Critics Said

  • Cash Box called it an excellent ballad.
  • Record World called it a good single choice.
  • The Guardian ranked it No. 73 on its 2020 list of the greatest UK number ones.
  • Writing in 2016, Troy L. Smith said Cetera gives the best vocal performance of any Chicago song.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote If You Leave Me Now by Chicago?

Peter Cetera, Chicago's bass player, wrote If You Leave Me Now and sang the lead vocal.

He composed it on guitar around the same time as Wishing You Were Here.

James William Guercio produced the 1976 recording, which appears on the album Chicago X.

Did If You Leave Me Now reach number one?

The song reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 for two weeks starting October 23, 1976.

It was Chicago's first number one single.

It also topped the Cash Box Top 100 and the Billboard Easy Listening chart, and it spent three weeks at number one in the UK.

Who played on If You Leave Me Now?

Per Peter Cetera's 1991 Group Portrait notes, the basic track was recorded live with Cetera singing, Guercio on acoustic guitar, Terry Kath on bass, Robert Lamm on piano and Danny Seraphine on drums.

Cetera's vocal was later redone, Kath added electric guitar, and Jimmie Haskell arranged the strings.

What album is If You Leave Me Now on?

If You Leave Me Now is on Chicago X, the band's 1976 album.

Columbia Records released it as a single on July 30, 1976, with Together Again on the B-side.

The recording took place in March and April 1976 and runs 3:58.

Did If You Leave Me Now win a Grammy?

If You Leave Me Now won two Grammy Awards, and it was nominated for Record of the Year.

One was Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus, the group's first Grammy.

The other was Best Arrangement Accompanying Vocalist(s), awarded to arranger Jimmie Haskell and producer Guercio.
