Copacabana Barry Manilow: The Story Behind Lola and Tony

By Charlie · Published October 18, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

Copacabana Barry Manilow is a three-act drama hidden inside a disco single.

A showgirl named Lola, a bartender named Tony and a gunshot all fit into one song.

It peaked at number eight on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1978 and won Manilow his only Grammy.

Below are the Rio hotel chat behind it, the plot, the players, the charts and the musical it became.

Copacabana Barry Manilow: From a Rio Hotel to a Story Song

A Question in Rio de Janeiro

Wikipedia says the song grew from a chat between Manilow and Bruce Sussman at the Copacabana Hotel in Rio de Janeiro.

They wondered whether anyone had ever written a song called "Copacabana".

Manilow knew the name from the other Copacabana, the New York nightclub he had visited often in the 1960s.

He asked Sussman and Jack Feldman to write the story, and then he wrote the music.

Wikipedia adds that singer and dancer Lola Falana inspired the song's famous lyric.

Lola, Tony and Rico: The Plot

Three Acts in Under Six Minutes

The song tells a complete story, which is rare for a single that was built for dancing.

Wikipedia summarizes the plot like this:

  1. Lola is a showgirl at the Copacabana, and Tony is her sweetheart and the club's bartender.
  2. Rico, a wealthy man, pursues Lola, and Tony steps in when Rico gets aggressive.
  3. The fight ends with a gunshot, and Tony dies.
  4. Thirty years later the club is a discotheque, and a middle-aged Lola still sits at the bar in her showgirl costume.

The Cheerful Tune Over a Sad Ending

The verses move at a bright, almost bouncy pace.

Then the last verse flips the mood, and the joyful chorus keeps playing underneath it.

Wikipedia says Lola has lost her youth, Tony and her stability by then.

The Sound: Disco, Samba and a Studio Band

A Latin Beat in a Pop Song

Wikipedia quotes Cash Box on the sound: "a Latin beat, congas and added percussion, strings and horns make it unusual".

That mix of samba rhythm and disco pulse is what separates Copacabana Barry Manilow from the ballads he was known for.

The album version runs 5:46, and the radio edit trims it to 3:58.

Who Played on It

Wikipedia lists the original personnel:

  • Barry Manilow on vocals and acoustic piano.
  • Bill Mays on keyboards and Mitch Holder on guitar.
  • Will Lee on bass, Ronnie Zito on drums and Alan Estes on percussion.
  • Artie Butler on orchestration.
  • Ginger Blake, Laura Creamer and Linda Dillard on backing vocals.

Legacy: A Grammy, a TV Movie and a West End Musical

Manilow's Only Grammy

Copacabana earned Manilow his first and only Grammy Award, for Best Male Pop Vocal Performance, in February 1979.

Wikipedia also calls it his first gold single for a song he wrote or co-wrote.

Barry Manilow in November 1979, photo by Alan Light, licensed CC BY 2.0.

From Single to Musical

In 1985 Manilow, Feldman and Sussman expanded the song into a full-length, made-for-television musical, also called Copacabana.

Wikipedia says that film was later developed into a two-act stage musical.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote Copacabana by Barry Manilow?

Barry Manilow, Jack Feldman and Bruce Sussman wrote Copacabana, according to Wikipedia.

Manilow wrote the music, and Feldman and Sussman wrote the lyrics.

Manilow and Ron Dante produced it, and it came out in June 1978 as the third single from the album Even Now.

What was Copacabana's peak on the Billboard Hot 100?

Copacabana peaked at number eight on the Billboard Hot 100, according to Wikipedia.

It reached number six on the US Adult Contemporary chart and number two in France.

This site's year-end data ranks it number 74 for 1978, and it is not among the weekly Hot 100 number ones.

What happens to Lola and Tony in Copacabana?

Tony, a bartender, dies in a fight over Lola with a wealthy man named Rico, and the fight ends with a gunshot.

Thirty years later the club is a discotheque and Lola, now middle-aged, still sits at the bar in her showgirl costume, having lost her youth, Tony and her stability.

Did Copacabana win a Grammy?

Yes, Copacabana earned Barry Manilow his first and only Grammy Award, for Best Male Pop Vocal Performance, in February 1979, according to Wikipedia.

It was also his first gold single for a song he wrote or co-wrote, and the RIAA later certified it Gold in the United States.

Was Copacabana made into a movie or musical?

Yes, in 1985 Manilow, Jack Feldman and Bruce Sussman expanded the song into a made-for-television musical also called Copacabana, according to Wikipedia.

It was later developed into a stage musical that ran for two years at the Prince of Wales Theatre in London's West End, followed by a UK tour.
