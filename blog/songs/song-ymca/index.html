Y.M.C.A. by Village People: Who Wrote It and How High It Charted

By Charlie · Published September 4, 2026 · Updated November 24, 2026

The YMCA song is Y.M.C.A. by Village People, written by Jacques Morali and Victor Willis and released in 1978.

Y.M.C.A. peaked at No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100 and reached No. 1 on the UK Singles Chart.

This page covers who wrote it, where the arm dance began, how it charted, and the fight over its name.

Who Wrote Y.M.C.A. and Where Did It Come From?

Jacques Morali and Victor Willis wrote Y.M.C.A. for Village People, and Morali produced it for Casablanca Records.

The Library of Congress says Morali wrote the music and Willis wrote the words.

Horace Ott arranged the horns and strings, according to SPIN and the Library of Congress.

How the YMCA Song Started as Filler

SPIN's 2008 oral history says Morali wrote the melody and chorus outline in about 20 minutes.

The same account says it was reportedly filler for the album Cruisin', and Morali passed it to Willis to finish.

Where Willis Finished the Words

Willis told the Library of Congress that he wrote several drafts on the road.

He recalled finishing the final draft in Vancouver.

Willis names the Buchanan Street Y in San Francisco and the West Side Y in New York as places that inspired the song.

Randy Jones told SPIN that Morali had visited the McBurney YMCA in New York.

Where Does the Y.M.C.A. Dance Come From?

Randy Jones told SPIN that Village People appeared on American Bandstand on January 6, 1979.

Jones said the studio audience was kids bused in from a cheerleader camp.

He said their raised hands in the chorus looked like a Y, so the kids began forming the letters.

Jones called the dance purely audience-generated, and said the group joined in.

  • Source for the Bandstand account: Randy Jones in SPIN's 2008 oral history.
  • Other tellings of the dance origin exist, and none comes from the original recording session.

How High Did Y.M.C.A. Chart?

The YMCA song peaked at No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100.

Le Freak by Chic and then Da Ya Think I'm Sexy? by Rod Stewart kept it from the top, according to the Library of Congress.

This site's Billboard data ranks it No. 8 among the biggest hits of 1979.

Both of the songs that blocked it reached No. 1, as the 70s number one hits hub shows.

On the UK Singles Chart, Y.M.C.A. reached No. 1 for 3 weeks and stayed on the chart for 16 weeks, according to the Official Charts Company.

Did the YMCA Sue Village People?

The YMCA filed suit on January 13, 1979, over use of its name, according to American Songwriter.

American Songwriter reports that the YMCA song's popularity cooled the fight and the case settled out of court with Village People.

Willis and the Rights

NPR reported in September 2013 that Willis had won a share of the income from Y.M.C.A.

The same report says he gained substantial control over the song and 32 others.

Why Did the Library of Congress Preserve Y.M.C.A.?

The Library of Congress added Y.M.C.A. to the National Recording Registry, announced on March 25, 2020.

American Songwriter describes the Registry as honoring recordings that are culturally, historically or aesthetically significant.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who are the credited writers of Y.M.C.A.?

Jacques Morali and Victor Willis are the credited writers, with Willis on the words.

Songfacts still lists Henri Belolo as a third writer, while Wikipedia says a 2015 court ruling removed his credit.

How long was Y.M.C.A. on the Billboard Hot 100?

SPIN says the single stayed on the Hot 100 for 26 weeks.

SPIN adds that it took 13 weeks after its debut to reach its peak.

Did anyone else claim to invent the Y.M.C.A. dance?

David Hodo told SPIN that the Japanese singer Hideki Saijo claimed to have invented the dance in Japan.

Hodo said the group straightened him out when they arrived there.

What did the YMCA say about the song later?

YMCA media relations manager Leah Pouw told SPIN in 2008, "We at the YMCA celebrate the song."
