One of These Nights: The Eagles' Turn Away From Country

One of These Nights is the sound of a country band deciding to chase something else.

Glenn Frey wrote the music with Spinners and Al Green records on the turntable.

Don Henley added a lyric about wanting more than you have.

The beat underneath it is a nod to disco.

It became the Eagles' second number one single in a single year.

It also carried their first album to the top of the chart.

Then the lineup that recorded it came apart.

One of These Nights Was a Country Band Chasing an R&B Groove

The Eagles built their first three albums on country rock.

This song was a deliberate turn away from that.

Glenn Frey has said he was playing Spinners and Al Green records when he started writing.

He wanted the track to have teeth and bite.

His own phrase for it was a nasty track with pretty vocals.

That tension between a hard groove and a smooth top line runs through the whole recording.

The decade is full of these genre crossovers, and the Random 70s Song Generator keeps turning up more of them.

How the Song Started at a Piano

Frey has described the first moment plainly.

He sat at a piano and played a minor descending progression.

Henley walked over and sang the opening line back at him.

The title phrase arrived before almost anything else in the song.

They wrote the rest of the lyric out from that single idea of wanting something you cannot reach yet.

The Disco Beat Henley Admitted To

Don Henley later pointed straight at the rhythm as the giveaway.

He called the four-on-the-floor bass drum pattern a nod to disco.

Over that steady pulse the band stacked soft harmonies and a falsetto hook.

The mix of a dance-floor kick and a country-rock band was new for the Eagles.

If that late-night, restless feel is what draws you in, the Mood Song Matcher lines up other records that sit in the same mood.

Recording Split Between Miami and Los Angeles

Bill Szymczyk produced the album.

The band cut the basic track at Criteria Studios in Miami in December 1974.

They moved to Los Angeles to record the guitars and the solo.

Then they went back to Miami in March 1975 to put the vocals on.

Randy Meisner missed the start of the sessions entirely.

He was snowed in at Scottsbluff, Nebraska.

Don Felder covered bass while Frey moved to piano and Bernie Leadon picked up a guitar.

Felder wrote the opening bass riff himself during that stretch.

He taught it to Meisner once the bassist finally reached the studio.

Don Felder's Solo Is a Saxophone in Disguise

Felder's guitar solo closes the track.

He has said it is really an alto saxophone part played on a guitar.

"If you listen to that guitar solo, it's really an alto saxophone solo," he told an interviewer in 2025.

The lines are blues based, built on long sustained string bends.

The tone is a thick, heavy distortion, unusual for an Eagles record.

A Second Number One in the Same Year

Asylum Records released the single on May 19, 1975.

It reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 for the week of August 2, 1975.

It held the top spot for a single week.

That made it the band's second chart-topping single of the year.

Best of My Love had been the first, back in March 1975.

Best of My Love was also the Eagles' first Hot 100 number one of any kind.

The album that followed became the band's first record to reach number one on the Billboard 200.

Why This Site's Data Shows 10, Not 1

This site's song database lists a billboard_peak of 10 for this track.

Read alone, that looks like a modest chart placement.

It is actually the song's rank on Billboard's year-end Hot 100 for 1975.

The year-end list weighs a full year of chart activity, so a one-week number one sits lower on it.

The real weekly high was the top of the chart, confirmed independently and matching the song's own fun_fact entry.

The Lineup That Made It Did Not Last

The five musicians in that press photo never finished another album together.

The wider arc of the group is covered in the Eagles the Band profile on this site.

Bernie Leadon Out, Don Felder In

This record was Bernie Leadon's last with the Eagles.

It was also Don Felder's first as a full member rather than a hired hand.

Leadon had pushed to keep the country-rock foundation intact.

The rest of the band wanted a harder, rock-driven sound.

He left after the album's tour.

Joe Walsh and the Road to Hotel California

On December 20, 1975, the Eagles announced Joe Walsh as his replacement.

Walsh had built his reputation with the James Gang.

The next Eagles album was Hotel California, released in December 1976.

That record leaned into exactly the darker, harder direction Leadon had resisted.

This single sits right at that turn, and the wider chart story runs through Music of the 70s.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is One of These Nights by the Eagles about?

One of These Nights is about wanting something you do not have yet, whether a person, money, or a place to call home.

Glenn Frey and Don Henley wrote it in 1975 as a deliberate move away from the band's country-rock sound.

Frey drew on Spinners and Al Green records, and Henley described the beat as a nod to disco.

Did One of These Nights reach number one?

One of These Nights reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 for the week of August 2, 1975, and held the top spot for one week.

It was the Eagles' second number one single of that year, after Best of My Love in March.

The parent album also became the band's first record to top the Billboard 200.

Who played the guitar solo on One of These Nights?

Don Felder played the guitar solo on this track.

He has said the solo is really an alto saxophone part played on guitar, built from blues-based lines and long sustained string bends.

Felder also wrote the song's opening bass riff and taught it to Randy Meisner, who had been snowed in during the early sessions.

Which album is One of These Nights from?

One of These Nights is the title track of the Eagles' fourth studio album, released in June 1975 on Asylum Records.

Bill Szymczyk produced it at Criteria Studios in Miami and in Los Angeles.

The record was the first Eagles album to reach number one, and the last to feature founding member Bernie Leadon.

Why did Bernie Leadon leave the Eagles after One of These Nights?

Bernie Leadon left because he wanted to protect the country-rock sound the band was built on, while the rest of the group wanted a harder rock direction.

This album, with its R&B and disco influence, made that split clear.

He departed after the tour, and Joe Walsh was announced as his replacement on December 20, 1975.
