Best Songs of the 70s: The Ultimate Decade Ranking

One single ballad outsold every other song of the entire decade.

"You Light Up My Life" by Debby Boone did something no song had done before.

It topped the chart for ten straight weeks.

That alone made it the biggest hit single of the whole 70s.

The best songs of the 70s were not all disco, and they were not all rock either.

The decade moved through at least four distinct moods before it was over.

It opened quiet and introspective, then turned toward glam and groove.

From there it exploded into disco at its commercial peak, and closed by pointing straight at the 80s.

This is a tour through the best songs of the 70s, era by era.

A few records stand outside any single era entirely.

The artists behind the best songs of the 70s: Paul McCartney, Rod Stewart, Elton John, the Bee Gees, and Debby Boone.

1970 to 1972: Where the Best Songs of the 70s Begin

The decade opened in the shadow of a breakup.

The Beatles were done, and each former member was suddenly on their own.

George Harrison got there first.

His 1970 song "My Sweet Lord" reached number one.

That made him the first of the four former Beatles to top the charts as a solo artist.

The mood around him was quiet and organic.

Simon & Garfunkel, Neil Young, and America each defined that early-70s sound in their own way.

  • Simon & Garfunkel with "Bridge Over Troubled Water"
  • Neil Young with "Heart of Gold"
  • America with "A Horse with No Name"

Soul music was moving in its own direction at the same time.

The Jackson 5's "I Want You Back" brought pure youthful energy to the top of the charts.

Marvin Gaye's "Let's Get It On" carried a very different, more grown kind of soul instead.

One of the decade's most memorable chart-toppers arrived under the saddest circumstances.

Janis Joplin reached number one in 1971 with "Me and Bobby McGee," released only after her death.

One Artist, Four Ways to the Top

No other artist matched Paul McCartney's reach across the decade.

He topped the charts in four completely different configurations.

  • As a solo artist
  • In a duo with Linda McCartney
  • As the leader of Wings
  • As a member of the Beatles

That kind of range belongs to its own category entirely, outside any single era of the decade.

How the 70s Charts Actually Worked

The Billboard Hot 100 is the main song chart of the American music industry.

It is updated every week by Billboard magazine.

During the 1970s, the chart was based on a combination of each single's weekly physical sales figures and its airplay on American radio stations.

That combination is part of why novelty hits and soundtrack singles could reach number one right alongside the era's biggest disco and rock stars.

1973 to 1975: Glam and Groove Rising

The sound of the charts grew more theatrical in the middle of the decade.

Elton John's "Bennie and the Jets" leaned all the way into that theatricality.

Singer-songwriters were still very much part of the picture, too.

Jim Croce and John Denver both built major careers on that more intimate, story-driven style.

Soul and funk were reaching for something bigger at the same time.

Stevie Wonder pushed the genre forward with two major hits.

  • "Superstition"
  • "Sir Duke"

Funk acts further from the mainstream spotlight were starting to break through as well.

  • Ohio Players
  • Labelle

One 1974 hit doubled back on the previous decade entirely.

Grand Funk took "The Loco-Motion" to number one that year.

The song had already been a hit once before, for Little Eva in the 1960s.

Disco itself was only just beginning to stir.

Van McCoy's "The Hustle" pulled the sound out of the clubs and toward the mainstream for the first time.

1976 to 1978: Disco's Peak

Disco went from a club sound to the decade's defining commercial force in these three years.

No act embodied that shift more completely than the Bee Gees.

They scored nine number-one hits that stayed at the top for a combined 27 weeks.

That run made them the most successful group of the entire decade.

Saturday Night Fever's soundtrack only cemented that dominance further.

Other acts pushed disco just as far in their own direction.

  • Donna Summer with "Hot Stuff"
  • Chic with "Le Freak"
  • KC and the Sunshine Band

Disco's commercial takeover produced some unexpected chart-toppers along the way.

A disco version of the Star Wars theme by Meco reached the top of the charts.

So did the novelty track "Disco Duck" by Rick Dees, and even a television theme, "Theme from S.W.A.T."

Not every major hit from these years was disco, though.

Several acts kept pop and rock just as commercially strong right alongside it.

  • The Eagles with "Hotel California" and "Heartache Tonight"
  • Fleetwood Mac with "Dreams"
  • The Carpenters with "Close to You"
  • Barbra Streisand with "The Way We Were"

1979: Turning Toward the 80s

By 1979, the charts were already leaning toward a different decade entirely.

Disco was still charting strong, with Chic and Donna Summer both still very much in the mix.

But a sharper, more modern sound was pushing in right alongside it.

  • Blondie with "Heart of Glass"
  • The Knack with "My Sharona"
  • Herb Alpert with "Rise"
  • "Pop Muzik" by M

Each of those songs pointed somewhere the 70s had not quite been yet.

  • New wave
  • Power pop
  • The electronic sound of the decade to come

Taken together, these four eras are what people usually mean when they talk about the best songs of the 70s.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the biggest hit single of the 70s?

"You Light Up My Life" by Debby Boone was the biggest hit single of the entire decade, and arguably the most dominant of the best songs of the 70s by that measure alone.

It was the first song in history to top the Billboard Hot 100 for ten straight weeks, a record no other song from the era came close to matching.

Which group had the most number-one hits in the 70s?

The Bee Gees were the most successful group of the 70s.

They scored nine number-one hits across the decade, and those songs stayed at the top of the charts for a combined 27 weeks, more cumulative time than any other act managed.

Which Beatle had the first number-one hit as a solo artist?

George Harrison was the first of the four former Beatles to reach number one as a solo artist.

His 1970 song "My Sweet Lord" topped the charts shortly after the group's breakup, ahead of any solo hit from Lennon, McCartney, or Starr.

How did Paul McCartney top the charts in more than one way?

Paul McCartney reached number one in four separate configurations during the 70s.

He did it as a solo artist, in a duo with Linda McCartney, as the leader of Wings, and as a member of the Beatles, a range no other artist of the decade matched.

Which song reached number one shortly after its singer's death?

Janis Joplin reached number one in 1971 with "Me and Bobby McGee," a song released only after her death.

It became one of the most memorable posthumous chart-toppers of the whole decade, arriving right at the start of the era it helped define.

What novelty songs became unlikely number-one hits in the 70s?

Disco's mainstream takeover produced some surprising chart-toppers.

A disco version of the Star Wars theme by Meco reached number one, and so did the novelty track "Disco Duck" by Rick Dees.

Even a television theme, "Theme from S.W.A.T.," climbed all the way to the top of the charts during this same stretch.
