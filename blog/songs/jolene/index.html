Jolene: The Chart Record It Finally Broke, 52 Years Later

Jolene spent 52 years as one of the most famous songs that never actually became a pop hit.

Dolly Parton wrote it about a real woman who kept flirting with her husband at the bank.

She borrowed the name from a little girl with red hair who asked for an autograph after a show.

The record topped the country chart in February 1974 and then quietly stalled everywhere else.

In September 2026, weeks after Parton's death, it finally cracked the Billboard Top 40 for the first time ever.

This is the chart-verified story, built from this site's own Billboard data and cross-checked against outside chart history.

The Bank Teller, the Fan, and the Name Dolly Parton Combined

Jolene did not come from a single moment of inspiration.

It came from two unrelated people who happened to collide in Dolly Parton's head at the same time.

The Woman at the Bank

A red-haired bank teller in Nashville had developed a habit of flirting with Parton's husband, Carl Dean, whenever he came in.

"She got this terrible crush on my husband," Parton has said.

"He just loved going to the bank because she paid him so much attention."

Parton, half joking and half not, decided to write a song about the situation before it went any further.

The Fan Who Gave It a Name

The name and the look of the character came from somewhere else entirely.

At one of her shows, a young fan with striking red hair, fair skin and green eyes asked Dolly Parton for an autograph.

When the girl said her name was Jolene, Parton reportedly told her, "That sounds like a song."

She combined the fan's name and appearance with the bank teller's behavior, and the song was born.

Parton has called it one of the most frequently covered songs of her entire catalog.

Recording Jolene at RCA Studio B

Parton cut Jolene at RCA Studio B in Nashville on May 22, 1973.

Producer Bob Ferguson ran the session.

Guitarist Chip Young played the thumb-picked line that opens the record, the part most listeners now hum without thinking about it.

Wayne Moss added a second, complementary steel-string guitar part that enters on the second refrain.

The finished track runs two minutes and 42 seconds, short even by the standards of country radio at the time.

RCA released it as a single in January 1974, with "Love, You're So Beautiful Tonight" on the B-side.

Three weeks after that Studio B session, Parton returned to record another song from the same stretch of writing.

That one became "I Will Always Love You", written as a farewell to her longtime partner Porter Wagoner.

Where Jolene Landed on the Charts in 1974

Jolene topped Billboard's country singles chart for the week of February 2, 1974.

This site's own country chart data confirms it as one of three solo number ones Parton scored that year.

On the year-end 1974 country chart it finished at number 6, a strong showing for a song with only one week at the top.

Why the Country Hit Barely Touched Pop

The pop side of the story looked very different.

Jolene did not reach the Hot 100 until the chart dated March 26, 1974, debuting at number 92.

It climbed no higher than number 60 before falling back off.

On Cash Box's competing pop chart it peaked at number 80, and on the Adult Contemporary chart it reached number 44.

A UK release two years later did better, reaching number 7 in 1976 and giving Parton her first UK chart hit.

The Chart Record Jolene Just Broke in 2026

Dolly Parton died on August 25, 2026, in Nashville, at age 80, after a brief battle with cancer.

In the week that followed, US streams of Jolene rose 583% and radio airplay audience reached 6.2 million listeners.

Stations that had rarely played her in years put her catalog back on the air.

The Mood Song Matcher still tags the song nostalgic and melancholy, a mood that only deepened that week.

On the Hot 100 dated September 5, 2026, Jolene reentered the chart at number 16.

That was its first time ever inside the Top 40, a full 52 years after its original 1974 chart run.

It was not alone in that surge.

It joined 26 other Parton recordings that simultaneously broke into the top 30 of the US iTunes chart that week, a record for the most tracks by one artist in that span.

Few songs on Music of the 70s have taken five decades to reach an audience this size.

The Grammy Recognition and the Covers That Followed

Jolene picked up two Grammy nominations in its own decade for Best Female Country Vocal Performance, in 1975 and again in 1976 for a live recording.

It did not win either time.

The Grammy Hall of Fame added the song to the induction class it announced in December 2013.

Three Covers, Three Very Different Outcomes

  • The White Stripes released a live version in 2004 that reached #16 on the UK Singles Chart and topped the UK Indie Singles Chart.
  • Pentatonix recorded it in 2016 with Parton herself as a guest vocalist, and the track won the Grammy for Best Country Duo/Group Performance in 2017.
  • Beyoncé reworked it on 2024's Cowboy Carter, taking it to #7 on the Hot 100, the highest chart position any version of the song has reached.

Parton publicly praised the Beyoncé version after it charted, saying Beyoncé was "giving that girl some trouble and she deserves it."

A Song Still Being Answered Back

Jolene has inspired its own small genre of response songs written from the other woman's point of view.

Cam released "Diane" in 2017 as a direct lyrical answer.

Chapel Hart followed with "You Can Have Him Jolene" in 2021.

Few country songs from the decade still generate new material five decades later.

This one remains one of the rare ones that does.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the real story behind Dolly Parton's Jolene?

Jolene was inspired by two real people, not one.

A red-haired bank teller had a habit of flirting with Dolly Parton's husband, Carl Dean, whenever he came in.

Parton borrowed the name from a young fan with red hair and green eyes who asked for an autograph at a show.

She combined the fan's name and looks with the bank teller's behavior to write the song.

Did Jolene ever reach number one on the pop charts?

No, Jolene never reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100.

It topped Billboard's country chart for the week of February 2, 1974, Parton's second solo country number one.

On the pop side it peaked at just number 60 in 1974, a common gap for country hits of that decade that never crossed over commercially.

Why did Jolene finally break the Billboard Top 40 in 2026?

Jolene entered the Billboard Hot 100 at number 16 on the chart dated September 5, 2026, its first time ever in the Top 40.

The jump followed Dolly Parton's death on August 25, 2026.

US streams of the song rose 583% in the week after her passing as fans and radio stations returned to her catalog.

Has anyone else had a hit with Jolene?

Several artists have charted with Jolene covers.

The White Stripes reached number 16 on the UK Singles Chart in 2004 with a live version.

Pentatonix, featuring Parton herself, won a Grammy for their 2016 recording.

Beyoncé's 2024 version from Cowboy Carter reached number 7 on the Hot 100, the highest chart peak any version of the song has ever hit.

Did Jolene win a Grammy?

Dolly Parton's original recording of Jolene was nominated for Best Female Country Vocal Performance in 1975 and again in 1976 for a live version, but it did not win either time.

The song was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in the class announced in December 2013.

A later cover, Pentatonix's 2016 version featuring Parton, did win a Grammy in 2017.
