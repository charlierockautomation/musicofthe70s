Wish You Were Here Pink Floyd: Radio Intro and a Lost Friend

By Charlie · Published October 18, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

Wish You Were Here Pink Floyd is a song that opens by sounding like someone tuning a radio.

It then settles into two acoustic guitars and one of the most quoted choruses of 1975.

Many listeners hear it as a goodbye to Syd Barrett, the founder the band left behind.

This page covers the meaning, the recording, a buried violin, the charts and the covers, and it sits beside our full list of Pink Floyd songs.

Wish You Were Here Pink Floyd Meaning: Barrett, Waters or Both?

The standard reading is that the song is about Syd Barrett.

Barrett co-founded Pink Floyd and left the band in 1968.

By 1975 the group had made The Dark Side of the Moon, and the next album was built around absence.

The Day Barrett Walked Into Abbey Road

On June 5, 1975, Barrett appeared at the studio while the band finished mixing Shine On You Crazy Diamond.

Wikipedia describes him as overweight, with a shaved head and eyebrows, carrying a plastic bag.

  • Roger Waters did not recognise him and was reportedly reduced to tears.
  • David Gilmour assumed he was EMI staff.
  • Richard Wright realised who it was.
  • Nick Mason did not recognise him at first and was horrified when Gilmour named him.

Mason called the conversation desultory and not entirely sensible.

American Songwriter reports the same date and that the bandmates did not recognise him for several minutes.

What the Band Says the Words Mean

Neither Waters nor Gilmour has given the song one fixed meaning.

In the documentary The Story of Wish You Were Here, Waters says the lyrics are directed at himself.

Gilmour says he never performs the song without thinking of Barrett.

Waters later said it is open to interpretation.

That gap is why the title works for a lost friend, a lost version of yourself, or a lost band.

How Pink Floyd Recorded Wish You Were Here in 1975

The band recorded the album from January to July 1975 at Abbey Road Studios in London.

Columbia released it in the US on September 13, a day after Harvest in the UK.

An Opening Built Like a Radio Dial

The song opens with a 12-string acoustic guitar part processed to sound like an AM radio.

On the album it comes straight out of Have a Cigar, as if someone is spinning past stations.

Those stations include a radio play and the finale of Tchaikovsky's Fourth Symphony.

American Songwriter describes a second, cleaner guitar that enters over the distant first one.

Gilmour then sings the verses in what American Songwriter calls a wounded baritone, before the full band joins.

Gilmour can be heard drinking, coughing and breathing heavily in the intro.

A popular belief ties that to quitting smoking, but Gilmour later said he never smoked.

Stéphane Grappelli and the Violin Nobody Credited

Gilmour invited the jazz violinist Stéphane Grappelli and suggested a country fiddle ending.

Grappelli charged a session fee of 300 pounds.

Yehudi Menuhin was recording in a downstairs studio and declined to take part.

In the mix, Grappelli's contribution was almost removed, and it can be heard faintly around 5:21.

Waters said crediting him in the sleeve notes for something so inaudible would be insulting.

Grappelli still received the agreed fee.

The 2011 Experience and Immersion editions include an alternative version with the violin much more prominent.

What the Critics Say

  • Rolling Stone ranked it number 302 on its 2021 list of the 500 greatest songs.
  • Billboard ranked it number one of the 50 greatest Pink Floyd songs.
  • Louder Sound ranked it number three.
  • Listeners of New York's Q104.3 voted it the 18th best rock song of all time in 2021.

Gilmour called it a very simple country song and said its emotional weight puts it among the band's best.

Covers and Live Versions of Wish You Were Here

Wish You Were Here Pink Floyd at Live 8 in 2005

In July 2005, Waters reunited with Gilmour, Wright and Mason at Live 8 in London.

They played the song together, and Waters and Gilmour shared the lead vocal.

The same year, Waters and Eric Clapton played it at the Tsunami Aid concert.

The 1995 Live Single and Later Editions

A live version recorded in Rome on September 20, 1994, came out as a single in the UK and Europe on July 20, 1995.

It reached number 13 on Billboard's Album Rock Tracks chart.

The 2025 Wish You Were Here 50 box set adds a previously unreleased pedal steel instrumental mix.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote Wish You Were Here by Pink Floyd?

David Gilmour and Roger Waters wrote Wish You Were Here, released on September 12, 1975.

Waters wrote the lyrics to an opening guitar part from Gilmour, and Gilmour sings the lead vocal.

Pink Floyd recorded it at Abbey Road Studios in London during the album sessions that ran from January to July 1975.

Is Wish You Were Here about Syd Barrett?

Wish You Were Here is often read as a tribute to Syd Barrett, the Pink Floyd founder who left in 1968.

Roger Waters has said the lyrics are aimed at himself, and later called them open to interpretation.

David Gilmour says he never plays the song without thinking of Barrett.

What is the radio sound at the start of Wish You Were Here?

The opening of Wish You Were Here is a 12-string acoustic guitar part processed to sound like an AM radio.

On the album, the track follows Have a Cigar as if a radio is being tuned through several stations, including a radio play and the finale of Tchaikovsky's Fourth Symphony.

Who played the violin on Wish You Were Here?

Jazz violinist Stéphane Grappelli played the violin on Wish You Were Here after David Gilmour invited him to add a country fiddle ending.

His part was almost mixed out and is faint near the 5:21 mark.

Grappelli received his agreed fee of 300 pounds.

Did Wish You Were Here reach number one?

The Wish You Were Here album reached number one on both the Billboard 200 in the US and the UK Albums Chart in 1975.

The title track was not a US chart single in the 1970s.

This site's Billboard Hot 100 data has no Pink Floyd entry for it.
