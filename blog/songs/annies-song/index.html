Annie's Song: John Denver's Ski-Lift Love Song

By Charlie · Published October 22, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

Annie's Song is the love letter John Denver wrote for his wife in about ten minutes.

It spent two weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in the summer of 1974.

It also topped the UK singles chart, which is rare for an American folk singer.

This page covers how it was written, how it was recorded, how it charted and who has covered it since.

Annie's Song: Written on an Aspen Ski Lift

The story of Annie's Song starts with a ride up a mountain, not a night in a studio.

Ten Minutes on a Chairlift

Wikipedia says Denver wrote the song in January 1973 as an ode to his wife, Annie Martell Denver.

He reportedly wrote it in about ten and a half minutes, on a ski lift going up Aspen Mountain in Colorado.

That is a fast turnaround for a melody that millions of people could hum by the summer of 1974.

If you like your 70s love songs gentle and unhurried, the Mood Song Matcher can line up more of the same mood.

The Tchaikovsky Question

Producer Milt Okun has said the melody resembled the second movement of Tchaikovsky's Symphony No. 5.

In Okun's account, only the first five notes of the Tchaikovsky remained after Denver reworked the tune.

That is Okun's recollection, as Wikipedia reports it, and not something the record itself proves.

Recording and Release

Denver wrote, recorded and performed the track himself, with Okun producing.

RCA Records released it in June 1974 as the lead single from the album Back Home Again.

The flip side was a quiet song called "Cool an' Green an' Shady."

You can hear Annie's Song on the Listen Now jukebox too.

Wikipedia does not name a studio, an orchestra or an arranger for the recording, so this page does not either.

On the Charts: Two Weeks at Number One

Annie's Song reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 for the chart weeks starting July 27 and August 3, 1974.

It was Denver's second US number one, and it replaced George McCrae's "Rock Your Baby" at the top.

Roberta Flack's "Feel Like Makin' Love" followed, which this site's weekly Hot 100 data confirms.

Year-End Standing

The year-end lists rank the song a little differently from the weekly run.

A year-end rank reflects a whole year of sales and airplay, not a peak, so it sits well below a number one.

Wikipedia reports these 1974 year-end positions.

  • US Adult Contemporary: No. 5.
  • US Hot 100: No. 25.
  • UK: No. 15.
  • Canada: No. 19.
  • Australia: No. 51.

Certifications

  • United States (RIAA): Gold, for 1,000,000 units.
  • United Kingdom (BPI): Platinum, for 600,000 units.
  • New Zealand (RMNZ): Platinum, for 30,000 units.

Wikipedia notes that the US figure is based on shipments and that the UK and New Zealand figures combine sales and streaming.

Legacy and Parodies

A song this plain and this sincere is easy to tease, and a few people did.

Denver's Big Years

Annie's Song was one of four Hot 100 number ones for Denver in the 1970s, according to our John Denver songs page.

Wikipedia calls it his only major hit single in the UK.

His earlier signature hit is its own story, and you can read it in Take Me Home, Country Roads.

Monty Python and the Football Terraces

Wikipedia lists two unlikely afterlives for the tune.

  • Monty Python parodied the opening bars on Monty Python's Contractual Obligation Album, then removed it and replaced it with an apology on later pressings, on legal advice.
  • Sheffield United supporters use the melody for "The Greasy Chip Butty Song."

The Basque band Egan also performed Bilintx's "Loiolan Jai Jai" to the same tune, according to Wikipedia.

Covers and Adaptations

The melody has travelled well beyond English, and one version is in Finnish.

James Galway

The flautist James Galway recorded an instrumental version four years after the original.

Wikipedia says it became his only major British hit.

Finnish Versions

The song has a Finnish life as "Olet mun kaikuluotain," which means "You're My Sonar."

  • Freeman recorded the Finnish version in 1976.
  • Ville Valo released a new Finnish version on July 4, 2016, on Love Records, with lyrics by Hector.
  • Valo's version reached number one on the Finnish Download Chart.
  • Its video, directed by Ykä Järvinen, won the 2017 Emma Award for Video of the Year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote Annie's Song?

John Denver wrote Annie's Song himself, as an ode to his wife, Annie Martell Denver.

Wikipedia says he wrote it in January 1973, reportedly in about ten and a half minutes, on a ski lift going up Aspen Mountain in Colorado.

Milt Okun produced the recording.

How many weeks was Annie's Song at number one?

Annie's Song spent two weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100, the chart weeks starting July 27 and August 3, 1974, according to this site's weekly data.

It replaced George McCrae's Rock Your Baby.

Roberta Flack's Feel Like Makin' Love took over the following week.

Did Annie's Song reach number one in the UK?

Annie's Song reached number one on the UK singles chart in 1974.

Wikipedia describes it as John Denver's only major hit single in Britain.

The BPI later certified it Platinum in the UK, and it also topped the charts in Canada and Ireland.

Is Annie's Song based on Tchaikovsky?

Producer Milt Okun said the melody of Annie's Song resembled the second movement of Tchaikovsky's Fifth Symphony.

Okun's account, as reported by Wikipedia, is that only the first five notes of the Tchaikovsky remained after John Denver reworked the tune.

It is Okun's claim, not an established fact.

What album is Annie's Song on?

Annie's Song is on John Denver's album Back Home Again.

RCA Records released it in June 1974 as the lead single from that album.

The B-side was Cool an' Green an' Shady, the single runs 2:58, and Milt Okun produced the recording.
