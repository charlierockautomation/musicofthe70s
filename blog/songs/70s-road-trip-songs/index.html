The Short Answer: What Makes a 70s Road Trip Song

A 70s road trip song is a Hot 100 hit from 1970 to 1979 that this site's song data tags for driving.

The list leans on classic rock, but it also holds country, pop and soft rock.

Here is what the list shows at a glance:

  • 17 of the 30 songs carry the classic rock tag in this site's song data.
  • Nine titles name a vehicle, from "Hot Rod Lincoln" to "Jet Airliner."
  • The longest run at number one is three weeks, held by America's "A Horse with No Name."
  • 6 songs reached number one, and 24 stayed on the year-end lists only.

How These 70s Road Trip Songs Were Chosen

The starting pool came from this site's own song data.

It tags every year-end Hot 100 song for 1970 to 1979 with a vibe.

260 songs are tagged drive and have a YouTube video on record.

From that pool, 30 made the list on three tests:

  • The song is not already on the 70s party, dance or love songs lists.
  • Its chart facts can be checked against the weekly or year-end Billboard data on this site.
  • Its chart line is computed from that data, never typed from memory.

5 songs in the drive pool already sit on those three companion lists, so they are left out here.

Where the Year-End Rank on These 70s Road Trip Songs Comes From

The Year-End Rank column is a position on that year's Billboard year-end list.

It is not the song's weekly peak.

"Band on the Run" sits at 22 for 1974, yet it reached number one on June 8, 1974.

Classic Rock Carries the Highway

17 of the 30 songs are tagged classic rock.

Two of them reached number one.

"Me and Bobby McGee" got there in March 1971, and "Band on the Run" in June 1974.

Four songs are tagged country.

1970 to 1973: The Busiest Stretch

1970 to 1973 holds the most songs, with 14.

It opens with Creedence Clearwater Revival's "Travelin' Band" and Vanity Fare's "Hitchin' a Ride," both from the 1970 list.

Four of the six number ones came in these years.

The last was Grand Funk Railroad's "We're an American Band" on September 29, 1973.

Jim Croce's "Bad, Bad Leroy Brown" took two weeks in July 1973.

Its Bad, Bad Leroy Brown page has the rest.

1974 to 1976: Wings, Truckers and Detectives

Nine songs fall in these years, from Golden Earring's "Radar Love" to Foghat's "Slow Ride."

"The Rockford Files Theme" by Mike Post is here at number 85 for 1975.

"Convoy" by C. W. McCall reached number one on January 10, 1976.

It followed "Saturday Night" and gave way to "I Write the Songs."

1977 and 1978: No Number Ones, Plenty of Miles

Five songs sit in these two years, and none reached number one.

Gerry Rafferty's "Baker Street" is the highest ranked, at number 26 on the 1978 year-end list.

Fleetwood Mac's "Don't Stop" at 52 and Jackson Browne's "Running on Empty" at 82 sit lower down.

1979: Two Songs, Two Stories

Dire Straits' "Sultans of Swing" is number 61 for 1979.

The Charlie Daniels Band's "The Devil Went Down to Georgia" is number 50 on the year-end list.

Neither reached number one.

"A Horse with No Name" Runs the Longest

America's "A Horse with No Name" reached number one on March 25, 1972, and held for three weeks.

It followed "Heart of Gold" and gave way to "The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face."

Two Weeks Each for Janis Joplin and Jim Croce

Janis Joplin's "Me and Bobby McGee" reached number one on March 20, 1971, for two weeks.

Jim Croce's "Bad, Bad Leroy Brown" followed on July 21, 1973, for two more.

A Rank Is Not a Peak

The year-end list measures a whole year of chart life, not the highest weekly position.

So these ranks say how big the song was, not whether it climbed to the top.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best 70s road trip songs?

The best 70s road trip songs on this list include John Denver's "Take Me Home, Country Roads," Golden Earring's "Radar Love," America's "A Horse with No Name" and Jackson Browne's "Running on Empty." All 30 are checked against Billboard chart data, and every one can be played from the table at the top of the page, one after another.

What is the number one road trip song of the 70s?

America's "A Horse with No Name" has the longest run at number one on this list, with three weeks from March 25, 1972. Six of the 30 songs reached number one on the weekly Billboard Hot 100 file on this site.

Do 70s road trip songs have to be about driving?

No, this list mixes songs that name a road, a ride or a vehicle in the title with songs picked from the drive vibe tag in this site's song data. Only the title words are counted on this page, so no song's meaning is guessed.

Did every 70s road trip song on this list reach number one?

No, 6 of the 30 road trip songs on this list reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100. The other 24 are year-end Hot 100 entries, including "Take Me Home, Country Roads" at number 8 for 1971 and "Baker Street" at number 26 for 1978.

Where can I listen to 70s road trip songs?

You can play all 30 70s road trip songs from the table at the top of this page, with no sign-in. Press Play on any row and the list continues song after song until you press Stop. You can also open the same songs on Listen Now, this site's 70s music player.
