Time in a Bottle Jim Croce: The Number One He Never Heard

Time in a Bottle Jim Croce wrote two years before anyone outside his house ever heard it.

He wrote it the night he learned his wife Ingrid was pregnant with their son.

It sat as a quiet album track for a year and a half after that.

ABC Records didn't release it as a single until after Croce died.

It topped the Hot 100 that December and January, three months too late for him to hear it happen.

This is the chart-verified story, built from this site's own Billboard data and outside chart history.

The Night He Wrote Time in a Bottle

Jim Croce wrote this song in 1970, well before he had a record deal that mattered.

He wrote it the same night he found out his wife Ingrid was pregnant.

A Song Written for a Son He Hadn't Met Yet

The couple's son, Adrian James Croce, was born the following year.

Croce wanted to hold onto that moment before it passed him by.

The lyrics talk about saving time in a bottle, keeping the first days like this forever.

He wouldn't get nearly as much time with that son as the song hoped for.

Croce's guitarist and writing partner, Maury Muehleisen, joined him around the same period.

The two had built a close musical partnership by the time this song reached tape.

An Album Track Nobody Picked as a Single

ABC Records released Croce's album "You Don't Mess Around with Jim" in April 1972.

Time in a Bottle Jim Croce sat quietly near the end of that tracklist.

Two other songs from the same record got the single treatment first.

  • The title track, a comic story about a Chicago pool hustler, became Croce's first real hit.
  • "Operator (That's Not the Way It Feels)" followed it to radio that October.
  • Time in a Bottle stayed an album cut, with no single release planned at all.

Nobody at ABC treated the song as a priority at the time.

It read as a tender, slower moment on an album otherwise full of character sketches and bar-room humor.

That would change, but not for a reason anyone wanted.

Released Only After His Death

Jim Croce died on September 20, 1973.

A small plane carrying him crashed on takeoff from Natchitoches, Louisiana, after a concert at Northwestern State University.

He'd had his first number one, Bad Bad Leroy Brown, just two months earlier.

A Television Movie Changed the Song's Fate

Time in a Bottle Jim Croce got a second life that fall for a reason nobody could have planned.

The song was featured in the television movie "She Lives!"

Listener demand picked up fast after that broadcast.

The Timeline From Death to Number One

  1. September 20, 1973: Croce dies in a plane crash near Natchitoches, Louisiana.
  2. Fall 1973: Time in a Bottle airs as part of the television movie "She Lives!"
  3. December 1973: ABC Records releases the song as a single.
  4. Late December 1973 into January 1974: the song holds number one on the Hot 100.

Croce never knew any of that would happen.

He'd already moved on to a third album's worth of songs before he died.

Where Time in a Bottle Jim Croce Landed on the Charts

This site's own weekly Billboard data confirms the exact chart run.

Time in a Bottle held number one on the Hot 100 for the chart week of December 29, 1973, and again for the week of January 5, 1974.

That's two weeks at the top, spanning the turn of the year.

On Billboard's year-end chart for 1974, it finished 24th.

Two Number Ones, Split by Life and Death

Jim Croce had exactly two number one singles on the Hot 100.

The timing between them still stands out on any chart-history list.

Time in a Bottle Jim Croce became the second of those two, arriving under very different circumstances than the first.

That mood read differently once listeners knew how his year had actually ended.

A song written to hold onto time became one people played to remember someone who ran out of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the real story behind Time in a Bottle by Jim Croce?

Jim Croce wrote Time in a Bottle in 1970, the night he learned his wife Ingrid was pregnant with their son, Adrian James Croce.

He recorded it two years later for his album You Don't Mess Around with Jim, but it stayed a quiet album track rather than a single until after his death.

Did Time in a Bottle reach number one while Jim Croce was alive?

No, Jim Croce was not alive when Time in a Bottle reached number one.

He died in a plane crash on September 20, 1973, and ABC Records released the song as a single that December. It topped the Billboard Hot 100 the last week of 1973 and held the top spot into January 1974.

Why was Time in a Bottle released as a single so late?

Time in a Bottle sat as an album track for a year and a half because ABC Records had already chosen other songs as singles.

Interest picked up after the song was featured in the television movie She Lives!, and ABC issued it as a single that December, three months after Croce's death.

How does Time in a Bottle compare to Bad Bad Leroy Brown?

Bad Bad Leroy Brown became Jim Croce's first number one in July 1973, while he was alive to hear it on the radio.

Time in a Bottle became his second number one that December and January, a posthumous chart-topper released three months after he died in a plane crash.

What album did Time in a Bottle originally appear on?

Time in a Bottle originally appeared on Jim Croce's 1972 album You Don't Mess Around with Jim, released by ABC Records in April of that year.

The same album carried the title track and Operator (That's Not the Way It Feels), both released as singles before Time in a Bottle ever was.
