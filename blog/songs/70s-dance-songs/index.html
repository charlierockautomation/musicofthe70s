The Short Answer: What Makes a 70s Dance Song

A 70s dance song is a Billboard Hot 100 hit from 1970 to 1979 built around a groove that people moved to.

The list below is tilted toward the disco floor, but it starts with Motown and funk.

Here is what the list shows at a glance:

  • 22 of the 30 songs carry the disco tag in this site's song data.
  • 7 titles name dancing outright, from "Dancing Machine" to "He's the Greatest Dancer."
  • The longest run at number one is eight weeks, held by the Bee Gees' "Night Fever."
  • 21 songs reached number one, and 9 stayed on the year-end lists only.

These 70s Dance Songs Skip the Party Singalongs

No song here repeats on the companion 70s party songs list.

That page leans on singalong hits like "Joy to the World" and "Y.M.C.A."

This one keeps the tracks where the beat does the work.

How These 70s Dance Songs Were Chosen

The starting pool came from this site's own song data, which tags every year-end Hot 100 song for 1970 to 1979 with a vibe.

235 songs are tagged dance and have a YouTube video on record.

From that pool, 30 made the list on three tests:

  • The song is not already on the 70s party songs list.
  • Its chart facts can be checked against the weekly or year-end Billboard data on this site.
  • Its chart line is computed from that data, never typed from memory.

That is why the story column holds dates and neighbors, not trivia.

Where the Year-End Rank on These 70s Dance Songs Comes From

The Year-End Rank column is a position on that year's Billboard year-end list.

It is not the song's weekly peak.

"Da Ya Think I'm Sexy?" sits at 4 for 1979, yet it spent four weeks at number one.

Dancing Is the Favorite Word

7 of the 30 titles contain "dance," "dancing," "dancer" or "dancin'."

"You Should Be Dancing" and "Shadow Dancing" both reached number one.

"Dance, Dance, Dance (Yowsah, Yowsah, Yowsah)" did not, and sits at number 20 on the 1978 year-end list.

Boogie, Shake and the Disco Label

"Jungle Boogie," "Boogie Fever" and "Boogie Nights" carry the other favorite word.

"(Shake, Shake, Shake) Shake Your Booty" and "Shake Your Body (Down to the Ground)" ask for the same thing a different way.

Two titles use the word disco itself, "Disco Duck" and "Disco Inferno."

1970 to 1973: Motown and Funk Lead the Way

The early years are the thinnest on the list.

"I Want You Back" by The Jackson 5 reached number one on January 31, 1970, and Billy Preston's "Will It Go Round in Circles" took two weeks in July 1973.

James Brown's "Get on the Good Foot" is here too, at number 99 on the 1972 year-end list. The Get on the Good Foot page tells the rest.

1974 to 1976: The Floor Gets Louder

1974 to 1976 holds the most songs, with 11.

It runs from "Love's Theme" in February 1974 to "Disco Duck" in October 1976.

On the weekly chart, Elton John and Kiki Dee's "Don't Go Breaking My Heart" gave way to "You Should Be Dancing" on September 4, 1976.

"(Shake, Shake, Shake) Shake Your Booty" took over a week later, and "Play That Funky Music" followed.

1977 and 1978: Saturday Night Fever Takes Over

"Dancing Queen" reached number one on April 9, 1977.

"Don't Give Up on Us" held the top spot in between, and Thelma Houston's "Don't Leave Me This Way" followed on April 23.

The Bee Gees' "Night Fever" and Andy Gibb's "Shadow Dancing" then ran for 15 weeks at number one between them in 1978.

1979: The Floor at Full Volume

1979 gives the list its busiest single year, with six number ones: "Da Ya Think I'm Sexy?," "Hot Stuff," "Ring My Bell," "Bad Girls," "Good Times" and "Don't Stop 'til You Get Enough."

"Night Fever" and "Shadow Dancing" Own 1978

The Bee Gees' "Night Fever" reached number one on March 18, 1978, and stayed for eight weeks.

Dance Songs That Never Hit Number One

9 songs on the list never topped the weekly Hot 100.

Each is here because the year-end list proves it was played everywhere.

  • "Get on the Good Foot" by James Brown: number 99 on the 1972 year-end Hot 100.
  • "Dancing Machine" by The Jackson 5: number 5 on the 1974 year-end Hot 100.
  • "Jungle Boogie" by Kool & the Gang: number 12 on the 1974 year-end Hot 100.
  • "Boogie Nights" by Heatwave: number 93 on the 1977 year-end Hot 100.
  • "Dance, Dance, Dance (Yowsah, Yowsah, Yowsah)" by Chic: number 20 on the 1978 year-end Hot 100.
  • "Disco Inferno" by The Trammps: number 54 on the 1978 year-end Hot 100.
  • "He's the Greatest Dancer" by Sister Sledge: number 45 on the 1979 year-end Hot 100.
  • "I Was Made for Dancin'" by Leif Garrett: number 37 on the 1979 year-end Hot 100.
  • "Shake Your Body (Down to the Ground)" by The Jacksons: number 25 on the 1979 year-end Hot 100.

A Rank Is Not a Peak

The year-end list measures a whole year of chart life, not the highest weekly position.

So these ranks say how big the song was, not whether it climbed to the top.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best 70s dance songs?

The best 70s dance songs on this list include the Bee Gees' "Night Fever," Andy Gibb's "Shadow Dancing," ABBA's "Dancing Queen" and Donna Summer's "Bad Girls." All 30 are checked against Billboard chart data, and every one can be played from the table at the top of the page, one after another.

What is the number one dance song of the 70s?

The longest run at number one on this list belongs to the Bee Gees' "Night Fever," with eight weeks from March 18, 1978. Andy Gibb's "Shadow Dancing" followed with seven weeks from June 17, 1978. Both come from the weekly Billboard Hot 100 file on this site.

What is the difference between 70s dance songs and 70s party songs?

A 70s dance song here is picked for the floor itself, often with dancing named in the title, while the companion party songs list leans on singalong hits. The two lists share no songs. Each has its own 30 tracks and its own player.

Did every 70s dance song on this list reach number one?

No, 21 of the 30 dance songs on this list reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100. The other 9 are year-end Hot 100 entries, including "Dancing Machine" at number 5 for 1974 and "Dance, Dance, Dance (Yowsah, Yowsah, Yowsah)" at number 20 for 1978.

Where can I listen to 70s dance songs?

You can play all 30 70s dance songs from the table at the top of this page, with no sign-in. Press Play on any row and the list continues song after song until you press Stop. You can also open the same songs on Listen Now, this site's 70s music player.
