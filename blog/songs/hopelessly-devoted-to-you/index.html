Hopelessly Devoted to You: Grease Ballad Written Mid-Shoot

By Charlie · Published October 18, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

Hopelessly Devoted to You exists because Olivia Newton-John was owed a solo and nobody had written one.

Halfway through shooting Grease, her contract still had no song to point at.

John Farrar, her personal producer, wrote one and handed it to the film's team.

It went on to reach number three on the Hot 100 and earn an Oscar nomination.

Below you will find the story of the writing, the chart run, the night it lost to Last Dance and the covers that followed.

Hopelessly Devoted to You Meaning: Sandy's Torch Song

Wikipedia classes the song as a torch song from the Grease soundtrack.

Olivia Newton-John sings it in the film as Sandy Olsson, an Australian newcomer.

Sandy is still in love with Danny Zuko, the greaser gang leader played by John Travolta.

Danny has dismissed her in favor of his friends, and the song is her answer to that.

What the Song Says in Plain Terms

The message is simple and one-sided: she knows he has pushed her away, and she cannot stop caring.

That is the whole drama of the number, and it is why it works as a stand-alone ballad away from the screen.

A 50s Love Ballad With a 70s Finish

Wikipedia collects three contemporary trade reviews of the single.

  • Record World called it a "'50s-inspired love ballad with the production sound of the '70s."
  • Billboard praised Newton-John's vocal and described the production as string-laden, romantic and catchy.
  • Cash Box said it had appeal across Top 40, middle-of-the-road and country formats.

That last note matters, because the song did cross over in a real way, as the chart section below shows.

How John Farrar Wrote the Song Mid-Shoot

The Solo Nobody Had Written

Newton-John's contract entitled her to a vocal solo in the film.

Halfway through shooting, nothing had been written for her.

Farrar wrote the song and submitted it to the production team.

The team was reluctant at first, then approved it.

The Longest Lyric He Ever Wrote

In a June 2004 interview, Farrar said he spent the longest period writing the lyrics of any song he had ever written.

The finished words are plain, and he has said they took him longer than any others he wrote.

Recording and Release

Wikipedia lists the recording as made in 1977 at United/Western Studio.

It adds that the song was shot and recorded after the rest of the film was finished.

RSO Records released the single in June 1978 in the UK, with "Love Is a Many-Splendored Thing" on the B-side.

The Australian single followed in August 1978.

Hopelessly Devoted to You on the Charts

According to Wikipedia, the single peaked at number three on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1978.

It also reached number three on the Cash Box Top 100.

This site's Billboard year-end data ranks it number 35 for 1978, and that is a year-end rank, not a peak.

The Oscar Nomination and the Loss to Last Dance

Hopelessly Devoted to You was nominated for Best Original Song at the 51st Academy Awards.

The ceremony took place on April 9, 1979, at the Dorothy Chandler Pavilion in Los Angeles.

Farrar was credited for both music and lyrics.

On Stage and in Covers

Not in the Original Stage Musical

The song was written for the film, so it was not part of the original Grease stage musical.

The 1994 stage revival used "Since I Don't Have You," a 1950s standard, in that spot.

The 2007 revival added Hopelessly Devoted to You to the score.

It was also sung by Julianne Hough in Grease: Live in 2016.

Sonia's 1994 Cover

English singer Sonia took over the role of Sandy in the West End production of Grease in 1994, replacing Debbie Gibson.

She released her version on July 18, 1994, as a double A-side with "The Anthem Medley."

Mark Cyrus produced it, and it ran 2:58.

It reached number 61 on the UK Singles Chart, her first single not to reach the UK top 40.

Alan Jones of Music Week gave it three out of five and called it a straightforward reading.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote Hopelessly Devoted to You?

John Farrar wrote Hopelessly Devoted to You, the ballad Olivia Newton-John sings as Sandy in the 1978 film Grease.

Farrar was her personal producer.

He wrote the music and the lyrics, and he also produced the recording, which came out on RSO Records in June 1978.

Why was Hopelessly Devoted to You added to Grease?

Hopelessly Devoted to You was added to Grease because Olivia Newton-John was contractually entitled to a vocal solo, and none had been written halfway through shooting.

John Farrar wrote one and submitted it.

The production team was reluctant at first but eventually approved the song.

Did Hopelessly Devoted to You reach number one?

Hopelessly Devoted to You peaked at number three on the US Billboard Hot 100 in 1978, so it did not reach number one in America.

It did top the charts in Canada, Belgium, Ireland and the Netherlands, and it reached number two in the UK and Australia.

Was Hopelessly Devoted to You nominated for an Oscar?

Hopelessly Devoted to You was nominated for Best Original Song at the 51st Academy Awards, held on April 9, 1979.

It lost to Last Dance by Paul Jabara, from the film Thank God It's Friday.

John Farrar was the nominated songwriter.

Is Hopelessly Devoted to You in the Grease stage musical?

Hopelessly Devoted to You was not part of the original Grease stage musical, because it was written for the 1978 film.

The 1994 revival used Since I Don't Have You in that spot instead.

The song was added to the score for the 2007 revival.
