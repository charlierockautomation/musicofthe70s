I Am Woman by Helen Reddy: The Story Behind the Anthem

By Charlie · Published October 18, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

I Am Woman is a song that flopped on its first release and then took five months to reach number one.

Helen Reddy first put it on a 1971 album, and almost nobody noticed.

A re-recording in 1972 crept onto the Hot 100 at number 99 and finally climbed to the top in December.

Below are the two authors who disagree about who did what, the charts, the Grammy speech and the honors that followed.

I Am Woman: Two Writers, Two Memories

Helen Reddy's Account

Wikipedia credits I Am Woman to two Australian musicians, Helen Reddy and Ray Burton.

Reddy says she was looking for a song about female empowerment and felt "divinely inspired" after a night in bed.

She wrote the lyric the next day and handed it to Burton.

Wikipedia notes that she offered no evidence for that account.

Ray Burton's Account

Burton remembers it differently, according to Wikipedia.

He says Reddy gave him scribbled lyrics and he wrote the song in about three hours one Sunday night.

He says he rewrote a few bits and built a melody around them.

Reddy insists that Burton did not change a word of the lyrics.

The 1972 Re-Recording That Became the Hit

A Quiet First Release

Reddy first recorded I Am Woman for her debut album, I Don't Know How to Love Him, released in May 1971.

Wikipedia says it also played during the closing credits of the film Stand Up and Be Counted.

That original version ran just over two minutes.

The hit we remember is a different recording.

The Session at SunWest Studios

Reddy re-recorded the song on April 23, 1972, at SunWest Studios, with Jay Senter producing, according to Wikipedia.

She was asked to add a verse and a chorus because the original was so short.

Wikipedia lists these session players:

  • Mike Deasy on guitar.
  • Jim Gordon on drums.
  • Leland Sklar on bass.
  • Kathy Deasy on backing vocals.

Reddy and Jeff Wald had expected a live band session.

Senter had already recorded the backing track, which had been intended for the B-side "Don't Mess with a Woman."

Reddy sang over it, and that is the version Capitol released in May 1972.

Legacy: A Grammy, a Registry and a Movement

Firsts and a Famous Speech

Wikipedia credits I Am Woman with several firsts:

  • The first Capitol Records number one since "Ode to Billie Joe" in 1967.
  • The first Billboard number one by an Australian-born artist.
  • The first Australian-penned song to win a Grammy.

Reddy won Best Pop Vocal Performance, Female, at the 15th Annual Grammy Awards on March 3, 1973.

In her acceptance speech she thanked God "because She makes everything possible."

An Anthem for the Women's Movement

The song became a feminist anthem for the women's liberation movement, according to Wikipedia.

Betty Friedan wrote that a 1973 NOW convention gala ended with the song, and attendees danced in a large circle.

Not every feminist liked it, and Wikipedia says some objected to the pregnancy imagery, especially after Roe v. Wade.

Reddy replied that the message was broader, about believing in yourself.

Honors Decades Later

Wikipedia reports two registry honors for the 1972 recording:

  • Added to Australia's Sounds of Australia registry in 2009.
  • Selected for the Library of Congress National Recording Registry in 2025.

BMI's announcement of the 2025 class lists the song with Reddy and Burton as its writers.

Bobbie Roy's country version reached number 51 on the US Country chart in early 1973.

Reddy died in September 2020, and the song climbed to number 2 on Australia's digital sales chart.

An ARIA Music Awards tribute that year featured Australian female singers and a virtual choir of more than twenty, introduced by Julia Gillard.

Wikipedia also covers Burton's later dispute over royalties, which he took to court in 1998 and Reddy disputes.

The song's afterlife is part of why Reddy still turns up all over Music of the 70s.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote I Am Woman?

Helen Reddy and Ray Burton, two Australian musicians, wrote I Am Woman, according to Wikipedia.

Reddy says she wrote the lyric herself and Burton changed none of it.

Burton says he wrote the song in about three hours one Sunday night.

Reddy performed it, and Capitol Records released it as a single in May 1972.

When did I Am Woman reach number one?

I Am Woman reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in the issue dated December 9, 1972, according to Wikipedia.

This site's weekly Hot 100 data agrees, giving it one week at the top.

The single had first entered the Hot 100 at number 99 in June 1972, more than five months earlier.

Did I Am Woman win a Grammy?

Yes, Helen Reddy won the Grammy for Best Pop Vocal Performance, Female, for I Am Woman at the 15th Annual Grammy Awards on March 3, 1973, according to Wikipedia.

Her acceptance speech thanked God, "because She makes everything possible."

Wikipedia calls it the first Australian-penned song to win a Grammy.

Is the I Am Woman single the same recording as the album version?

No, the hit single of I Am Woman is a re-recording made on April 23, 1972, according to Wikipedia.

Reddy first recorded the song for her 1971 debut album, I Don't Know How to Love Him.

That version ran just over two minutes, so she added a verse and chorus for the single.

Is I Am Woman in the National Recording Registry?

Yes, the Library of Congress selected Helen Reddy's 1972 recording of I Am Woman for the National Recording Registry in 2025, according to Wikipedia.

BMI's announcement of the 2025 class also lists the song, written by Reddy and Ray Burton.

The recording was also added to Australia's Sounds of Australia registry in 2009.
