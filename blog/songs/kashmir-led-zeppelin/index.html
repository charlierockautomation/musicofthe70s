Kashmir Led Zeppelin: The 8:37 Song, Explained

By Charlie · Published October 19, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

Kashmir by Led Zeppelin is named for a place the band never visited.

Robert Plant wrote the lyric after a drive through southern Morocco, and the 8:37 track appears on Physical Graffiti, released in 1975.

Its riff sits in a different meter from its vocal, and an orchestra of strings and brass answers the guitar.

This Kashmir Led Zeppelin guide covers the story, the recording, the chart numbers, the rankings and the lawsuit.

Kashmir Led Zeppelin: A Moroccan Drive and a Riff in Two Meters

Where the Lyric Came From

According to Wikipedia, Robert Plant wrote the lyrics in 1973, right after the band's 1973 US tour.

None of the band members had visited Kashmir.

Plant was inspired by a drive through a desert area in southern Morocco.

How the Riff Was Built

Jimmy Page and John Bonham recorded a demo in late 1973.

Plant later added lyrics and a middle section.

In early 1974, John Paul Jones added the orchestration, and session players were brought in for strings and horns.

Jones also added a Mellotron part.

The finished track is on Physical Graffiti, one of the albums covered in our Led Zeppelin songs post.

DADGAD and Two Meters at Once

Page played Kashmir in DADGAD tuning, which he also used on White Summer and Black Mountain Side.

The guitar riff is in triple meter, and the vocal is in quadruple meter.

Wikipedia calls that a polymeter.

Page used a Danelectro, which was unusual for him, because he normally played a Gibson Les Paul or a Fender Telecaster.

Who Played on It

Wikipedia credits these musicians:

  • Robert Plant: vocals.
  • Jimmy Page: electric guitars, six-string and twelve-string.
  • John Paul Jones: bass guitar and Mellotron.
  • John Bonham: drums.
  • Unidentified musicians: strings and brass.

Kashmir Live, in Film and in Court

Kashmir Led Zeppelin on Stage

Led Zeppelin played Kashmir at almost every concert after its 1975 debut.

Wikipedia lists these notable live versions:

  • Knebworth, 1979: the version on the Led Zeppelin DVD from 2003.
  • Atlantic Records 40th Anniversary concert, 1988.
  • Page and Plant with an Egyptian and Moroccan orchestra, on No Quarter in 1994, and with an orchestra on their 1995 tour.
  • The O2, London, December 10, 2007, with Jason Bonham on drums, released on Celebration Day in 2012.

The O2 performance earned a 2014 Grammy nomination for Best Rock Performance.

Signifying Rapper and the 1994 Lawsuit

The 1988 Schoolly D song Signifying Rapper sampled Kashmir.

It was then used in the 1992 film Bad Lieutenant.

In 1994, Page and Plant successfully sued HBO to remove the song from televised showings of the film.

A copyright ruling also ordered Live Home Video and Aries Film Releasing to destroy unsold copies of the film.
