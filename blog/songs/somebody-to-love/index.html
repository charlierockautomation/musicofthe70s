Somebody to Love: The Song Freddie Mercury Wrote Alone

Somebody to Love came out of a single voice, not four.

Freddie Mercury wrote every word and every note by himself.

He built it as his own answer to the gospel records he loved.

The goal was an Aretha Franklin feeling, played by a rock band.

Queen then multitracked just three singers into something that plays back like a hundred.

The song became one of the biggest hits off A Day at the Races, Queen's first fully self-produced album.

Mercury reportedly liked it more than Bohemian Rhapsody, the song that had come out one year earlier.

Here's the real writing, recording, and chart story, verified against this site's own Billboard data.

Somebody to Love Was Freddie Mercury's Alone

Freddie Mercury sat down and wrote this song without any of his bandmates in the room.

The songwriting credit on the record reads Mercury alone.

No shared line runs with May, Taylor, or Deacon.

He wanted a song built on gospel music, the sound he had grown up hearing on Aretha Franklin records.

That single influence shaped the whole arrangement, from the piano chords to the vocal phrasing.

Roger Taylor on the Gospel Sound Behind It

Drummer Roger Taylor later described exactly where the sound came from.

He called the track "Aretha Franklin-influenced," adding that "Freddie's very much into that."

Taylor said the band "tried to keep the track in a loose, gospel-type feel."

That matched Mercury's original idea, rather than a standard Queen rock arrangement.

It shows how often that same drive to reinvent shaped the rest of the band's decade.

Three Singers Built a 100-Voice Choir

Somebody to Love came together during the sessions for A Day at the Races, Queen's sixth studio album.

The band worked across four studios that year:

  • The Manor, Oxfordshire
  • Sarm East, London
  • Wessex Sound, London
  • Advision, London

Sessions ran in two blocks, from mid-July through late August, then September through mid-November 1976.

Somebody to Love itself was tracked in two separate parts.

Those parts were fused together and completed on October 22, 1976.

Mixing ran from October 18 through 24 at Sarm East Studios.

Queen's First Fully Self-Produced Album

Earlier Queen albums had leaned on outside production help, most often from Roy Thomas Baker.

A Day at the Races marked the first time the band produced an entire record entirely on its own.

Engineer Mike Stone ran the sessions, with Gary Langan assisting.

For the vocal sound, Mercury, Brian May, and Roger Taylor recorded pass after pass of layered harmony.

They stacked their three real voices into something that plays back like a full gospel choir.

Somebody to Love's Real Chart Story

Queen released this single on November 12, 1976.

It entered the UK Singles Chart on November 27.

It climbed all the way to No. 2 by December 11, spending nine weeks on the chart in total.

In the United States, the song reached No. 13 on the Billboard Hot 100 in early 1977.

Why This Site's Own Data Shows 88, Not 13

This site's own song database lists a billboard_peak field of 88 for this track.

On its own, that field looks like a fairly weak chart showing.

Music of the 70s tracks that exact kind of mismatch across its whole database.

It's actually the song's rank on Billboard's year-end chart for all of 1977, not its real weekly peak.

The true weekly high on the Hot 100 was No. 13.

That number comes from independent chart history, not the site's own year-end field alone.

The Song Freddie Mercury Ranked Above Bohemian Rhapsody

Peter Hince, Queen's longtime road crew chief, later spoke about how Mercury viewed this song.

Hince said Mercury saw it as a purer piece of songwriting than Bohemian Rhapsody.

Bohemian Rhapsody had come out just one year earlier and gone on to become the band's most famous single.

Somebody to Love was, by most accounts, the song Mercury personally liked writing the most.

A Tribute Performance That Introduced It to a New Generation

George Michael performed this song with the surviving members of Queen at the Freddie Mercury Tribute Concert.

That concert took place at Wembley Stadium on April 20, 1992.

His live version later appeared on a 1993 EP release.

That EP reached No. 1 on the UK Singles Chart.

The single also earned real certifications on its own strength, decades after its original 1976 release.

  • United States: 5x Platinum (RIAA, 5,000,000 units)
  • United Kingdom: 2x Platinum (BPI, 2011 release, 1,200,000 units)
  • Italy: 2x Platinum (FIMI, 200,000 units)
  • New Zealand: 3x Platinum (RMNZ, 90,000 units)

A song written by one person in private became one of Queen's most enduring group performances.

Nearly fifty years later, it still sounds like a full choir every single time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What inspired Freddie Mercury to write Somebody to Love?

Freddie Mercury wrote Somebody to Love on his own, drawing on the gospel music he loved.

Aretha Franklin's records were a direct influence on the sound.

Drummer Roger Taylor later said the band deliberately kept a loose, gospel-type feel to match Mercury's original idea.

No other Queen member shares the songwriting credit.

How did Queen make three singers sound like a 100-voice choir on Somebody to Love?

Freddie Mercury, Brian May, and Roger Taylor recorded pass after pass of layered harmony vocals.

Engineers stacked those overdubs on top of each other during the sessions.

The result was three real voices playing back like a full gospel choir.

The song was tracked in two separate parts that were later fused into one finished recording.

What chart position did Somebody to Love reach in the US and UK?

This single reached No. 13 on the Billboard Hot 100 in the United States.

It climbed to No. 2 on the UK Singles Chart in December 1976.

It also topped both major Dutch singles charts and reached No. 5 in Canada.

The song is certified 5 times Platinum in the US and 2 times Platinum in the UK.

Was Somebody to Love recorded during A Day at the Races?

Yes, this song was recorded in 1976 for A Day at the Races, Queen's sixth studio album.

The band worked across four London-area studios that year.

The finished single was mixed at Sarm East Studios in October 1976.

It was the first Queen album the band produced entirely on its own.

Did Freddie Mercury prefer Somebody to Love over Bohemian Rhapsody?

Freddie Mercury was widely reported to consider this song his favorite he ever wrote.

Queen's longtime road crew chief Peter Hince said Mercury viewed it as a purer piece of songwriting.

That comparison was against Bohemian Rhapsody, the hit that had come out one year earlier.

Both songs remain among Queen's most enduring 1970s singles.
