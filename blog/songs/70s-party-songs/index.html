The Short Answer: What Makes a 70s Party Song

A 70s party song is a Billboard Hot 100 hit from 1970 to 1979 that people danced to, sang along with, or both.

That covers more than the disco floor.

It takes in pop singalongs, funk grooves and a Beethoven remix.

The decade's chart rewarded all of them, and 27 of the 30 on this page reached number one.

Here is what the list shows at a glance:

  • The earliest song on the list reached number one in April 1970, and the latest charted on the 1979 year-end list.
  • 1974 has the most songs on the list, with 5, and every one of them reached number one.
  • Two songs tie for the longest run at number one, with 6 weeks each.
  • Three songs missed number one, and each has a story of its own further down.

70s Party Songs Were Not Only Disco

Disco gets the credit, and it earns most of it.

But "Mama Told Me (Not to Come)," "Joy to the World" and "Crocodile Rock" all reached number one before disco took over the chart.

So the label here follows the room, not the genre.

How These 70s Party Songs Were Chosen

The starting pool came from this site's own song data, not from a critic's mood board.

It holds 1,000 year-end Hot 100 songs for 1970 to 1979, and each one carries a vibe tag.

299 of them are tagged party or dance and have a YouTube video on record.

From that pool, 30 made the list on three tests:

  • The song works as a party or dance record, in its rhythm or its singalong chorus.
  • Its chart facts can be checked against the weekly or year-end Billboard data on this site.
  • Its story line can be backed by the weekly chart file or by this site's own Songs pages.

That is why the list leans toward big chart hits.

A song with a great dance floor reputation but no checkable chart record did not make the cut.

It is not the song's weekly peak.

"Joy to the World" Started the Decade's Party

Three Dog Night's "Joy to the World" reached number one in the spring of 1971.

It stayed there for six straight weeks.

It then finished as Billboard's top single of the year.

The full story is on the Joy to the World page.

"Le Freak" Took Six Weeks in Three Stretches

Chic's "Le Freak" first reached number one on December 9, 1978.

It lost the top spot, came back, lost it again and returned for a last run that ended on February 3, 1979.

Barbra Streisand and Neil Diamond's "You Don't Bring Me Flowers" and the Bee Gees' "Too Much Heaven" took the weeks in between.

Added up, that is six weeks at number one, tied for the most on this list.

Disco Carries the List

17 of the 30 songs carry the disco tag, from "Rock the Boat" in 1974 to "Y.M.C.A." in 1979.

For the genre's full arc, read 70s disco music, which follows how it peaked and nearly died.

Funk, Soul and Rock Still Get a Seat

"Superstition," "Play That Funky Music," "Keep on Truckin'" and "September" are tagged funk.

"Joy to the World," "Crocodile Rock" and "The Loco-Motion" are tagged classic rock.

None of those songs needed a mirror ball to get a room moving.

Back to Back: Party Songs That Passed the Baton

The weekly chart file shows which song held number one right before and right after each one on the list.

Several of these party songs handed off to each other:

  • "Superstition" gave way to "Crocodile Rock" on February 3, 1973.
  • "TSOP (The Sound of Philadelphia)" was followed by "The Loco-Motion" on May 4, 1974.
  • "Rock the Boat" held on July 6, 1974, and "Rock Your Baby" took the next week.
  • "Fly, Robin, Fly" came between KC and the Sunshine Band's two weeks of "That's the Way (I Like It)."
  • "Play That Funky Music" was followed by "A Fifth of Beethoven," then "Disco Duck."
  • "Love Train" held number one on March 24, 1973, between two stretches of "Killing Me Softly with His Song."

All of these hand-offs come straight from the weekly chart file.

An Odd Couple at the Top

"A Fifth of Beethoven" held number one for one week on October 9, 1976.

The next song to take over was Rick Dees and His Cast of Idiots' "Disco Duck."

The chart really did put a Beethoven remix and a novelty duck back to back at the top.

Party Songs That Stopped Short of Number One

Three songs on the list never topped the Hot 100.

Each one is here because the chart does not tell the whole story.

"Last Dance" Won the Oscar

Paul Jabara wrote it for the 1978 film Thank God It's Friday.

Donna Summer recorded it, and it reached number three on the Hot 100 in August 1978.

It also won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

The Last Dance page has the rest.

"Y.M.C.A." Stalled at Number Two

Jacques Morali and Victor Willis wrote it together in a New York studio in 1978.

Casablanca Records released it on October 9, 1978.

It stalled at number two on the weekly Hot 100 in early 1979.

The Y.M.C.A. page covers the dance and the Library of Congress pick.

"September" Never Passed Number Eight

Maurice White and Allee Willis wrote it in 1978, and it came out on November 18 of that year.

It never got higher than number eight on the Hot 100.

It sits at rank 78 on the 1979 year-end list.

The September page tells how it was written.

1974 Is the Peak Year

Five songs from 1974 made the list, and every one of them reached number one.

That year gave us "Hooked on a Feeling," "TSOP," "The Loco-Motion," "Rock the Boat" and "Rock Your Baby."

All five hit the top between April 6 and July 20, 1974.

The Decade Closes on the Dance Floor

"Le Freak" and "I Will Survive" carried the party into 1979.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best 70s party songs?

The best 70s party songs on this site's list include Chic's "Le Freak," Gloria Gaynor's "I Will Survive," the Bee Gees' "Stayin' Alive" and Three Dog Night's "Joy to the World." All 30 are checked against Billboard chart data and can be played from the table at the top of the page.

What is the number one party song of the 70s?

Two songs on this list tie for the longest run at number one: Three Dog Night's "Joy to the World" in 1971 and Chic's "Le Freak" in 1978 and 1979. Each spent six weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100. "Joy to the World" was also Billboard's top single of 1971.

What are the best 70s dance songs?

The best 70s dance songs here run from "Love Train" and "Rock Your Baby" in the early part of the decade to "Le Freak," "I Will Survive" and "September" at the end. 17 of the 30 songs are tagged disco in this site's data, and the rest are funk, soul, pop and classic rock.

Did every 70s party song on this list reach number one?

No, 27 of the 30 party songs on this list reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100. Donna Summer's "Last Dance" peaked at number three, Village People's "Y.M.C.A." stalled at number two, and Earth, Wind & Fire's "September" never got higher than number eight.
