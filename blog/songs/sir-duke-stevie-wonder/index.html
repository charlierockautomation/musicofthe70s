Sir Duke Stevie Wonder: The Tribute That Topped the Charts

By Charlie · Published September 15, 2026

Sir Duke Stevie Wonder wrote as a thank-you note to a man who never got to hear it.

Duke Ellington died on May 24, 1974, one of the giants who shaped the music Wonder grew up on.

Wonder didn't want him forgotten, so he built an entire song around saying so.

He named Count Basie, Glenn Miller, Louis Armstrong and Ella Fitzgerald in the same three minutes.

The song came from Songs in the Key of Life, the double album that would sweep that year's Grammys.

Motown released it as a single in March 1977, and it reached number one within weeks.

This is the chart-verified story, built from this site's own Billboard year-end and weekly data and cross-checked against outside chart and interview sources.

Why Stevie Wonder Wrote Sir Duke for Duke Ellington

The Bandleader Wonder Didn't Want Forgotten

Duke Ellington was the jazz composer and bandleader who spent five decades shaping American popular music.

He died on May 24, 1974, in New York, from lung cancer and pneumonia.

More than 12,000 people attended his funeral.

Wonder later explained what pushed him to write about it.

"I knew the title from the beginning but wanted it to be about the musicians who did something for us," Wonder said.

"So soon they are forgotten. I wanted to show my appreciation. They gave us something that is supposed to be forever."

That idea, gratitude before forgetting sets in, became the whole spine of Sir Duke Stevie Wonder built.

The Four Names Sir Duke Refuses to Let You Forget

Wonder didn't stop at Ellington's name.

The lyrics list several more musicians he felt owed the same thank you:

  • Count Basie, the bandleader known for his stripped-down, swinging piano style.
  • Glenn Miller, the big band leader behind some of the era's most recognizable dance orchestras.
  • Louis Armstrong, referred to by his nickname, Satchmo.
  • Ella Fitzgerald, the vocalist Wonder placed alongside the other three as essential to the sound.

Five names, one three-minute song, all tied to the era just before rock and soul took over the charts.

Inside the Sir Duke Studio Session Stevie Wonder Ran Alone

Stevie Wonder wrote and produced the track himself, the same way he'd built most of his classic period catalog.

He played electric piano, percussion, and handled the lead vocal.

A tight studio band filled in everything else.

The Guitarist Who Later Had a Hit of His Own

Michael Sembello played lead guitar on the session.

Six years later, he'd have his own hit as a performer.

Sembello wrote and recorded "Maniac," the 1983 chart-topper from the Flashdance soundtrack.

The Mood Song Matcher tags Sir Duke happy and built for dancing, a mood Sembello clearly carried into his own later career.

Few sidemen on a Wonder session went on to headline a soundtrack hit of their own.

Where Sir Duke Stevie Wonder Landed on the Charts

Motown released Sir Duke on March 22, 1977, as the album's second US single, following "I Wish."

It entered the Billboard Hot 100 on March 27, 1977, debuting at number 74.

From there it climbed fast.

Prefer to hear it before reading the rest of its chart run? Stream Sir Duke on Listen Now.

Sir Duke Stevie Wonder reached number one on the Hot 100 for the week of May 21, 1977.

This site's own weekly Billboard data credits it with three weeks at the top, running through June 10, 1977.

It also hit number one on the R&B chart the same spring.

The single spent 17 weeks on the Hot 100 in total.

A Grammy Sweep Built Around One Tribute Song

Few tribute songs anywhere in the Music of the 70s catalog landed inside an album this decorated.

Songs in the Key of Life won Album of the Year at the 19th Grammy Awards.

Wonder also won Producer of the Year and Best Male Pop Vocal Performance that same night.

A separate Best Male R&B Vocal Performance award went to the album's other big single, "I Wish."

Wonder skipped the ceremony entirely.

He was visiting Africa at the time and accepted all three of his personal awards by satellite from Nigeria.

Part of a Run, Not a One-Off

Sir Duke arrived in the middle of Wonder's most decorated stretch as a recording artist.

"Superstition" had already opened that run in 1973.

"You Are the Sunshine of My Life" followed the same year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who did Stevie Wonder write Sir Duke about?

Sir Duke Stevie Wonder wrote as a tribute to Duke Ellington, the jazz bandleader and composer who died on May 24, 1974.

Wonder said he wanted the song to be about musicians who did something lasting for music but risked being forgotten.

He built the entire track around Ellington's name while the loss was still recent.

What other musicians does Sir Duke name check?

Alongside Duke Ellington, the lyrics to Sir Duke name Count Basie, Glenn Miller, Louis Armstrong and Ella Fitzgerald.

Wonder grouped all five as artists who gave listeners something meant to last forever.

Louis Armstrong appears under his nickname, Satchmo, matching how fans and fellow musicians referred to him.

How high did Sir Duke Stevie Wonder chart?

Sir Duke reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 for three weeks in 1977, matching its number one run on the R&B chart.

It peaked at number 2 on the UK Singles Chart and closed out the year at number 18 on this site's own 1977 year-end Hot 100.

What album is Sir Duke from?

Sir Duke comes from Songs in the Key of Life, the double album Stevie Wonder released on September 28, 1976.

Motown issued Sir Duke as the album's second US single on March 22, 1977, following I Wish.

It became the biggest hit from an album already stacked with them.

Did Sir Duke win a Grammy?

Sir Duke itself was not entered as a standalone Grammy category winner, but its parent album, Songs in the Key of Life, won Album of the Year at the 19th Grammy Awards.

Wonder also won Producer of the Year and Best Male Pop Vocal Performance that night, accepting all three awards by satellite from Nigeria.
