Midnight Train to Georgia by Gladys Knight: The Story

By Charlie · Published October 19, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

Midnight Train to Georgia began life as a song about a plane to Houston.

Jim Weatherly wrote it, and a producer in Atlanta asked for one small change to the title.

Gladys Knight and the Pips turned it into a two-week number one in the fall of 1973.

Below are the origin story, the one-take vocal, the chart run, the Grammy and the honors that keep arriving.

Midnight Train to Georgia: From Houston Plane to Georgia Train

Midnight Plane to Houston

Jim Weatherly wrote the song, and Wikipedia says he first recorded it himself as "Midnight Plane to Houston."

That version came out on Jimmy Bowen's Amos Records.

Weatherly told the story of a phone call with Farrah Fawcett, who mentioned taking a midnight plane to Houston.

He later said he used her and Lee Majors "as kind of like characters."

Sonny Limbo Changes the Destination

Sonny Limbo, working in Atlanta, wanted Cissy Houston to record the song.

He suggested changing the title to "Midnight Train to Georgia," according to Wikipedia.

Houston cut it in 1973 as "Midnite Train to Georgia."

Gladys Knight & the Pips recorded their own version soon afterward, and theirs is the one the radio kept.

Recording the Hit: One Take in Detroit

The Backing Track in New Jersey

Tony Camillo produced and arranged the record, with Knight and the Pips co-producing, according to Wikipedia.

Ed Stasium recorded and mixed it.

The rhythm section laid down the first track at Venture Sound in New Jersey.

Overdubs then added piano, organ, strings and horns.

Wikipedia lists these players:

  • Bob Babbitt on bass.
  • Andrew Smith on drums.
  • Jeff Mironov on guitar.
  • Randy Brecker on trumpet and Michael Brecker on saxophone.
  • Meco Monardo on trombone.

Knight's One-Take Vocal

Knight recorded her lead vocal in a single take at Artie Fields Studio in Detroit, according to Wikipedia.

She later recorded a punch-in of one line in New York City.

Merald "Bubba" Knight, Eddie Patten and William Guest sang the background parts.

Those answering Pips are half the charm, and the record would sound bare without them.

On the Charts: Two Weeks at Number One

The Climb to the Top

The single debuted on the Billboard Hot 100 at number 71, according to Wikipedia.

It reached number one in the issue dated October 27, 1973.

This site's weekly Hot 100 data lists it at number one for the chart weeks of October 27 and November 3, 1973, two weeks in all.

It was the first Hot 100 number one for Gladys Knight & the Pips, and it sits on our list of 70s number one hits.

Legacy: A Grammy, a Hall of Fame and the Movies

Awards and Honors

Wikipedia reports these honors for the recording:

  • The 1974 Grammy Award for Best R&B Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus.
  • A place in the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1999.
  • Selection for the Library of Congress National Recording Registry in 2026.

The recording's run of honors is part of why Gladys Knight turns up all over Music of the 70s.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote Midnight Train to Georgia?

Jim Weatherly wrote Midnight Train to Georgia, according to Wikipedia.

He first recorded it as Midnight Plane to Houston on Amos Records.

Producer Sonny Limbo wanted Cissy Houston to cut it and suggested the new title.

Gladys Knight & the Pips recorded their own version in 1973, and it became the hit.

When did Midnight Train to Georgia reach number one?

Midnight Train to Georgia reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in the issue dated October 27, 1973, according to Wikipedia.

This site's weekly Hot 100 data gives it two weeks at the top.

The single had entered the Hot 100 at number 71, and it was the first Hot 100 number one for Gladys Knight & the Pips.

Did Midnight Train to Georgia win a Grammy?

Yes, Gladys Knight & the Pips won the 1974 Grammy Award for Best R&B Vocal Performance by a Duo, Group or Chorus for Midnight Train to Georgia, according to Wikipedia.

The recording entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1999.

Wikipedia also says the Library of Congress picked it for the National Recording Registry in 2026.

How was the Midnight Train to Georgia vocal recorded?

Gladys Knight recorded her lead vocal for Midnight Train to Georgia in a single take at Artie Fields Studio in Detroit, according to Wikipedia.

She later recorded a punch-in of one line in New York City.

The backing track had been cut earlier at Venture Sound in New Jersey.

What is the original version of Midnight Train to Georgia?

The earliest version of Midnight Train to Georgia is Jim Weatherly's own recording, titled Midnight Plane to Houston, according to Wikipedia.

Cissy Houston then recorded it in 1973 as Midnite Train to Georgia.

The Gladys Knight & the Pips version, released in August 1973, is the one that topped the Hot 100.
