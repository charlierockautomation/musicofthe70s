Take Me Home Country Roads: John Denver's 1971 Hit

By Charlie · Published October 19, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

Take Me Home Country Roads is the song most people can sing before they know who wrote it.

Bill Danoff, Taffy Nivert and John Denver finished it on the morning of December 30, 1970.

By August 1971 it sat at number two on the Billboard Hot 100.

Below are the writing story, the chart run, the West Virginia connection and the covers that kept it moving.

Take Me Home Country Roads: How Three Writers Finished It

A Drive on Clopper Road

According to Wikipedia, the title idea came to Taffy Nivert while she drove along Clopper Road in Montgomery County, Maryland.

She was heading toward a family gathering in Gaithersburg.

Her husband Bill Danoff turned the idea into a song with her.

Denver heard the unfinished tune and joined in.

The three of them finished it on the morning of December 30, 1970.

That night Denver premiered it at The Cellar Door in Washington, D.C.

Recording and Release

The song was recorded in New York City in January 1971.

RCA released it as a single on April 12, 1971, from the album Poems, Prayers & Promises.

Original pressings credited "John Denver with Fat City."

You can read how the song fits his whole run in our list of John Denver songs.

On the Charts: A Number Two Hit That Still Won Number One

Stuck Behind the Bee Gees

Wikipedia says the single peaked at number two on the Billboard Hot 100 for the week ending August 28, 1971.

The Bee Gees' "How Can You Mend a Broken Heart" was ahead of it.

This site's weekly Hot 100 data lists that Bee Gees song at number one for four straight chart weeks in August 1971.

We tell that story in our post on How Can You Mend a Broken Heart.

So the Denver single never held the top spot on the Hot 100 itself.

Number One Elsewhere

Wikipedia also lists it at number one on the Record World pop singles chart and on the Cash Box Top 100.

Those trade charts counted sales and airplay in their own way, which is why they could disagree with Billboard.

West Virginia, Honors and Certifications

One of Four State Songs

On March 7, 2014, the West Virginia legislature approved a resolution naming the song one of four official state songs, according to Wikipedia.

Governor Earl Ray Tomblin signed it the next day.

That is a long road from a drive in Maryland to a statehouse vote.

Hall of Fame and Registry

Wikipedia lists two honors for the recording:

  • Grammy Hall of Fame: 1998.
  • Library of Congress National Recording Registry: 2023.

Gold, Platinum and Beyond

The RIAA certified the single gold on August 18, 1971, and platinum on April 10, 2017.

Covers and Revivals

Olivia Newton-John, Toots and the Hermes House Band

Wikipedia lists three notable covers, each with its own approach:

  • Olivia Newton-John, January 1973: a version that reached number 15 on the UK Singles Chart and number 5 in Ireland.
  • Toots and the Maytals, 1974: a reggae reading included on the album In the Dark.
  • Hermes House Band, 2001: a dance-pop version that reached number 7 in the UK and number 2 in Germany.

Newer Lives for an Old Song

A version used in the video game Fallout 76 reached number one on the iTunes singles chart after its July 4, 2018 release, according to Wikipedia.

In 2024, Machine Gun Kelly and Jelly Roll's "Lonely Road," which interpolates the song, reached number 33 on the Hot 100.
