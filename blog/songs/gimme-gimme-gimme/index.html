Gimme Gimme Gimme: The ABBA Song America Never Got

Gimme Gimme Gimme became one of ABBA's biggest club hits of 1979 without ever getting an official US single release.

Atlantic Records pressed promotional copies, then canceled the release entirely.

The timing was brutal.

The single arrived just three months after a Chicago stadium riot aimed squarely at disco records.

Here's the real story behind the ABBA song America was never officially allowed to buy.

The Gimme Gimme Gimme Single America Never Actually Got

A Release Atlantic Records Pulled Before It Started

Gimme Gimme Gimme never had an official US single release at all.

ABBA's American label, Atlantic Records, actually pressed promotional copies first.

Then the label canceled the release outright, before it ever reached store shelves.

Reasoning traced back to one thing: disco's reputation had collapsed fast in the US that year.

Every other major ABBA single from the decade got a real American release, even the ones that barely charted.

This one didn't get the chance at all.

Three Months After Disco Demolition Night

The timing could not have been worse.

ABBA released the single on October 12, 1979.

That's almost exactly three months after Disco Demolition Night, the Chicago stadium riot that this site's own Disco Music of the 70s post covers in full.

A Chicago DJ blew up a crate of disco records between games of a White Sox doubleheader on July 12, 1979.

US radio and record labels spent the following months actively backing away from anything that sounded like disco.

Atlantic's decision landed squarely inside that window.

The song still became a real US club and DJ favorite over time, just never through a store-bought single.

Why Gimme Gimme Gimme Sounds Like Nothing Else ABBA Wrote

Built to Answer Donna Summer's Hot Stuff

Benny Andersson and Björn Ulvaeus wrote this one after hearing a specific record.

That record was Donna Summer's "Hot Stuff," a rock-tinged disco hit from earlier in 1979.

It pushed them toward a darker, more pulsing sound than ABBA's usual pop instincts.

An ARP Odyssey synthesizer carries the song's driving melody line.

ABBA recorded the track in August 1979 at Polar Music Studios in Stockholm.

It wasn't written for any specific album, just as a standalone single to promote an upcoming tour.

A Lonely Narrator, Not a Love Song

Agnetha Fältskog sings the lead vocal, and the lyric she's singing isn't really a love song.

The narrator is a woman alone late at night, aching for company that hasn't arrived.

The song's full official title makes that explicit: "Gimme! Gimme! Gimme! (A Man After Midnight)."

That's a sharper, more isolated feeling than the wistful longing in this site's own Dancing Queen post.

It's closer kin to the group's disco pivot than to their earlier ballads.

The Real Story Behind the Gimme Gimme Gimme Music Video

The video is far simpler than the song's dramatic sound might suggest.

Director Lasse Hallström filmed it on September 5, 1979, at Studio A inside Polar Music Studios.

Hallström directed several of ABBA's videos across the decade, including the one behind this site's own Benny Andersson post.

There's no set, no dancers, and no concept beyond the band performing the track live in the studio.

The camera simply pushes in and pulls back across all four members as they play.

That stripped-down approach let the song's studio production carry the whole four minutes on its own.

How Gimme Gimme Gimme Actually Performed Around the World

Outside North America, this was a genuine smash.

Gimme Gimme Gimme reached number one on the charts in five separate markets.

  • Belgium
  • Denmark
  • Finland
  • Ireland
  • Japan's international chart

It peaked at number three in the UK, kept off the top spot that autumn.

That's a step below the UK number one this site's own Mamma Mia Song post covers, and below Waterloo before it.

France and the Netherlands both carried it to number two.

Even without a proper American release, it still ranks among ABBA's most recognized songs today, largely on club play and, later, streaming.

Madonna, Mamma Mia, and Every DJ Set Since

The Sample Madonna Had to Ask Permission For

Andersson and Ulvaeus almost never let anyone sample ABBA's music.

Madonna wanted to for her 2005 single "Hung Up," built directly around this song's instrumental hook.

She wrote the pair a personal letter asking for permission before recording it.

They said yes.

It became only the second officially approved ABBA sample ever, after the Fugees used "The Name of the Game" back in 1996.

"Hung Up" went on to become one of the biggest hits of Madonna's entire career.

A Different Song in the Movie Than the One ABBA Wrote

The 2008 Mamma Mia! film didn't just borrow this song's title, unlike its own soundtrack namesake.

Amanda Seyfried recorded a full studio version for that film's soundtrack album.

Only a shorter, partial version actually plays on screen during the movie itself.

Nearly three decades separate ABBA's original 1979 recording from that film.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Gimme Gimme Gimme song actually mean?

Gimme Gimme Gimme is about loneliness, not romance in the usual ABBA sense.

The full title is "Gimme! Gimme! Gimme! (A Man After Midnight)," and the lyric follows a woman alone late at night, aching for company that never shows up.

Agnetha Fältskog sang the lead vocal, written after Benny Andersson and Björn Ulvaeus heard Donna Summer's "Hot Stuff."

Why didn't Gimme Gimme Gimme come out as a single in the US?

Atlantic Records canceled the American and Canadian single release entirely, even after pressing promotional copies.

The label's reasoning traced back to disco's rapidly collapsing reputation in the US that year.

The single arrived on October 12, 1979, almost exactly three months after Disco Demolition Night in Chicago, right as US radio was actively turning against the genre.

Did Gimme Gimme Gimme reach number one anywhere?

It topped the charts in Belgium, Denmark, Finland, Ireland, and Japan's international chart during 1979.

In the UK it peaked at number three, held out of the top spot by other singles that autumn.

France and the Netherlands both took it to number two.

Its strongest showings came almost entirely outside North America.

Who sampled the Gimme Gimme Gimme song?

Madonna sampled it for her 2005 single "Hung Up," one of the biggest hits of her career.

Andersson and Ulvaeus rarely allow ABBA's songs to be sampled, so Madonna wrote them a personal letter asking for permission before recording it.

It became only the second officially approved ABBA sample ever, after the Fugees used "The Name of the Game" in 1996.

Was Gimme Gimme Gimme written for the movie Mamma Mia?

No, the song predates the film by nearly three decades.

ABBA recorded it in August 1979, released it that October, and the first Mamma Mia! movie didn't arrive until 2008.

Amanda Seyfried recorded a full studio version of the song for that film's soundtrack, since only a partial version plays during the actual movie scene.
