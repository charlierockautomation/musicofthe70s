Night Fever: The Bee Gees Song That Renamed a Movie

By Charlie · Published September 21, 2026

Night Fever didn't just top the charts in 1978, it renamed the movie built around it.

The Bee Gees had already recorded the song before Robert Stigwood's film even had a permanent title.

Director John Badham heard it and folded its own title straight into the movie's name.

That's how "Saturday Night" became Saturday Night Fever.

The song then spent eight weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100.

That was the longest run of any single that year.

This is the chart-verified story behind it.

It's built from this site's own Billboard data and cross-checked against outside chart and interview sources.

How Night Fever Talked a Movie Into Changing Its Name

A Magazine Article, Renamed Twice Before the Cameras Rolled

Robin Gibb traced the whole project back to a 1976 New York magazine article called "Tribal Rites of the New Saturday Night."

Producer Robert Stigwood turned that piece into a movie built around Brooklyn disco nightlife.

Its working title shortened down to just "Saturday Night," a nod to the 2001 Odyssey, the real Brooklyn club where the story's characters spent their nights.

The Bee Gees wrote the song separately, alongside the rest of the soundtrack.

They had no idea it would end up doing double duty as the film's own name.

The group's career is full of turns like this one, sudden and unplanned.

Read the full arc of that story on this site's Bee Gees page.

John Badham Heard the Song and Folded Its Title Into the Film's

Director John Badham took over the project and listened through the Bee Gees' finished soundtrack reels.

One track stood out to him as the clearest statement of what the whole movie was actually about.

That track was Night Fever.

Badham pulled the song's title straight into the movie's own name.

RSO Records released it as the third single from that same soundtrack.

That release came in January 1978.

A song written to fill space on an album ended up naming the film it came from.

A String Line Borrowed From the Radio, Recorded in Two Countries

The Percy Faith Riff Barry Gibb Turned Into a Disco Hook

The song's opening string figure has a specific, unlikely source.

Blue Weaver, the group's keyboardist, was running through Percy Faith's 1959 orchestral hit "Theme From A Summer Place."

He played it in the studio one morning.

Barry Gibb heard it from the next room and recognized the shape of something new inside it.

He built the song's string hook directly out of that decades-old easy-listening standard.

Weaver's ARP String Ensemble carried that line through the finished track, alongside Fender Rhodes electric piano and harpsichord.

A song built for the dance floor grew out of an orchestral theme.

Nobody in that room had planned to use it that way.

Half Recorded in France, Finished in Miami

The Bee Gees tracked the song's earliest sessions at Chateau d'Herouville, a studio outside Paris, around April 1977.

They finished it that September at Criteria Studios in Miami.

It was the same room that produced most of the rest of the soundtrack.

The Musicians Who Played on the Session

Barry, Robin and Maurice Gibb split lead, harmony and backing vocals between them, with Barry also playing rhythm guitar.

Alan Kendall took the lead guitar part, and Dennis Bryon played drums.

Session guitarist Lee Ritenour added a second rhythm guitar track.

Albhy Galuten and Karl Richardson produced the song alongside the brothers themselves.

Eight Weeks at Number One, and the Song Right Behind It

A Slow Climb, Then a Lock on the Top of the Hot 100

Night Fever didn't enter the Hot 100 anywhere near the top.

It debuted at number 76 in early 1978.

One week later it jumped 44 spots to number 32.

From there it climbed 32, 17, 8, 5, 2, then finally number 1.

This site's own weekly Billboard archive puts that run at eight straight weeks atop the chart.

That stretch ran from the week of March 18 through the week ending May 12, 1978.

It's the longest unbroken stay at number one by any single that year.

Not Even the Only Bee Gees Hit of the Same Stretch

That eight-week run came with an odd asterisk.

For the first five of those weeks, Stayin' Alive held the number two spot right behind it.

It was another Bee Gees single from the same soundtrack.

This site's own year-end archive ranks the song at number 2 for all of 1978.

That's one spot behind Andy Gibb's "Shadow Dancing."

Across the year, the Bee Gees logged a combined 13 weeks at number one.

That's more than any other act managed in 1978.

A Grammy, a Platinum Single, and a Song Still in Rotation

Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo or Group

Night Fever won the Grammy for Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo or Group in 1979.

That award came at the same ceremony where the wider soundtrack won Album of the Year.

The RIAA later certified the single Platinum, for sales topping 2.5 million copies.

The Live B-Side Nobody Remembers

The single's B-side wasn't a new song at all.

It was "Down the Road," a live recording pulled from the group's own 1977 album Here at Last... Bee Gees... Live.

A song built from a half-heard orchestral riff, cut across two studios on two continents, ended up naming the movie it was written for.

That movie became one of the best-selling soundtracks ever made.

The group's falsetto era reached even further the following year with You Should Be Dancing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What movie did Night Fever by the Bee Gees name?

Night Fever gave its own title to Saturday Night Fever, the 1977 film it was written for.

Director John Badham heard the finished song.

He felt it captured the movie better than the working title "Saturday Night" did.

So he folded the song's title into the film's own name before release.

How many weeks was Night Fever number one?

Night Fever spent eight weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in the spring of 1978.

This site's own weekly chart archive confirms that run started the week of March 18.

It held the top spot through the week ending May 12.

That was the longest unbroken run at the top by any single that year.

Who wrote and produced Night Fever?

Barry, Robin and Maurice Gibb wrote Night Fever together.

They were the same trio behind most of the soundtrack.

They produced the track themselves alongside Albhy Galuten and Karl Richardson.

The Bee Gees recorded it across two studios.

They started at Chateau d'Herouville in France and finished at Criteria Studios in Miami.

Did Night Fever win a Grammy?

Night Fever won the Grammy for Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo or Group in 1979.

That award came at the same ceremony where the soundtrack itself won Album of the Year.

The single also earned a Platinum certification from the RIAA for topping 2.5 million copies sold.

What inspired the string intro on Night Fever?

The string line that opens Night Fever traces back to Percy Faith's 1959 orchestral hit Theme From A Summer Place.

Bee Gees keyboardist Blue Weaver was playing through that older song in the studio one morning.

Barry Gibb built the song's own hook out of what he heard.
