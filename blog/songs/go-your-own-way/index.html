Go Your Own Way: Buckingham's Breakup Song Explained

By Charlie · Published October 23, 2026

▶ Jump to the video

Go Your Own Way is Fleetwood Mac's breakup song, and Lindsey Buckingham wrote it about a bandmate.

It was the lead single from Rumours, released in December 1976.

It peaked at number 10 on the Billboard Hot 100 and reached number one in the Netherlands and Belgium.

This page covers how it was written, how it was recorded, how it charted and who has covered it since.

Go Your Own Way: Written About a Bandmate

Most breakup songs are written alone and mailed away, but this one had to be rehearsed with its subject.

A Haunted Rental in Florida

Wikipedia says Buckingham wrote the song at a rented house in Florida, between legs of the Fleetwood Mac tour.

It was the first song he brought to the Rumours sessions.

Drummer Mick Fleetwood remembered the house as having a bad, haunted feel.

Buckingham described the writing as a stream of consciousness built on raw emotion.

Engineer Ken Caillat has said he was unsure the song had potential when he first heard the demo.

The Line Stevie Nicks Wanted Gone

Early versions had a different chorus, and they did not yet include the line about "packing up, shacking up."

Wikipedia says Stevie Nicks asked Buckingham to remove that line, because it implied she wanted to be with other men.

He kept it, and Nicks has said he wrote it to provoke her.

In a 2022 New Yorker interview, Nicks called this song and her own "Dreams" counter songs, two views of the same breakup.

Her side of the story has its own page, Dreams Fleetwood Mac.

Sheryl Crow has said she felt the song was written for her when she was seventeen.

Recording the Rumours Single

The song took about four months to develop, across three studios in 1976.

Three Studios, Three Producers

Wikipedia credits the production to Fleetwood Mac, Richard Dashut and Ken Caillat.

Sessions took place at these studios.

  • The Record Plant in Sausalito, California.
  • Wally Heider's Studio 3 in Hollywood.
  • Criteria Studios in Miami.

How the Sound Was Built

Buckingham based the drum feel on the groove of the Rolling Stones' "Street Fighting Man."

Mick Fleetwood played across the toms and hit the bass drum on the middle beat, which gives the song its tumbling gallop.

John McVie's first bass part was busier, so Buckingham asked for straight eighth notes on the verses and more freedom on the choruses.

Buckingham tracked early guitar on a 1959 Fender Stratocaster.

Later he layered a distorted electric guitar over a cleaner one, so the rhythm guitars read as a single instrument.

Christine McVie played Hammond organ and sang backing vocals.

The acoustic guitar went on late in the process.

The Spliced Guitar Solo

Buckingham struggled with the solo, so Caillat finished it at Criteria in Miami.

He spliced together six lead-guitar takes by working the faders, according to Wikipedia.

A Shure SM57 microphone captured the solo.

Dashut said the kick drum and the compressed rhythm guitar produced a pumping effect that might not have happened in a digital mix.

The Single at a Glance

Warner Bros. rushed the record out early to catch the US Christmas market in 1976.

Album pre-orders reached 800,000, which Wikipedia describes as reportedly the label's largest advance sale at the time.

On the Charts: A Top 10 Hit Across the Atlantic

Go Your Own Way did not reach number one in the United States.

It did reach number one in the Netherlands and Belgium.

The Billboard Hot 100 Run

The single debuted at number 71 on the Hot 100 dated January 8, 1977.

It peaked at number 10 on March 12, 1977, and held there for two weeks, according to Wikipedia.

It spent 11 weeks in the top 40.

Rumours would soon supply the band's first number one with "Dreams" in June 1977, according to this site's weekly Hot 100 data.

Critics and the Live Show

What Critics Said

The Washington Post's Alex Ward called it the best single choice on the album when it came out.

Billboard said it fit mainstream rock better than anything on the band's 1975 album, and compared it to Jefferson Starship.

Bud Scoppa called it a "pathos-laden scorcher."

Later lists have been just as kind.

  • Rolling Stone ranked it No. 120 on its 500 Greatest Songs list in 2010, and No. 401 in 2021.
  • NME put it at No. 33 on its 50 Most Explosive Choruses list in 2012.
  • It is on the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's 500 Songs that Shaped Rock and Roll list.
  • It was nominated for the 1978 Grammy for Best Vocal Arrangement for Two or More Voices, and lost to the Eagles' "New Kid in Town."

Closing Out Fleetwood Mac Shows

The band first played it live on the 1977 Rumours Tour, more than 100 times.

It was played at every stop on the Tusk Tour, and a Cleveland recording appeared on the 1980 album Live.

Wikipedia says it was the main set closer on The Dance tour in 1997, and again the last song of the main set in 2003 and on the 2013 tour.

The only time the band played it without both Buckingham and Nicks was the 1994 to 1995 tour.

Covers and Later Uses

The song has had a second life through other singers and screens.

  • The Cranberries recorded a version for the 2002 Complete Sessions release of To the Faithful Departed.
  • Wilson Phillips put a new version on their 2004 reunion album California, which reached No. 13 on the Billboard Adult Contemporary chart.
  • Lea Michele sang it on Glee in 2011, and that version peaked at No. 45 in the US and No. 51 in the UK.
  • Lissie's 2012 version appeared on the Safe Haven soundtrack and in A Discovery of Witches.
  • Ben Platt and Aly & AJ performed it on Platt's Reverie Tour and on Dick Clark's New Year's Rockin' Eve in 2023.
  • A 2025 PayPal campaign with Will Ferrell used an altered version.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who wrote Go Your Own Way?

Lindsey Buckingham wrote Go Your Own Way for Fleetwood Mac, and Wikipedia says it addresses his breakup with bandmate Stevie Nicks.

He wrote it at a rented house in Florida between legs of the band's tour.

It was the first song he brought to the Rumours sessions.

What number did Go Your Own Way reach on the Billboard Hot 100?

Go Your Own Way peaked at number 10 on the Billboard Hot 100 on March 12, 1977, and stayed there two weeks, according to Wikipedia.

It debuted at number 71 on the chart dated January 8, 1977.

It was not a number one, and it ranked 94th on the 1977 year-end Hot 100.

What is the line Stevie Nicks disliked in Go Your Own Way?

Stevie Nicks objected to the line about packing up and shacking up, which implied she was seeing other men.

Wikipedia says she asked Buckingham to remove it, but he kept it.

Nicks said he wrote the line to provoke her, and in 2022 she called this song and Dreams counter songs.

Was Go Your Own Way the first single from Rumours?

Go Your Own Way was the lead single from Fleetwood Mac's album Rumours.

Warner Bros. released it in the US on December 20, 1976, and in the UK on February 11, 1977.

The B-side was Silver Springs, a Stevie Nicks song that was left off the original album.

Who has covered Go Your Own Way?

Go Your Own Way has been covered by the Cranberries, Wilson Phillips, the cast of Glee and Lissie, among others.

Wilson Phillips reached number 13 on the Billboard Adult Contemporary chart in 2004, and Lea Michele's Glee version peaked at number 45 in the US.

Ben Platt and Aly & AJ have also performed it.
