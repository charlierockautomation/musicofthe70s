Those ten songs spent 22 weeks at number one between them.

The Short Answer: Who Was on Top Each Year

For May 29 in music history, the number one song was a different record in every year from 1970 to 1979.

That is the Hot 100 chart week that includes May 29.

Here is what the ten weeks show at a glance:

  • The ten songs spent 22 weeks at number one in total.
  • "Reunited" had the longest run, with four weeks, and "American Woman," "The Streak" and "Sir Duke" tie for second with three each.
  • 5 of the ten songs were in their first week at number one that week, and three songs had only that one week.
  • 2 were somewhere in the middle of a longer run, and 3 were in the final week of their runs.

How the May 29 Week Was Picked

Billboard chart weeks do not line up with the calendar, so there is no single week called May 29.

This May 29 in music history page takes the weekly Hot 100 week that contains May 29 in each year.

That week began on a different date each year:

  • 1970: the chart week of May 23, 1970, with "American Woman / No Sugar Tonight" by The Guess Who on top.
  • 1971: the chart week of May 29, 1971, with "Brown Sugar" by The Rolling Stones on top.
  • 1972: the chart week of May 27, 1972, with "Oh Girl" by The Chi-Lites on top.
  • 1973: the chart week of May 26, 1973, with "Frankenstein" by The Edgar Winter Group on top.
  • 1974: the chart week of May 25, 1974, with "The Streak" by Ray Stevens on top.
  • 1975: the chart week of May 24, 1975, with "Shining Star" by Earth, Wind & Fire on top.
  • 1976: the chart week of May 29, 1976, with "Love Hangover" by Diana Ross on top.
  • 1977: the chart week of May 28, 1977, with "Sir Duke" by Stevie Wonder on top.
  • 1978: the chart week of May 27, 1978, with "With a Little Luck" by Wings on top.
  • 1979: the chart week of May 26, 1979, with "Reunited" by Peaches & Herb on top.

Chart weeks and weeks at number one come from the weekly Hot 100 file on this site.

May 29 in Music History, 1970 to 1972: An Improvised Riff, a Muscle Shoals Session and a Harmonica Ballad

The first three Mays of the decade brought a Canadian rock band's stage jam, a Rolling Stones single recorded in Alabama and a soul ballad built around a harmonica.

1970: "American Woman" by the Guess Who

Wikipedia says the music and lyrics of "American Woman" were improvised on stage during a concert in Southern Ontario.

Guitarist Randy Bachman recalled it being a concert in Kitchener, while singer Burton Cummings said it was at the Broom and Stone, a curling rink in Scarborough.

The band noticed a kid with a cassette recorder making a bootleg and asked him for the tape, then wrote down the words Cummings had made up.

Jack Richardson produced the single, which was recorded on August 13, 1969, at RCA's Mid-America Recording Center in Chicago.

It came out as a single in March 1970, backed with "No Sugar Tonight," and the RIAA certified it gold on May 22, 1970.

This site's weekly data shows three weeks at number one, from May 9 to May 23, 1970, where the chart lists it as "American Woman / No Sugar Tonight."

So the chart week that contains May 29 was the third and last.

The song is number 3 on the 1970 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

1971: "Brown Sugar" by the Rolling Stones

"Brown Sugar" is credited to Jagger-Richards, but Wikipedia says it was primarily the work of Mick Jagger.

He wrote it sometime during the filming of Ned Kelly in 1969.

The Stones recorded it over three days, December 2 to 4, 1969, at Muscle Shoals Sound Studio in Sheffield, Alabama.

It was not released until over a year later because of legal wranglings with the band's former label.

The Stones debuted it live at the Altamont Speedway concert on December 6, 1969, at the request of guitarist Mick Taylor.

Wikipedia says it became a number one hit in both the United States and Canada.

This site's weekly data shows two weeks at number one, May 29 and June 5, 1971.

So the chart week that contains May 29 was the first of two.

The song is number 16 on the 1971 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

1972: "Oh Girl" by the Chi-Lites

Eugene Record wrote "Oh Girl," sang lead and produced the Chi-Lites' recording.

It came out on Brunswick Records in 1972 and appears on the album A Lonely Man.

The lyrics centre on a relationship on the verge of a break-up, and a harmonica runs through the song.

Wikipedia calls it the group's first and only number one on the Hot 100, where it spent one week in May 1972.

It also reached number one on the R&B chart the next month and stayed there two weeks.

This site's weekly data agrees, with the chart week of May 27, 1972.

The song is number 13 on the 1972 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

May 29 in Music History, 1973 to 1975: A Monster Instrumental, a Streaking Novelty and a Night-Sky Funk Anthem

The middle of the decade brought a heavily edited rock instrumental, a novelty hit about streaking and the only Hot 100 number one for Earth, Wind & Fire.

1973: "Frankenstein" by the Edgar Winter Group

Edgar Winter composed "Frankenstein," and Rick Derringer produced the single.

Wikipedia says drummer Chuck Ruff coined the title because of the massive editing of the original studio recording.

The band had drifted into less structured jams, so the final track was spliced together from many sections.

It is an instrumental from the 1972 album They Only Come Out at Night, and Winter described its "monster-like, lumbering beat."

It sold over a million copies, and the RIAA certified it gold on June 19, 1973.

This site's weekly data shows one week at number one, the chart week of May 26, 1973.

The song is number 16 on the 1973 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

1974: "The Streak" by Ray Stevens

Ray Stevens wrote, produced and sang "The Streak," a country novelty song released in February 1974.

Wikipedia says Stevens first got the idea while reading a news magazine on an airplane, which had a brief item about streaking.

He has said that 15 other streaking songs were already out by the time his was released.

He put his out less than a week before the televised 46th Academy Awards, where Robert Opel streaked across the stage behind host David Niven.

It was Stevens' second number one on the Hot 100, and it also topped the UK singles chart for one week in June 1974.

This site's weekly data shows three weeks at number one, from May 18 to June 1, 1974.

So the chart week that contains May 29 was the second of three.

The song is number 8 on the 1974 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

1975: "Shining Star" by Earth, Wind & Fire

Maurice White produced "Shining Star," which White, Larry Dunn and Philip Bailey composed.

The idea came to White while he was strolling at night during the band's recording of That's the Way of the World at Caribou Ranch.

He was inspired by looking up at the starry sky.

Wikipedia says it rose to number one on both the Hot 100 and the Hot Soul Songs chart, and it is the group's only single to top the Hot 100.

It also won the Grammy Award for Best R&B Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocals.

This site's weekly data shows one week at number one, the chart week of May 24, 1975.

The song is number 7 on the 1975 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

More on the band is on the Earth Wind and Fire songs page.

May 29 in Music History, 1976 to 1979: A Strobe-Light Session, a Jazz Tribute, a Beatle's Band and a Sequel Duet

The last four Mays brought a disco-era record for Diana Ross, Stevie Wonder's thank-you to jazz, Paul McCartney's Wings and the longest run of the ten.

1976: "Love Hangover" by Diana Ross

Marilyn McLeod and Pam Sawyer wrote "Love Hangover," and Hal Davis produced it.

Davis told engineer Russ Terrana to install a strobe light so Ross could get into a "disco" mindset.

As the song moved from ballad to uptempo, Ross became more comfortable, and Wikipedia says she even imitated Billie Holiday.

The 5th Dimension released their own version, and the two versions entered the chart the same day.

Wikipedia says it reached number one on May 29, 1976, and that Casey Kasem reported on American Top 40 that it was Ross's fourth number one, passing Connie Francis, Helen Reddy, Roberta Flack and Cher, who each had three.

This site's weekly data agrees, with two weeks at number one, May 29 and June 5, 1976.

So the chart week that contains May 29 was the first of two.

The song is number 15 on the 1976 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

More on the singer is on the Diana Ross songs page.

1977: "Sir Duke" by Stevie Wonder

Stevie Wonder composed and performed "Sir Duke" for his 1976 album Songs in the Key of Life.

He wrote it as a tribute to Duke Ellington, who died in 1974.

The lyrics also name Count Basie, Glenn Miller, Louis Armstrong and Ella Fitzgerald.

Wonder said he knew the title from the beginning but wanted the song to be about the musicians who did something for him.

It was released as a single in 1977 and topped both the Hot 100 and the Black Singles chart.

This site's weekly data shows three weeks at number one, from May 21 to June 4, 1977.

So the chart week that contains May 29 was the second of three.

The song is number 18 on the 1977 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

The full story is on the Sir Duke page.

1978: "With a Little Luck" by Wings

Wikipedia says "With a Little Luck" was written in Scotland and was the follow-up to Wings' "Mull of Kintyre."

The band recorded it in May 1977 aboard the boat Fair Carol in the Virgin Islands, which had been fitted with a 24-track studio.

Paul McCartney played electric piano, bass and synthesiser on the track.

It was the first single from the album London Town and came out in the US on March 17, 1978.

While it was at the top, McCartney announced a new Wings line-up with lead guitarist Laurence Juber and drummer Steve Holley.

This site's weekly data shows two weeks at number one, May 20 and May 27, 1978.

So the chart week that contains May 29 was the second and last.

The song is number 18 on the 1978 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

1979: "Reunited" by Peaches & Herb

Dino Fekaris and Freddie Perren wrote "Reunited," the second single from the duo's album 2 Hot.

Wikipedia calls it the sequel to Peaches & Herb's 1968 hit "(We'll Be) United," which was a cover of the Intruders' 1966 hit.

It spent four weeks at number one on both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart in 1979 and sold over two million copies.

This site's weekly data agrees, with four weeks from May 5 to May 26, 1979.

So the chart week that contains May 29 was the fourth and last of the longest run of the ten.

The song is number 5 on the 1979 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

Where May 29 Fell in Each Run

The weeks split three ways:

  • 5 songs were in their first week at number one: "Brown Sugar," "Oh Girl," "Frankenstein," "Shining Star" and "Love Hangover."
  • 2 were in the middle of a longer run: "The Streak" and "Sir Duke."
  • 3 were in the last week of their runs: "American Woman," "With a Little Luck" and "Reunited."

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the number one song on May 29 in music history in the 1970s?

The number one song for the Hot 100 chart week that includes May 29 changed every year. It was "American Woman" in 1970, "Brown Sugar" in 1971, "Oh Girl" in 1972, "Frankenstein" in 1973 and "The Streak" in 1974. Five more follow, ending with "Reunited" in 1979.

Which 70s number one song stayed on top the longest around May 29?

Peaches & Herb's "Reunited" held the longest run of the ten songs, with four weeks, according to this site's weekly Hot 100 data. It ran from May 5 to May 26, 1979. The Guess Who's "American Woman," Ray Stevens' "The Streak" and Stevie Wonder's "Sir Duke" tie for second with three weeks each.

What was the number one song on May 29, 1971?

The Rolling Stones' "Brown Sugar" was number one for the Hot 100 chart week that began May 29, 1971. It was the first of its two weeks at number one, according to this site's weekly Hot 100 data. Billboard ranked it number 16 for 1971, which matches the 1971 year-end Hot 100 in this site's data.

How can I find the number one song from my own birthday?

You can find the number one song from your birthday with the Birthday #1 Song Finder on this site. Enter a birthday from the 1970s and it shows the song that topped the Billboard Hot 100 that week, using the same weekly chart data as the ten songs on this page.

Why does the chart week change from year to year?

This site's weekly Hot 100 file dates each chart week from a Saturday, and that date moves each year. The week containing May 29 began May 29 in 1971 and 1976, May 28 in 1977, May 27 in 1972 and 1978, May 26 in 1973 and 1979, May 25 in 1974, May 24 in 1975 and May 23 in 1970.
