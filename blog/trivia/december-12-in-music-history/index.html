December 12 in Music History: The 1970s Number Ones

By Charlie · Published December 12, 2026

On December 12 in music history, the number one song in America changed with every year of the 1970s.

The Billboard Hot 100 chart week that contains December 12 had a different song on top in all ten years.

Those ten songs spent 39 weeks at number one between them.

The Short Answer: Who Was on Top Each Year

For December 12 in music history, the Hot 100 chart week that includes December 12, the number one song was a different record in every year from 1970 to 1979.

Here is what the ten weeks show at a glance:

  • The ten songs spent 39 weeks at number one in total.
  • "You Light Up My Life" had the longest run, with ten weeks starting October 15, 1977, and December 12 fell in its ninth.
  • 5 of the ten songs were in their first week at number one that week.
  • 4 were somewhere in the middle of a longer run, and one was in the final week of its run.

How the December 12 Week Was Picked

Billboard chart weeks do not line up with the calendar, so there is no single week called December 12.

This page takes the weekly Hot 100 week that contains December 12 in each year.

That week began on a different date each year:

  • 1970: the chart week of December 12, 1970, with "The Tears of a Clown" by Smokey Robinson & the Miracles on top.
  • 1971: the chart week of December 11, 1971, with "Family Affair" by Sly and the Family Stone on top.
  • 1972: the chart week of December 9, 1972, with "I Am Woman" by Helen Reddy on top.
  • 1973: the chart week of December 8, 1973, with "Top of the World" by The Carpenters on top.
  • 1974: the chart week of December 7, 1974, with "Kung Fu Fighting" by Carl Douglas on top.
  • 1975: the chart week of December 6, 1975, with "Fly, Robin, Fly" by Silver Convention on top.
  • 1976: the chart week of December 11, 1976, with "Tonight's the Night (Gonna Be Alright)" by Rod Stewart on top.
  • 1977: the chart week of December 10, 1977, with "You Light Up My Life" by Debby Boone on top.
  • 1978: the chart week of December 9, 1978, with "Le Freak" by Chic on top.
  • 1979: the chart week of December 8, 1979, with "Babe" by Styx on top.

December 12 in Music History, 1970 to 1972: A Motown Hit, a Funk Landmark and an Anthem

The first three Decembers of the decade brought a Motown classic, a drum-machine funk record and a song that became an anthem.

1970: "The Tears of a Clown" by Smokey Robinson and the Miracles

Stevie Wonder wrote the music, and Hank Cosby produced the instrumental track.

Smokey Robinson wrote the lyrics and sang lead, and the song first appeared on the 1967 Miracles album Make It Happen.

Wikipedia says Motown Britain released it as a single first, in July 1970, and it reached number one in the UK before the US version came out.

Charles R. Sirard played the bassoon, which is one of the few hit pop singles to feature that instrument.

It was the Miracles' first and only number one with Robinson as lead singer.

The run began December 12, so the chart week that contains December 12 was the first of its two weeks.

1971: "Family Affair" by Sly and the Family Stone

Sly Stone wrote and produced "Family Affair," and it came from the album There's a Riot Goin' On.

Epic released the single on November 6, 1971, and Wikipedia calls it the band's third and final number one pop single.

It also topped the R&B chart, and the drum-machine rhythm was reused later by the Temptations and by Stevie Wonder.

The song is number 79 on the 1972 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

It spent three weeks at number one, and December 11 was the second.

1972: "I Am Woman" by Helen Reddy

Helen Reddy and Ray Burton, both Australian, wrote "I Am Woman."

Wikipedia says it debuted at number 99 in the June 24, 1972 issue and reached number one in the December 9, 1972 issue.

Reddy won the Grammy for Best Female Pop Vocal Performance, and it was the first Australian-penned song to win a Grammy.

It was also the first Hot 100 number one by an Australian-born artist.

This site's weekly data shows one week at number one, the week of December 9.

More of her records are on Helen Reddy Songs.

December 12 in Music History, 1973 to 1975: A Sibling Duo, a Kung Fu Craze and a German Disco Hit

The middle of the decade brought a duo's second number one, a B-side that took over the charts and a German studio group.

1973: "Top of the World" by the Carpenters

Richard Carpenter wrote the music and John Bettis wrote the lyrics.

The album version came from 1972, and the single version was recorded on August 15, 1973.

Karen Carpenter re-recorded her lead vocal for the single because she was not satisfied with the original.

Wikipedia says the single followed Lynn Anderson's country cover, which reached number two on the country chart.

It was the duo's second number one on the Hot 100.

The song is number 39 on the 1974 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

It held the top for two weeks, and December 8 was the second and last.

More of their records are on Carpenters Songs.

1974: "Kung Fu Fighting" by Carl Douglas

Carl Douglas wrote "Kung Fu Fighting," and Biddu produced it.

Biddu had hired Douglas to sing "I Want to Give You My Everything" and needed a B-side.

Only about ten minutes of a three-hour session were left, so just two takes were recorded.

Wikipedia says Robin Blanchflower of Pye Records insisted it be the A-side.

It reached number one on both the Hot 100 and the Hot Soul Singles chart.

The song is number 15 on the 1975 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

Douglas also appears on the 70s one-hit wonders list.

The run began December 7, so the week that contains December 12 was the first of two.

1975: "Fly, Robin, Fly" by Silver Convention

Sylvester Levay and Michael Kunze wrote "Fly, Robin, Fly," and it was the third single from the album Save Me.

Wikipedia calls it a chart-topper with only six words.

Silver Convention became the second German act to top the US charts, after Bert Kaempfert.

The song won the 1976 Grammy for Best R&B Instrumental Performance.

It held the top for three weeks, and December 6 was the second.

The song is number 14 on the 1976 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

December 12 in Music History, 1976 to 1979: Two Long Runs, a Disco Comeback and a Power Ballad

The last four Decembers brought the decade's two longest runs, a disco record that kept returning and a decade-closing ballad.

1976: "Tonight's the Night (Gonna Be Alright)" by Rod Stewart

Rod Stewart wrote "Tonight's the Night (Gonna Be Alright)," and Tom Dowd produced it for the album A Night on the Town.

Jerry Jumonville played the saxophone solo.

Wikipedia says the BBC originally banned the song.

It reached number one on November 13, 1976 and stayed there eight consecutive weeks, until January 1, 1977.

December 11 was the fifth of those eight weeks.

The song is number 1 on the 1977 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

1977: "You Light Up My Life" by Debby Boone

Joseph Brooks wrote "You Light Up My Life" and produced Debby Boone's version.

It won him the Academy Award for Best Original Song and the Grammy for Song of the Year.

Boone's version was recorded in April 1977 and released on August 16, 1977.

It ran ten weeks at number one, from October 15 to December 17, 1977.

December 10 was the ninth of the ten weeks.

The song is number 3 on the 1978 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

1978: "Le Freak" by Chic

Bernard Edwards and Nile Rodgers of Chic wrote and produced "Le Freak."

Atlantic released it on September 21, 1978, as the first single from C'est Chic.

Rodgers says it was devised around New Year's Eve 1977 after he and Edwards were refused entry to Studio 54.

It was Chic's first number one on the Hot 100, and it also topped the R&B chart.

This site's weekly data shows six weeks at number one, in three separate stretches.

The chart week of December 9 was its first, then Barbra Streisand and Neil Diamond took over, and it returned December 23 and again January 20.

The song is number 3 on the 1979 year-end Hot 100, in this site's year-end data.

1979: "Babe" by Styx

Dennis DeYoung wrote "Babe" as a birthday present for his wife, Suzanne.

It was the lead single from the album Cornerstone, released on September 14, 1979.

Wikipedia says it was not meant for Styx at first, and J.Y. Young and Tommy Shaw convinced DeYoung to include his demo.

It was Styx's first and only number one in the US, and the penultimate number one of the 1970s.

It spent two weeks at number one, starting December 8.

Where December 12 Fell in Each Run

The weeks split three ways:

  • 5 songs were in their first week at number one: "The Tears of a Clown," "I Am Woman," "Kung Fu Fighting," "Le Freak" and "Babe."
  • 4 were in the middle of a longer run: "Family Affair," "Fly, Robin, Fly," "Tonight's the Night (Gonna Be Alright)" and "You Light Up My Life."
  • One was in the last week of its run: "Top of the World."

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the number one song on December 12 in music history in the 1970s?

The number one song for the Hot 100 chart week that includes December 12 changed every year. It was "The Tears of a Clown" in 1970, "Family Affair" in 1971, "I Am Woman" in 1972, "Top of the World" in 1973 and "Kung Fu Fighting" in 1974. The list ends with five more, up to "Babe" in 1979.

Which 70s number one song stayed on top the longest around December 12?

Debby Boone's "You Light Up My Life" stayed on top the longest, with ten weeks at number one from October 15 to December 17, 1977, according to this site's weekly Hot 100 data. The chart week that contains December 12 was the ninth of those ten weeks.

What was the number one song on December 12, 1978?

Chic's "Le Freak" was number one for the Hot 100 chart week of December 9 to 15, 1978. Barbra Streisand and Neil Diamond's "You Don't Bring Me Flowers" took the top spot the next week, and "Le Freak" came back for two weeks starting December 23 and three more starting January 20, 1979, six weeks in all, according to this site's weekly Hot 100 data.

How can I find the number one song from my own birthday?

You can find the number one song from your birthday with the Birthday #1 Song Finder on this site. Enter a birthday from the 1970s and it shows the song that topped the Billboard Hot 100 that week, using the same weekly chart data as the ten songs on this page.

Why does the chart week change from year to year?

This site's weekly Hot 100 file dates each chart week from a Saturday, and that date moves each year. The week containing December 12 began December 12 in 1970, December 11 in 1971 and 1976, December 10 in 1977, December 9 in 1972 and 1978, December 8 in 1973 and 1979, December 7 in 1974 and December 6 in 1975.
