Top Songs of 1971: The Year's Biggest Hits & Trends

By Charlie · Published July 29, 2026 · Updated October 6, 2026

▶ Jump to the playable list

The top songs of 1971 tell a story of soft-rock ballads, novelty pop, and rising soul music.

Three Dog Night owned the very top of the chart that year.

But the real story of 1971 is how many different sounds fought for space just beneath it.

The Carpenters quietly became the most consistent hitmakers of the year.

The Osmonds turned a teenage heartthrob into a chart regular.

And soul singers used the radio to say something that mattered.

Here's a closer look at the artists, trends, and singles that defined the top songs of 1971.

Three Dog Night's "Joy to the World" Ruled the Top Songs of 1971

Billboard's Year-End Hot 100 named "Joy to the World" the single biggest song of the year.

Nothing else came close.

Three Dog Night recorded the track almost as an afterthought.

They tucked it onto the back of their 1970 album Naturally instead of pushing it as a lead single.

Once a Seattle radio DJ started spinning it anyway, the song took off on its own.

It rode the weekly Hot 100 to six consecutive weeks at number one that spring.

Written by singer-songwriter Hoyt Axton, the song opens with a line about a bullfrog named Jeremiah.

That lyric is so strange it somehow became part of the charm.

That combination, an album cut turning into the year's runaway single, is rare.

It's a big part of why the top songs of 1971 still get talked about decades later.

That kind of slow-burn success is rare in any era of pop music.

The Carpenters Owned the Year With Three Hits

Three Dog Night had the single biggest song of the year.

But no act placed more songs on the year-end list than The Carpenters.

Three separate singles from the duo made the 1971 chart, more than any other artist that year.

Karen Carpenter's vocals stayed warm and unhurried against Richard Carpenter's lush arrangements.

That sound felt like a deliberate counterweight to the heavier rock still dominating FM radio.

That soft-rock approach didn't just chart.

It helped define an entire lane of early-70s pop that other acts spent the rest of the decade chasing.

Three hits in one year from a single act is a rare feat.

It's one of the clearest signals of just how dominant The Carpenters were among the top songs of 1971.

1971's Wave of Double-Sided Singles

One of 1971's more unusual trends was the double-sided hit.

That's a single where both the A-side and the B-side found real airplay and chart attention on their own.

Several major names had exactly this kind of success in 1971.

  • Rod Stewart
  • Carole King
  • George Harrison
  • Chicago
  • Paul McCartney

That pattern lines up with a bigger shift happening in music at the time.

Singer-songwriters increasingly wrote albums meant to be heard as complete statements, not built around one obvious single.

When a record has two sides strong enough to both become hits, that's rare.

It's usually a sign the artist behind it was working at the peak of their craft.

Donny Osmond's Breakout Crossover Year

Few stories from 1971 are as unusual as Donny Osmond's.

He landed on the year-end chart four separate times.

Two came as a solo artist, and two came as part of his family group, The Osmonds.

That dual presence, his own name and his family's name charting in the same year, was rare.

It made him one of the most visible young performers in the country almost overnight.

It also set the template for clean-cut, TV-friendly teen stardom.

A whole wave of early-70s pop acts followed his lead.

Few teen idols managed that same crossover balance again in the years that followed.

Soul and R&B's Commercial Rise

Soul and R&B weren't a side note to 1971.

They were a genuine commercial force sitting near the top of the charts all year.

Several major soul and R&B acts had real mainstream success during this stretch.

  • The Temptations
  • Al Green
  • Honey Cone
  • Jean Knight

Marvin Gaye's contribution stood apart from the rest for a different reason entirely.

With "What's Going On" and "Mercy Mercy Me (The Ecology)," he brought socially conscious themes into the mainstream conversation.

That willingness to say something real, wrapped in a sound smooth enough for daytime radio, stood out.

It's part of what makes 1971's chart such a rich moment to look back on.

Looking back at the top songs of 1971 as a whole, no other year opened quite the same way.

Soft rock, novelty pop, and message-driven soul all shared the same airwaves.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the number one song of 1971?

Three Dog Night's "Joy to the World" was the top song of 1971 according to Billboard's Year-End Hot 100.

Written by Hoyt Axton, the song became one of the year's most inescapable radio hits.

It spent six consecutive weeks at number one on the weekly chart that spring.

That run helped make Three Dog Night one of the most consistent hitmakers of the early 70s.

Which artist had the most songs on the 1971 year-end chart?

The Carpenters placed three singles on Billboard's Year-End Hot 100 for 1971, more than any other act that year.

Their soft-rock sound, built around Karen Carpenter's vocals, made them one of the defining acts of the early 70s.

It also helped push a gentler, more melodic style into the mainstream alongside heavier rock.

How many times did Donny Osmond chart in 1971?

Donny Osmond appeared on the 1971 year-end chart four times total.

Two came as a solo artist, and two came as a member of his family group, The Osmonds.

That crossover success made him one of the most visible young stars of the year.

He bridged teen pop stardom and family-friendly variety television at the same time.

What were double-sided hit singles, and why were there so many in 1971?

A double-sided hit is a single where both the A-side and the B-side became popular on their own.

Each side could chart or earn real airplay independently.

In 1971, artists like Rod Stewart, Carole King, George Harrison, Chicago, and Paul McCartney all had double-sided successes.

That reflected the strength of that era's singer-songwriter movement.

Which soul and R&B artists were big in 1971?

Soul and R&B had a major commercial presence in 1971.

Acts like The Temptations, Al Green, Honey Cone, and Jean Knight led the way.

Marvin Gaye also broke through with socially conscious songs like "What's Going On" and "Mercy Mercy Me (The Ecology)."

Those songs brought real-world themes into the mainstream pop charts.
