Top Songs of 1975: The Year's Biggest Hits & Trends

By Charlie · Published August 2, 2026 · Updated October 6, 2026

▶ Jump to the playable list

The top songs of 1975 were led by a song that almost went to someone else first.

Captain & Tennille's "Love Will Keep Us Together" was originally a Neil Sedaka song.

Sedaka had written and recorded it two years earlier.

It took the husband-and-wife duo's cover to turn it into the definitive number one hit of the year.

Beyond that chart-topper, 1975 belonged to Elton John more than any other artist.

He landed three separate songs on the year-end list.

This is a closer look at the songs and artists behind the top songs of 1975.

Chart data: Billboard Year-End Hot 100, 1975.

Captain & Tennille's Cover Topped the Top Songs of 1975

Billboard's Year-End Hot 100 named "Love Will Keep Us Together" the top song of 1975.

Its path to the top wasn't a straight one.

The song was written by Neil Sedaka and Howard Greenfield.

Sedaka had already recorded his own version back in 1973.

It never became a major hit for him.

Captain & Tennille recorded their own cover two years later.

It took off in a way the original never had.

Something about the arrangement clicked with radio in a way Sedaka's version simply hadn't.

The cover went on to become the top-selling single of the entire year.

It also won the Grammy Award for Record of the Year.

Glen Campbell and Elton John Rounded Out the Top Three

Right behind Captain & Tennille, the top of the 1975 chart mixed country crossover with rock star songwriting.

Glen Campbell's "Rhinestone Cowboy" ranked #2 for the year, while Elton John's "Philadelphia Freedom" took #3.

Both songs still sound instantly recognizable decades later.

That Elton John hit carried a personal story behind it too.

He wrote the song as a tribute to his good friend, tennis star Billie Jean King.

The song's title came from her Philadelphia Freedoms professional tennis team.

It's an unusual origin story for a song this well remembered.

Most listeners have no idea a tennis franchise inspired it.

That kind of range is striking, a country ballad and a pop hit inspired by tennis.

It's part of what makes 1975 such an interesting year to look back on.

Few years mixed those two worlds quite this naturally.

Freddy Fender's Two Top-30 Hits

Freddy Fender had one of the most successful years of any single artist in 1975.

He didn't even need three songs to do it.

His bilingual country ballad "Before the Next Teardrop Falls" ranked #4 for the year.

He followed it up with "Wasted Days and Wasted Nights" at #27.

Landing two songs that high on the year-end list was a real breakout achievement.

Both blended country storytelling with genuine bilingual crossover appeal.

Few artists that year moved as easily between English and Spanish audiences as Fender did.

That combination made Fender one of the year's most distinctive success stories.

Elton John Was the Year's Busiest Artist

Fender had a strong year with two hits.

But no artist placed more songs on the 1975 year-end list than Elton John.

Beyond "Philadelphia Freedom" at #3, John charted with two more songs that year.

  • "Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds" — #35
  • "Someone Saved My Life Tonight" — #92

That gave him three songs on the year-end list, more than any other act.

No single artist dominated the top songs of 1975 quite like he did.

That stretch showed off real range too.

A Beatles cover, a friend tribute, and a deeply personal song all landed in the same year's chart.

Few artists could pull off that kind of variety in a single twelve-month stretch.

Elton John simply made it look easy.

A Long List of Artists With Multiple Hits

Beyond the artists at the very top, 1975 had an unusually long list of multi-hit acts.

Each placed more than one song on the year-end chart.

  • Linda Ronstadt
  • Barry Manilow
  • Earth, Wind & Fire
  • The Carpenters
  • Paul Anka & Odia Coates
  • B.T. Express
  • Average White Band
  • Ringo Starr
  • America
  • Frankie Valli
  • John Denver
  • Neil Sedaka
  • Eagles
  • Olivia Newton-John

That's fourteen more acts landing multiple hits in the same year, on top of Elton John and Freddy Fender.

It's a real sign of how deep and competitive the pop landscape had become by 1975.

That depth is a big part of what makes the top songs of 1975 worth revisiting.
