How to Listen

Pick a station and press Play.

The video shows in the player above the controls, and the station plays that genre on shuffle, one song rolling into the next.

Or open the Jukebox Grid and find one song by title, artist, genre or year.

Every song plays as a YouTube video with YouTube's own controls, so you can pause, seek, turn on captions or go fullscreen.

Anything without a playable video shows as pending or unavailable in the Jukebox Grid.

How the Station Keeps Playing

Nothing plays until you press Play or pick a song.

After that, each song rolls into the next while the player is on screen.

If you scroll the player out of view or switch to another tab, the station waits between songs.

It carries on when the player is back on screen, or when you press Continue.

One Song at a Time

For a single pick, Random 70s Song draws one at random and the Mood Song Matcher finds one to fit how you feel.

What Counts as a Hit Here

At the end of every year, Billboard ranked the 100 biggest singles of that year, and those ten Year-End Hot 100 lists make up the whole catalog.

That gives 100 songs from each year, from Bridge Over Troubled Water at the top of 1970 to My Sharona at the top of 1979.

The runner-up spots hold some of the best-known records of the decade, like Night Fever by the Bee Gees at No. 2 for 1978 and Bad Girls by Donna Summer at No. 2 for 1979.

How the site handles this chart data is set out on the methodology page.

How the Sound Shifted

Using this site's genre labels, the Soul station holds 29 songs from 1970 and only 5 from 1979.

Classic rock held steadier, with 24 songs in 1970 and between 12 and 18 in most years after.

The Stations at a Glance

Listen Now draws on 1,000 songs from Billboard's Year-End Hot 100 lists for 1970 to 1979, 100 songs from each year.

Each song is filed under one station, using this site's own genre labels.

All counts and chart positions below come straight from that list.

Pop Station

The widest net on the dial: melodic, polished singles from the decade's chart-topping vocalists and groups.

230 songs from 1970 to 1979, busiest in 1977 (35 songs).

By era: 96 early 70s, 71 late 70s, 63 mid 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: Tie a Yellow Ribbon Round the Ole Oak Tree by Tony Orlando and Dawn (1973, #1); The Way We Were by Barbra Streisand (1974, #1); Love Will Keep Us Together by Captain & Tennille (1975, #1).

Most charted artists here: The Carpenters (6), Barry Manilow (5), and Helen Reddy (5).

Soul Station

Vocal-driven soul and Motown-style records, from smooth ballads to uptempo cuts.

185 songs from 1970 to 1979, busiest in 1970 (29 songs).

By era: 103 early 70s, 53 mid 70s, 29 late 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face by Roberta Flack (1972, #1); Killing Me Softly with His Song by Roberta Flack (1973, #3); Let's Get It On by Marvin Gaye (1973, #4).

Most charted artists here: Gladys Knight & the Pips (7), Al Green (6), and Stevie Wonder (6).

Soft Rock Station

Mellow, melodic rock and singer-songwriter hits that lean on harmony and warmth.

165 songs from 1970 to 1979, busiest in 1979 (25 songs).

By era: 58 early 70s, 56 late 70s, 51 mid 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: Bridge Over Troubled Water by Simon & Garfunkel (1970, #1); Tonight's the Night (Gonna Be Alright) by Rod Stewart (1977, #1); Seasons in the Sun by Terry Jacks (1974, #2).

Most charted artists here: Bee Gees (5), Paul Simon (5), and The Carpenters (5).

Classic Rock Station

Guitar-led rock and pop-rock staples from the decade's biggest rock acts.

156 songs from 1970 to 1979, busiest in 1970 (24 songs).

By era: 72 early 70s, 46 mid 70s, 38 late 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: Joy to the World by Three Dog Night (1971, #1); My Sharona by The Knack (1979, #1); Maggie May / Reason to Believe by Rod Stewart (1971, #2).

Most charted artists here: Elton John (7), Chicago (6), and Three Dog Night (5).

Disco Station

Dance-floor records built on a steady beat, from early disco to the late-decade peak.

94 songs from 1974 to 1979, busiest in 1979 (34 songs).

By era: 63 late 70s, 31 mid 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: Shadow Dancing by Andy Gibb (1978, #1); Night Fever by Bee Gees (1978, #2); Bad Girls by Donna Summer (1979, #2).

Most charted artists here: Bee Gees (5), Donna Summer (5), and KC and the Sunshine Band (5).

Country Station

Country and country-pop singles that also made the Billboard Hot 100.

60 songs from 1970 to 1979, busiest in 1975 (10 songs).

By era: 25 early 70s, 20 mid 70s, 15 late 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: Rhinestone Cowboy by Glen Campbell (1975, #2); Before the Next Teardrop Falls by Freddy Fender (1975, #4); Why Me by Kris Kristofferson (1973, #6).

Most charted artists here: Anne Murray (4), Glen Campbell (4), and Charlie Rich (3).

Funk Station

Groove-first records built around bass lines, horns and rhythm.

58 songs from 1970 to 1979, busiest in 1973 (13 songs).

By era: 28 early 70s, 20 mid 70s, 10 late 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: Come and Get Your Love by Redbone (1974, #4); Play That Funky Music by Wild Cherry (1976, #5); I Gotcha by Joe Tex (1972, #6).

Most charted artists here: War (7), Ohio Players (4), and Stevie Wonder (4).

Hard Rock Station

Heavier, louder guitar rock from the rock side of the chart.

28 songs from 1970 to 1979, busiest in 1973 (5 songs).

By era: 12 early 70s, 9 mid 70s, 7 late 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: Frankenstein by The Edgar Winter Group (1973, #16); The Ballroom Blitz by Sweet (1975, #17); We're an American Band by Grand Funk Railroad (1973, #23).

Most charted artists here: Aerosmith (2), Bad Company (2), and Foreigner (2).

R&B Station

Rhythm and blues records from across the decade, a small station with plenty of variety.

13 songs from 1970 to 1979, busiest in 1979 (6 songs).

By era: 7 late 70s, 4 mid 70s, 2 early 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: Lovin' You by Minnie Riperton (1975, #14); Fire by The Pointer Sisters (1979, #15); Pillow Talk by Sylvia Robinson (1973, #22).

Most charted artists here: Raydio (2), B.B. King (1), and Bee Gees (1).

Prog Rock Station

Progressive and art-rock acts whose singles made the Hot 100, the smallest station.

11 songs from 1972 to 1979, busiest in 1972 (2 songs).

By era: 4 early 70s, 4 late 70s, 3 mid 70s.

Highest on the Year-End Hot 100: The Logical Song by Supertramp (1979, #27); Nights in White Satin by The Moody Blues (1972, #32); Dust in the Wind by Kansas (1978, #39).

Most charted artists here: Kansas (2), Electric Light Orchestra (1), and Focus (1).

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Listen Now free?

Yes, there is no sign-up, no account and no charge.

What was the second-biggest hit of 1970?

(They Long to Be) Close to You by The Carpenters, right behind Bridge Over Troubled Water on the 1970 Year-End list.

What was the No. 2 song of 1976?

Don't Go Breaking My Heart by Elton John and Kiki Dee, behind Silly Love Songs by Wings.

Which year has the most disco songs here?

1979, with 34 of that year's 100 songs on the Disco station, up from 5 in 1974.
